$100M Money Models

Alex Hormozi 32 videos · ~6 hours Book 3 of the $100M trilogy Synthesized June 2026

Book 3 answers the final question in the trilogy: how do we get customers to buy? Offers tells you what to sell. Leads tells you how to find people. Money Models tells you how to structure the transaction so you make more money in 30 days than it cost you to acquire the customer — which removes cash as a constraint on growth.

The Big Idea
30-Day Gross Profit > CAC = Self-Funding Growth

Overview: Why Money Models Matter

Most businesses have great offers and decent leads but still struggle to scale because they run out of cash between acquiring a customer and getting paid back. Money Models solves this. The goal is Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA): structure your offers so customers finance their own acquisition cost — you never have to carry debt to grow.

Gym Launch

Started with $1K. Reached $17M profit in 20 months. 30-day GP:CAC ratio = 12:1. Year 1 ROAS = 100:1 ($100K spend → $10M).

Allan (software)

Scaled from $0 to $1.7M/month in 6 months using these money model structures.

The Rental Car Lesson

$19/day advertised → ~$400 actual transaction via car upgrade + insurance + gas + checkout. Every business has hidden upsell potential.

Hormozi's Rule

"Simple scales — have as few offers as possible to make maximum GP in the shortest time." Add complexity only after the core works.

The Three Core Numbers

Every business that scales has mastered three numbers. These are the levers of CFA.

Number Definition Key Insight
CAC
Customer Acquisition Cost		 All costs to get one customer: ad spend + payroll + commissions + software Outreach ≈ $500 · Content ≈ $1,100 · Paid ads ≈ $3,500. "Free" is not free — it brings broke people and closes at the same rate.
Gross Profit Revenue minus variable COGS only (not overhead) Service businesses need 80%+ gross margin to scale. GP is the "arms race of business" — whoever can spend more to acquire wins.
Payback Period Days until cumulative GP from customer > CAC Shorter = easier to scale. Upsell timing, enrollment fees, and layaway compress this toward zero.
Required Ratio
LTGP : CAC ≥ 3:1

Improving Gross Profit

Three levers — pick one to focus on at a time:

  • More: Sell more units of the same thing (volume)
  • Better: Increase price or quality without proportional cost increase
  • New: Add complementary offers that monetize existing customers

The Penny Gap

The difference between free and $0.01 is enormous. 9x more people take free vs. $0.01. But free doesn't mean better customers — same close rates, only lead cost differs (Hormozi's 20-location test). Use this gap strategically, not reflexively.

Discount Psychology

  • Four ways to frame the same discount: % off · $ off · portion off · crossed-out price
  • Insane discounts (50%+) change behavior; moderate discounts (10-20%) don't move the needle
  • Free = 100% off. The mechanics are identical — just the presentation differs

Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA)

CFA is the mechanism that enables unlimited scale. Three levels:

Level 1 — Sustainable

Lifetime GP > CAC. You're profitable over the customer's life. Minimum bar — not yet scalable on its own.

Minimum

Level 2 — Self-Funding

30-Day GP > CAC. Gross profit from one customer in first 30 days covers cost to get them. You can recycle your credit card. No debt while growing.

Target

Level 3 — Limitless

30-Day GP > 2× CAC. The business doubles itself on house money. Every dollar spent on ads returns two or more within 30 days.

Limitless Scale
Why 30 Days?

The length of the interest-free credit card window. If your 30-day GP exceeds your CAC, you never have to carry interest while growing. Gym Launch hit 12:1 in 30-day GP:CAC in year one.

The Money Model: Four Offer Types

A money model is a deliberate sequence of offers designed to maximize how many customers you get, how much they pay, and how fast they pay it — all within 30 days where possible.

The Four-Prong Model
Attraction → Upsell → Downsell → Continuity
Prong Goal When It Fires
Attraction Lower CAC · Get leads to engage and become customers Front of the funnel — what you advertise or offer first
Upsell Maximize GP per customer in 30 days Immediately after or shortly after first purchase
Downsell Convert "no" into some version of "yes" After a prospect declines an offer
Continuity Stabilize cash flow · Maximize LTV · Boost company value Last offer in the sequence — after all other offers are made

Upsells often make up the majority of total profit even though they represent a minority of unit sales. McDonald's example: a burger alone = $0.25 gross profit. Burger + fries + soda = $1.75 gross profit — 8× more with the same customer acquisition cost.

Attraction Offers

Attraction offers get leads to engage — and often double as your first conversion. They work by being free or deeply discounted, with a clear path to monetization through the offers that follow. There are 6 types.

1 · Win Your Money Back

Set a goal for the customer. If they hit it, they get their money back as cash or store credit. The goal drives the behaviors your business needs anyway (showing up to sales consults, posting results, tracking progress).

How It Works

  • Three versions: Results-only / actions-only / both (actions + results)
  • 78% success rate when designed correctly
  • At the halfway point, credit their winnings toward a long-term program — "make everyone a winner"

Design Criteria for the Goal

  • Easy to track objectively
  • Drives results that also advertise your business (photos, posts, referrals)
  • Includes mandatory sales appointments (so you can upsell)
  • Uses credit over cash rebate when possible — extend credit over 12 months, not lump-sum upfront
Prerequisite

Only use if your refund rate is below 5%. If your product doesn't reliably get results, fix the product first.

2 · Free Giveaways

Advertise a chance to win a grand prize (your flagship offer). Everyone who enters also wins a participation trophy — a 10–30% discount or partial scholarship. You get contact info, pain-point data, and a built-in sales opportunity with every entry.

Structure

  • Grand prize: Must match the scale of what you sell (a free program, not a t-shirt)
  • Participation prize: Meaningful discount on the real offer — everyone gets something
  • Urgency at 3 moments: to enter / to claim / to use (3–7 day deadline for claiming)
  • Referral loop: "Refer a winner and you win too" = double prizes, double leads
Sales Intel

Add eligibility questions to the entry form. "How long have you been dealing with X?" "What's your budget?" These become your sales ammo when you call to award the prize.

3 · Decoy Offers

Advertise a free or discounted version of your offer. When the lead engages, present both the decoy and the premium side-by-side. 70–80% take the premium. This is an assumed close — everyone buys one or the other.

Steps

  1. 1Advertise the outcome, not the vehicle. "Free 21-day transformation" — not "free workout." This gives you flexibility in how you present the offer when the lead arrives.
  2. 2Ask the sorting question: "Are you here for free stuff or lasting results?" — Everyone says results. Now you have permission to pitch premium first.
  3. 3Present premium with excitement, then contrast with decoy. Let the gap do the work.
  4. 4Ask which will get them to their goal faster — forces them to choose premium themselves.
  5. 5If they take the decoy: surprise them with a free premium feature as goodwill, then upsell at the halfway point.
Key Rule

The contrast between decoy and premium must be huge. If people keep taking the decoy, the premium isn't valuable enough — or the decoy is too good.

4 · Buy X Get Y Free

A 33% discount sounds weak. "Buy two get one free" is the same math but converts dramatically better. This offer reframes pricing — same economics, radically different perception.

The Core Principle

More free things beats fewer expensive free things. "Buy one, get two free" beats "buy two, get one free" — even if the economics are identical. Quantity perception matters more than individual value.

Same Price, Different Framing

Buy 3 shirts for $10 each = $30 total
Buy 1 shirt, get 2 free for $30 = same

The second drives dramatically more attention and response.

Duration Example

All three are 18 months for $1,800:
Buy 12 get 6 free · Buy 9 get 9 free · Buy 6 get 12 free

Buy 6 get 12 converts the best — most free stuff.

Cash Management Rule

If you take prepayments for service you haven't delivered, put the cash in a separate account and pay yourself monthly as you deliver. Cap prepayments at 10% of your recurring customer base. Never use it as an emergency cash play if you can't deliver.

Hyper-buyer insight: People who prepay for long durations are your most likely future buyers. They forget they already paid and their wallet replenishes. Keep making them offers.

5 · Pay Less Now or Pay More Later

Two flavors: (1) Card down for $0 today → billed full price after they've consumed and are satisfied. (2) Discounted price + bonuses if they pay now and lock in. Always get the card first on the "pay later" version, then offer "pay now" as the upgrade.

Key Numbers

  • 10% of pay-later people cancel = normal. Above 10% = fix the promise, conditions, or price
  • Card on file eliminates the friction of a second buying decision — the biggest upsell lever you have
  • For recurring: offer to lock in a lower rate for life by paying now vs. going month-to-month at a higher rate

6 · Free with Consumption

Webinars, challenges, events, books — free content that delivers real value and microwave-accelerates trust. Compress weeks of trust-building into days. The bigger the ticket, the longer the runway you need.

Conversion Benchmarks

  • Mass market virtual event: 2–5% of opt-ins
  • Live webinar attendees: ~10% close rate
  • Strategy calls: 25%+ (your highest-intent channel)
What to Teach

Teach concepts, not tactics. Tactics make them feel they can DIY it. Concepts make them want the done-for-them version. Focus on breaking three beliefs: (1) their circumstances, (2) other people, (3) themselves.

Upsell Offers

An upsell is literally whatever you offer next. It fires immediately after the first purchase — or within the first 30 days. Upsells often represent the majority of profit even with minority of unit volume. "You may be one upsell away from making yours a dynasty."

What to Upsell

  • More: Quantity (one burger → two burgers)
  • Better: Quality upgrade (mystery meat → sirloin)
  • New: Complementary add-on (burger → fries + soda)

Five Timing Windows

Sell at the point of greatest deprivation, not greatest satisfaction:

  1. Immediately after the sale
  2. Next step: 24–72 hours later
  3. After their first big win
  4. Halfway through the program
  5. Last chance / exit point

Upsell 1 · Classic Upsell

Core Frame: "You can't have X without Y." Provide a service or product for free, and make the natural next purchase feel required. The closer to required it feels, the higher the uptake rate. Target: 80%+ close rate on the natural next thing.

The No Sale

Train customers to say "no" to general questions — then structure "you don't want anything else, do you?" so that saying no actually means agreeing to the assumed bundle. The fur coat storage story: "Do you want anything else?" → "No" → customer walks out with storage + conditioning + ear muffs for $30.

Storage Unit Model

Free month of storage → must buy lock ($47) → need boxes → hand truck rental → insurance ($10/month, 90% margin). Free month created $127 average transaction.

Weight Loss Clinic

Services priced at $5/month → second day sells $5K in supplements. The front-end service is the door; supplements are the business.

Benchmark

If you're not getting 80%+ close rates on your classic upsell, the natural connection between X and Y isn't clear enough. Make it feel more required (ethically).

Upsell 2 · Menu Upsell

The most elegant upsell system. Four steps that combine into a single seamless close:

  1. 1Unsell — Cross out what they don't need. "You're not pregnant, so we won't do the lymphatic massage." This builds goodwill and credibility before you sell anything. Employees love this — it feels like a "homie hookup" but is scripted by design.
  2. 2Prescribe — Tell them exactly what they do need and how they'll use it, as though they've already bought it. "Since your shoulder hurts, we'll heat you up first, then hit your trigger points, then do dynamic stretches." Detailed post-purchase instruction increases buy-in dramatically.
  3. 3A/B Choice — Never ask "do you want it?" Ask "do you prefer chocolate or vanilla?" Every choice is a buying choice. Add a nudge: "Most people with big dogs like beef." The illusion of choice + removal of decision friction.
  4. 4Card on File Close — "Do you want to use the card you have on file?" Eliminates the second decision of reaching for a wallet. Works even in a rush — "Card on file?" "Yeah, fine." Zero friction = sale.

The Economist Decoy Trick

When you want customers to buy both A and B: add a third option (C) that's just A at the price of A+B. Now A+B feels like a no-brainer. Example: digital $59 / print only $125 / digital+print $125 — print-only makes digital+print the obvious winner.

If you sell out

Take payment and deliver later. Set the expectation clearly ("in your hands in 7 days"). People care far more about missed expectations than delays. If you say 7 and deliver in 4, they love you. If you say 7 and deliver in 10, they hate you.

Upsell 3 · Anchor Upsell

Present the most expensive version first — 5–10× your main offer price. Expect the customer to gasp. Come to the rescue with the main offer, which now feels like a bargain. Works via contrast: your $2,000 suit seems reasonable after you considered a $16,000 one.

  1. 1Present the anchor — Commit to it seriously. If you treat it like a fake, so will the customer. They must fully consider purchasing it for the anchor to work. Their brain does the heavy lifting of justifying the price.
  2. 2Get the gasp — If they don't react to the price, you didn't anchor high enough. No gasp = no anchor.
  3. 3Come to the rescue — "Do you care about [secondary feature]?" Change only secondary features between anchor and main offer. Primary features should be identical so the customer's value calculation transfers.
  4. 4Close on how to pay — At this point the main offer feels like a steal. Ask which card they prefer.

Why It Pays Even When Anchors Don't Sell

1 in 10 people buy the premium anchor → that's 10× the revenue from 10% of customers. If your main offer is $2K and the anchor is $16K: even if only 1/10 buys the anchor, it adds $1,600 to the average sale of every 10 customers — nearly doubling revenue. Plus: every customer spends more on accessories because they feel like they got a deal.

Rolls-Royce Lesson

Rolls-Royce stopped showing at car shows (where $400K seemed egregious) and started showing at yacht and jet shows (where $400K feels like candy on the way out). Everything is relative to what it's positioned next to.

Upsell 4 · Rollover Upsell

Credit some or all of a customer's previous purchase toward your next offer. Works in four situations: re-engaging old customers, rescuing upset customers, stealing competitors' upset customers, and upselling current customers.

The Four "Whos"

WhoHow to RollScript
Old customers (6+ months gone) Win-back campaign: "I owe you money" "Hey Sandra, I owe you $500. I want to credit it toward staying pain-free for good."
Your upset customers Alternative to a refund — a do-over "That's on me. Let's credit everything you paid toward the thing you actually wanted."
Competitors' upset customers Scrape 1-star reviews, offer credit for switching "I'll credit whatever you have left with them. You start getting benefits now, don't lose a thing."
Current customers Spread credit over time toward a premium upgrade "Since you've enjoyed X, let me credit your first purchase toward a full year at a better rate."

Pricing Rules

  • Upsell must be at least 4× more than the rollover credit (= 25% max discount)
  • Add urgency: make the credit offer valid only in this moment ("take it now or pay full price later")
  • Ideal target: upsell is 5× or more expensive than the credit amount

The Gift Card Play

Sell $200 gift cards for $20 (90% off). Limit 2 per person, only for others (holiday/birthday). When the friend comes in, roll the $200 card into a $1,000+ offer. You got paid $20 to acquire a hot referral who then buys a $1,000 product with $200 credit built in.

Downsell Offers

A downsell fires after a customer says no. The goal is not to lower your price — it's to find a version of "yes" that still makes you money. Never offer the same thing for less (destroys trust). Always offer something different for less.

The Cardinal Sin

The car insurance salesman lowered $5K insurance to $400. The customer didn't buy — not because of price, but because they no longer trusted the seller or the original price. You traded trust for a chance at a buck. Not worth it.

Downsell 1 · Payment Plans

People reject offers because they cost too much today — not because they cost too much in total. Payment plans address the timing problem, not the price problem.

The Seesaw Anchor

Present the payment plan price first ($12K), then offer a prepayment discount ($10K). Now paying in full feels like a win, not a penalty. Standard approach of "here's $10K, or $12K in payments" makes the payment plan feel like punishment.

Seven-Step Downsell Flow

  1. 1Anchor with payment plan price. Present the full payment plan rate first. Offer prepay discount to "save money."
  2. 2Third-party financing. Car dealer model — financing company pays you today, customer pays them. Adding 3rd-party financing increases revenue by ~35% on average. Credit card = "would you rather I pick your payment terms or you?" → they say them → use your card.
  3. 3Half now, half on payday. "When do you get paid?" Schedule the second payment on their paycheck date. Higher likelihood of collection.
  4. 4Temperature check. "Scale of 1–10, how badly do you want this?" If 8+, continue. If 7 or below, pivot to feature downsell.
  5. 5Three-payment split. ⅓ now, ⅓ next paycheck, ⅓ paycheck after that. Gets 3 payments inside 30 days.
  6. 6Evenly spread over service duration. Last resort — spread payments across the full term.
  7. 7Free trial with penalty. Final step — covered in the next section.
Layaway

Pay before you receive. Customer pays installments, gets the thing when paid off. Aligns incentives — they want to pay fast to get the thing. Used for high-ticket B2B: "You can fly out the moment you've paid it off." Agency went from wanting 6-month payment plan to paying in 30 days.

Downsell 2 · Trial with Penalty

Customer gets the product or service free as long as they meet your terms. If they don't meet the terms, they pay. Turns "no" into a trial that's optimized to convert to a paying customer.

Setup Process

  1. 1Offer it last. Only after all other downsells fail. Don't advertise trials — reserve them for the people who've said no to everything else.
  2. 2Always get a card. "What card can I put down just to complete your account profile?" If they bulk: "This is just how we've always done it." Works every time.
  3. 3Sell staying and paying. "If we get you the results, will you stay long term?" If no, don't do the trial. If yes, you've pre-sold continued engagement.
  4. 4Get the card, then explain the fees. Explain penalties after you have payment info — much lower resistance. Have them initial next to each fee clause.

What to Do at Trial's End

  • They love it: Auto-billing is already set up. Still pitch upgrades at the midpoint and end.
  • They hate it: Get angry on their behalf. "That's on me." Ask to make it up to them → offer the higher-tier version. About half buy it.
  • They didn't use it: Offer to waive the fees if they get back on track. Fees are leverage for re-engagement, not revenue.

Downsell 3 · Feature Downsells

Lower the price by removing features. The first removal is strategic: remove something highly valuable and lower the price only slightly — customers often re-upsell themselves back to the original. The second removal takes away something they don't care about with a big price decrease.

Feature Removal Vectors (Services)

  • Response time (5 min → overnight)
  • Availability (anytime → office hours)
  • Sessions (4/month → 2/month)
  • Provider seniority (owner → junior staff)
  • Guarantee (unconditional → conditional → removed)
  • Personalization (custom → generic)
  • Support channel (calls → chat → email only)
The Opening Move

Remove the guarantee first, lower price minimally. Many re-upsell themselves because seeing the price of the guarantee makes them want it back. This move gets more people to buy the full package than any other first downsell.

Existing Customer Feature Downsell

Proactively reach out to customers who aren't using all your features. Tell them you're going to lower their rate to match what they're actually using. These customers never leave — and they become your highest-LTV segment because churn is near zero. "Wow, these guys actually want me to pay less."

Price + Barter

If they absolutely can't pay less but you want to make the deal: trade price for advertising. Lower price by $100 in exchange for reviews, testimonials, daily posts, and 2 warm referrals. This also gives you cover when another customer asks for a discount: "Sure, do what Susan does." They'll usually choose to pay full price.

Continuity Offers

You sell once and get paid again and again. Continuity is the fourth pillar — deployed last because the other three generate cash today while continuity generates the recurring revenue that multiplies LTV and company value.

LTV Formula
LTV = (Price / Monthly Churn %) × Gross Margin %

Reducing churn is the highest-leverage action in a continuity business. Going from 10% monthly churn to 3% = 3× LTV. "Conquering churn" — making fewer people leave each month — is the secret to massive compounding value.

Continuity Business Multiples

A $10M transactional business might trade at 4× EBITDA. A $10M recurring revenue business can trade at 8–15× EBITDA — a difference of $24M to $45M in enterprise value for the same revenue. "If they stay, buyers will pay."

Recurring vs. Reoccurring

  • Recurring: Fixed subscription. Netflix, SaaS. Predictable monthly cash.
  • Reoccurring: Regular buyer without a contract. Coca-Cola. Track cohort re-purchase rates to prove value to buyers.

The Downell-the-Upsell Strategy

Sell a premium intensive up front (high price, short duration), then offer continuity at a lower monthly rate as the "better deal" — more value for less per week. Gym Launch: $16K intensive → $200/month membership. Looks like a downgrade in price; actually converts more customers into long-term recurring revenue.

Continuity 1 · Bonus Offers

Give something that has more value than the first continuity payment to get someone to sign up. Advertise the bonus, not the membership. The bonus is what you're selling; the membership is how they get it.

Newsletter Example

$24,000 of back courses free when you try $399/month newsletter. Or skip the trial, pay $299/month today and lock in the rate for life. This combines continuity bonus + lifetime discount in one offer.

Gym Example

$600–800 challenge given free with membership signup. "You know what, I'll give you the challenge for free if you just sign up as a member." Closed 70–80% of walkins.

Pricing for Continuity vs. Cash

Based on tested data from brick-and-mortar fitness (transfers to most service businesses):

Standalone Price vs. Monthly Rate% Who Choose Continuity
1.33× monthly rate50% continuity
1.66× monthly rate60% continuity
2.0× monthly rate70% continuity
2.3× monthly rate80% continuity
2.66× monthly rate90% continuity
3.0× monthly rate~95% continuity

People pay a ~33% premium to avoid being on continuity. Price accordingly based on how much upfront cash you need vs. how much recurring you want.

Status Titles as Continuity Bonuses

Silver / Gold / Diamond / Double Diamond status with milestone rewards. Place first milestone just past your highest-churn month. Make later milestones progressively further apart (video game pacing). One business found customers cared more about their title than any tangible bonus — "Hi, I'm Double Diamond."

Continuity 2 · Discount Offers

Give free time or a discount if they sign up and commit. Four ways to structure where the free time falls:

StructureCash ImpactChurn ImpactBest For
Front-loaded: Free months up front, contract starts after Delays cash Higher churn (less skin in game) Industries with enforcement (storage, cell, real estate)
Back-loaded: Free months earned after completing contract Full cash now Lower churn (they must earn it) Highest LTV play — customers earn their discount
Spread: Discount applied across all payments Some cash now Moderate Best of both worlds — advertise "3 months free," spread it over term
First + Last: Pay first and last month upfront, get free months Covers CAC Low (skin in game) Covers commissions + CAC before delivering free months
Contract Math Trap

When you offer 3 months free on a 12-month commitment, make the contract 12+3 = 15 months, NOT 9+3 = 12. Don't let the free time eat your commitment length. That's 33% more revenue for no additional work.

Three Continuity Payment Hacks

  • Bill every 4 weeks (not monthly): 13 billing cycles/year instead of 12 = 8.3% more revenue. Present as weekly price ($50/week), bill at $200/4 weeks. No measurable difference in take rates.
  • Add 3% processing fee: "X plus a 3% processing fee." Pair with: waive fee for second payment method. Now they give you an ACH/bank account as backup. Reduces involuntary churn dramatically.
  • ACH as second form of payment: Bank accounts change far less often than credit cards. ACH = lowest decline rates, lowest processing costs. Get card + ACH whenever possible.

Lifetime Discounts at Churn Points

Offer a lifetime lower rate earned after your highest-churn month. If average customer drops at month 4, put the lifetime discount at month 5. Tell them at months 2 and 3 that it's coming. This pulls people through the emotional low of cancellation with a tangible reward in sight.

Continuity 3 · Waived Fee Offer

Present a startup/onboarding fee (3–5× monthly rate). Then offer to wave it entirely if they commit to 12+ months. If they cancel inside the term, they pay the fee they would have paid month-to-month. "The cost of quitting exceeds the cost of staying."

Simple Formula
Fee = Monthly Rate × 5 · Waved with 12-month Commitment

The fee does double duty: gets them to start (to avoid paying it) and gets them to stick (to avoid paying it on exit). After they complete the commitment, the fee goes away permanently — this is what makes it fair.

Justification Script

"Whenever we onboard a new customer, it takes more resources than an existing one. So we charge a one-time setup fee to deliver the best experience. If you commit long-term, we'll cover those costs for you."

Build Your Money Model

Four steps. Start with one offer per stage. Perfect each before adding complexity.

  1. 1Pick your Attraction Offer. Choose one of the 6. The goal is simply to get leads to engage and become customers. Focus on outcome, not vehicle.
  2. 2Pick your Upsell Offer. The natural "next thing" after the attraction. Classic upsell is the easiest starting point (can't have X without Y). Target 80%+ uptake rate.
  3. 3Pick your Downsell Offer. Start with payment plans. Add feature downsells once you know what customers value most.
  4. 4Pick your Continuity Offer. Add last, after the other three generate cash. Downell the upsell — offer continuity at a lower monthly rate than the intensive.

Gym Launch Money Model (Real Example)

  • Attraction: Win Your Money Back (6-week challenge)
  • Upsell 1: Buy X Get Y Free (prepay months of service)
  • Upsell 2: Menu Upsell on supplements at nutrition consult
  • Upsell 3: Continuity Bonus (challenge gifted free with membership)
  • Downsell: Free Trial with Penalty → nutrition consult → supplement sale
  • Result: $60 revenue/show → $960 revenue/show (16× uplift)
Simple Scales

"Have as few of these as humanly possible because the fewer pieces in your business, the fewer things can break." Start with the simplest thing that accomplishes CFA. Scale only after it works reliably.

Attraction Offers

Dentiflow's primary attraction offer should remove all friction from the first "yes." The goal is to get a practice to experience the system working — once they see patients booking, the value is self-evident.

Decoy Offer (recommended)

Best Fit

Strong Option

Free with Consumption

Case study video + "here's exactly what we did at 32 Dental" as the lead content. Free education compresses trust. The case study IS the attraction — not a brochure.

The natural upsells for a dental recall system — things the practice will need anyway, they just have to decide whether to get them from Dentiflow or elsewhere:

Menu Upsell in Practice

When a practice is signing up, apply the four-step menu close. Unsell what they don't need ("You don't need the multi-location module right now — one location first"). Prescribe what they do need ("You'll want to add no-show recovery in month 2, here's exactly how you'd use it"). A/B close ("Would you want to start with recall-only, or recall + no-show recovery bundled?"). Card on file from day one for recurring billing.

Present a white-glove "done for you" tier first: full implementation + custom templates + monthly strategy calls + Dentrix integration at a premium price. If they bulk, step down to self-serve + support. Most take the middle. The anchor makes the mid-tier look like a deal — and a meaningful subset will buy the premium, which is where the margin is.

Continuity Structure

Dentiflow is inherently a continuity business. Patients need to be recalled monthly. The system doesn't stop working — practices don't churn unless you give them a reason to.

Continuity Bonus

Bonus for signing up: first campaign done-with-you (our team builds the first segment, imports, and launches together). This has real value and is something we'd do anyway — slap a price on it and give it free with the monthly subscription.

Waived Fee

Setup/onboarding fee of 3–5× monthly rate, waved with a 6-month commitment. Increases stickiness and fronts cash to cover early delivery costs. "If you commit to 6 months, I'll cover the setup."

Bill Every 4 Weeks

Not monthly. 13 billing cycles = 8.3% more revenue with no change in perceived value. Present as weekly ("$X/week") but bill at 4-week cadence.

Offer Stack to Build Toward

  1. Attraction: Free 100-text pilot (decoy) OR 30-day money-back guarantee on first recall campaign
  2. Classic Upsell: "You'll need no-show recovery — patients book and don't show without it" (80% uptake target)
  3. Menu Upsell: Unsell complexity, prescribe the right tier, A/B on billing cadence, card on file from day one
  4. Anchor: White-glove done-for-you tier at 5–10× base price. Gets the gasp. Sells more mid-tier. Some buy the anchor.
  5. Downsell: Payment plan first → feature downsell (drop no-show recovery → recall only at lower rate) → free trial with penalty if needed
  6. Continuity: Monthly subscription with waived onboarding fee on 6-month commit. Buy X Get Y Free on annual prepay.

The Rollover Play for New Practice Acquisition

Once there are practices to reference, the rollover upsell becomes a cold outreach tool: scrape practices that have tried and abandoned competitor recall software (check reviews of Weave, Lighthouse, RevenueWell). Approach with: "We'll credit whatever you paid for [competitor] toward your first 3 months with us. You don't lose a thing and you can see what a system that actually works looks like." This is the competitor's-upset-customers rollover — and it's a referral pipeline that compounds.

Case Study IS the Free-with-Consumption Offer

The 32 Dental case study video functions as a Free with Consumption attraction offer. The content delivers real value (proof it works, exactly how it was done). The pitch at the end: "This is what we built. We can build the same thing for you." The bigger the ticket practice (multi-location, DSO), the more content they need before buying — which validates investing in multiple case study assets.

Churn Prevention = LTV

Dentiflow's LTV is determined almost entirely by churn rate. A practice that stays 24 months at $800/month = $19,200. A practice that stays 6 months = $4,800. Every dollar spent improving results, reporting, and engagement reduces churn — and the compounding effect on LTV is massive. This is where to spend engineering time.

The Hormozi Principle Applied

Simple scales. The most important thing Dentiflow can do right now is not add more product — it's to get the existing money model working at one practice, prove CFA, and then replicate. One attraction offer. One upsell. One continuity term. Perfect it. Then add the next layer. The 4.9% conversion rate at 32 Dental is the proof. The next step is making that proof legible enough to close the second arms-length customer on a warm referral.
