Key Definitions

Lead

A person you can contact — they exist in your world as a name + phone/email.

Engaged Lead

A person who has shown interest in what you sell. They raised their hand.

Advertising

The process of making your offer known. Nothing more, nothing less. All four methods are just different ways of doing this.

The Core Equation
Double engaged leads → double revenue (everything else equal)
Hormozi's Origin Point

First Facebook ad ever: plain text, no image. "I'm looking for 5 Chino Hills residents to take part in a free six-week challenge…" $1,000 spend → $5,700 in sales. Lead gen doesn't have to be complicated.

Lead Magnets

A lead magnet is a complete solution to a narrow problem — given free or heavily discounted — that creates engaged leads from strangers. It solves one problem so well that it naturally reveals the next problem your core offer solves.

Three Types

Reveals a Problem

Diagnosis, audit, assessment. Shows them something is wrong that they didn't know about. Creates urgency.

Example: "Free website speed audit" → your site is slow → buy our optimization service.

Free Trial / Sample

A taste of the real thing. Once they've experienced value, they want more.

Example: Free 6-week gym challenge. After 6 weeks of progress, they want to keep going — you sell the year.

One Step of Many

Solve step 1 for free. The natural question becomes "what's step 2?" — which you sell.

Example: Free keyword research → they need you to write the content.

Four Delivery Mechanisms

  • Software — tool, calculator, dashboard, free tier
  • Information — PDF, video, guide, masterclass, webinar
  • Service — free consultation, done-for-you sample, audit
  • Physical product — free + shipping, sample kit

Three Rules for a Good Lead Magnet

  1. Name it well — the title IS the offer. Test variants. Specificity wins. "Lose 10 lbs in 30 days" beats "weight loss tips."
  2. Make it easy to consume — offer multiple formats (video + PDF + audio). Friction = drop-off.
  3. Make it genuinely good — give away the secrets, sell the implementation. If it's mediocre, they'll assume your paid product is worse.
Always Attach a CTA

Every lead magnet must end with a call to action, ideally with scarcity or urgency. "You got the free thing — here's the next step. Only X spots left."

The Right Order

Start with warm outreach (fastest revenue). Add cold outreach (scalable, reliable). Build content (compounds over time). Run paid ads last (requires data + cash flow from the first three). Each prior step gives you avatar data that makes paid ads cheaper to learn.

The Rule of 100

Do 100 primary actions per day for 100 days straight in the method you've chosen. 100 outreaches/day. 100 minutes of content/day. $100/day in ads. This is the baseline, not the ceiling.

Warm Outreach 1:1 · Warm

The fastest way to get your first 5 customers. You have more warm contacts than you think. This is step zero for any business.

The 5-Step Warm Outreach System

  1. 1
    Build the list — every person you know: past clients, friends, family, former colleagues, social media followers, anyone in your phone contacts. No filter yet.
  2. 2
    Pick one platform — wherever they are. Don't spread across all of them to start.
  3. 3
    Warm them up — engage genuinely for a few days before pitching. Like, comment, react to their content.
  4. 4
    Reach out with the ACA Framework — Acknowledge (what they're up to), Compliment (genuine, specific), Ask (one question to move the conversation forward). Never lead with your pitch.
  5. 5
    Offer the lead magnet — when it's natural, offer the free thing. Then ask: "Do you know anyone who might benefit from this?" Referrals compound warm outreach.

The Pricing Ramp

Start free (get 5 case studies). Then raise price 20% every 5 clients. Your confidence and proof both increase; so does what you can charge. Never stay free once you have results.

The Hinge Method (Referrals)

3-way intro: "Hey [friend], I want to connect you with [prospect] — I think you'd both get value from talking." You're the hinge. Works because both parties are already pre-sold via the relationship.

The Pre-Play-Post Guarantee Tactic

  • Pre: Tell them what you're going to do and what they'll get.
  • Play: Do it. Deliver.
  • Post: Recap what you just did and the result. Then ask for the next step.

This builds perceived likelihood of achievement at each stage and makes close rates much higher.

Content: Creation 1:Many · Warm

Content is warm outreach at scale — it keeps creating engaged leads while you sleep. The goal is building an audience of people who know, like, and trust you before you ever pitch them.

The Hook / Retain / Reward Framework

Hook

Gets attention. The headline, thumbnail, first sentence, or first frame. If you don't win here, nothing else matters. Spend 80% of your effort on the hook.

Retain

Keeps them watching/reading. Deliver value continuously. Every line sells the next line.

Reward

Pays off the hook's promise. Leaves them feeling like they got something real — and primed for your CTA.

5 Topic Sources

SourceWhat It IsExample
Far pastLife lessons, formative experiences"The mistake I made at 22 that cost me 3 years"
Recent pastCalendar: meetings, decisions, observations"What I learned from yesterday's sales call"
PresentReal-time capture — what you're doing right now"I'm mid-launch right now — here's what's working"
TrendsYour expert take on what's happening in the industry"Why the new FTC rules will kill most supplement brands"
ManufacturedDeliberate experimentation to create content"I tested 100 cold call openers — here's the winner"

7 Headline Characteristics That Drive Clicks

A strong headline uses at least two of these: Recent (news hook) · Relevant (matters to them) · Celebrity (name drops) · Proximity (local/specific) · Conflict (tension/controversy) · Unusual (surprising/counterintuitive) · Ongoing (serialized, creates anticipation)

Three Retention Structures

  • Lists — "7 ways to…" Scannable, completable, easy to consume.
  • Steps — Sequential process. Each step creates desire for the next.
  • Stories — Narrative arc. Highest retention when done well.
Critical Rule

Make all content for strangers. If your audience already knows you, it should still make sense to someone who has never heard of you. This forces clarity and makes content shareable.

Two Ask Methods

Integrated Ask

CTA woven into a value piece. "If this helped, you'll love [thing]." Keeps give:ask ratio high. Use most often.

Intermittent Ask

Occasional full-sell piece. Direct pitch. Lowers ratio temporarily — use sparingly (once every 4–10 pieces).

Depth vs Width Scaling

Depth First, Then Width

Go deep on one platform, build a loyal audience, monetize well — then expand to more platforms with the same content. Lower risk. Hormozi's preference for new businesses.

Width First, Then Depth

Cross-post everywhere early to find which platform resonates — then double down on the winner. Faster feedback, more diluted effort.

7 Content Lessons (Hard Won)

  1. How-to → How-I — "How to lose weight" is generic. "How I lost 30 lbs while running a company" is a story with specific proof.
  2. Repeat yourself — Your best ideas need to be heard 5–7 times before they land. Say them in different ways.
  3. Puddles → Ponds → Lakes → Oceans — Start niche, expand audience as you master the niche.
  4. Content creates sales tools — Your best posts become your best sales scripts. The market writes your pitch deck for you.
  5. Free content retains paying customers — Existing customers who consume your free content churn less. It's an asset, not just acquisition.
  6. No shorter attention spans — People don't have shorter attention spans. They have higher standards. Make better content.
  7. Don't pre-schedule — Hormozi records when inspired, posts when contextually relevant. Pre-scheduled content loses timing edge.
Data Point

78% of Gym Launch customers consumed 2+ pieces of long-form content before buying. Content doesn't just acquire — it pre-sells at scale.

Hormozi's Production Cadence

Records ~2 days/month. Output: ~30 short clips + 2–4 long-form pieces. The long-form generates the shorts. Batching creates consistency without daily effort.

Cold Outreach 1:1 · Cold

Reaching out to strangers who have never heard of you. Harder than warm, but powerful because it's private (competition can't see it), reliable (no algorithm), stable (no platform risk), and makes your business more sellable.

The 3 Problems with Strangers

Can't contact them

Fix: Build the list. Scrape (software), buy (brokers), or elbow grease (manually from Facebook groups, LinkedIn, directories).

Ignore you

Fix: Personalize + lead with huge fast value (your lead magnet). Scripts below 3rd-grade reading level get 50% more responses.

Not interested

Fix: Better targeting. More specific list. Wrong audience = not fixable by better copy. Fix the list first.

The Voicemail Script

"Hey [First Name], it's [Your Name], calling in reference to [competitor / industry thing they care about]. Give me a call back at [number]."

Pattern interrupt. No sell. Creates curiosity. Works because they don't know it's a cold call.

Cold Outreach Mechanics

Follow-upMost sales happen on follow-up 4–7. Follow up more times in more ways.

Paid Ads: Making Ads 1:Many · Cold

Paid ads interrupt strangers' content. You pay the platform (Facebook, Google, YouTube, etc.) to rent their audience. Reach is guaranteed — the question is efficiency (return vs. cost).

Mindset Shift

All ads work. The question is never "do paid ads work?" It's "how efficiently can they work for me?" This is entirely a solvable optimization problem.

The 4 Problems Paid Ads Must Solve

  1. Where to advertise (which platform)
  2. Who sees the ad (targeting)
  3. What the ad says (creative)
  4. How to capture their information (landing page)

1. Where to Advertise

Pick a platform where all 4 are true:

  • You've used it as a consumer (understand the format)
  • You can target your ideal customer there
  • You know how to format an ad for it
  • Minimum spend is accessible to you
Best Starting Point

Place ads where your competitors do. Replicate before you iterate. Find ads that have run the longest with the most engagement — those are proven. Study the ad library (Facebook, TikTok, Google all have them). Transcribe 50 high-performing ads. Let them swirl. Write your own version.

2. Targeting

Lookalike Audiences

Upload your warm outreach list + cold outreach list to the platform. It finds people who "look like" your list at scale. Better list = better lookalike. Customer list > opt-in list > cold list.

Demographic / Interest Filters

Layer on age, gender, income, interests, geography. Add filters, but not too many. Narrow is usually more efficient — until you hit your constraint.

Local Business Insight

Local ads are among the easiest to run. A 2–3 mile radius is already hyper-targeted. Don't add more than 1 demographic filter. Local trust = built-in credibility (people recognize the street name). You barely need to sell — just get them to opt in and call them.

3. What the Ad Says — The 3-Part Structure

Call Out (Hook)

Gets their attention. 80% of your effort goes here. If you don't win the first 5 seconds, nothing else matters.

Value (Retain)

Why they should care. Uses the 8-element value framework below. Makes benefits as large as possible, costs as small as possible.

Call to Action (Reward)

Exactly what to do next. Spell it out: click this button, call this number, go to this website. Clear > clever.

Call Out Types

TypeHow It WorksExample
LabelsPut them in a group they identify with"Clark County homeowners" / "Gym owners in Colorado"
Yes QuestionsImplied yes answers that make them nod"Do you wake up with cotton mouth? Do you toss and turn at night?"
If-Then StatementsConditional qualification"If you run over $10,000/month in ads, I can save you 20%+"
Ridiculous ResultsBizarre/rare outcome that creates curiosity"Massage studio booked out 2 years in advance — clients furious"

Non-Verbal Call Outs

  • Contrast — Bright colors, movement, sound sticks out from the feed.
  • Likeness — People whose features and language match the target avatar. If you want diverse customers, use diverse faces in ads.
  • Scene — Visually show what the yes question implies. Person tossing and turning in bed = sleep problem ad. Local landmark = local trust.

The Value Framework: What × Who × When

Three dimensions multiplied together give you unlimited unique ad angles from a single offer.

WHAT — The 8 Value Elements

Positive (Toward)Negative (Away From)
Dream outcomeNightmare (life without it)
Perceived likelihood of achievementRisk (chance it won't work)
SpeedTime delay (how slow current path is)
Ease (low skill/effort needed)Sacrifice (what they'll give up without it)

WHO — Status Perspectives

Show each "what" element from the perspective of every person whose opinion the customer cares about: spouse, kids, boss, colleagues, rivals, friends, parents. "Your wife notices you pulling your belt two notches tighter" hits differently than "you lost 20 lbs."

WHEN — Timeline

Show past (how they used to feel / what they've missed out on), present (their situation right now), and future (what their life looks like with/without your solution). Negative future visualization is especially powerful.

Unlimited Angles
8 value elements × N status perspectives × 3 timelines = unlimited unique ads

Hook Split-Testing System

Record 10 pieces of core value content. Record 30 different hooks separately. Paste each hook onto each value piece.

Hormozi's System
10 value pieces × 30 hooks = 300 ad variations to test
Why You Can't Scale

The top 0.1% of Facebook advertisers test 11× more creative than everyone else. Your ads don't scale because they're not good enough — not because the platform capped you. Make more volume. The platform wants your money; it hasn't capped you. Your creative quality has.

4. Landing Pages

Three layouts that work. Pick one and match it exactly to your ad (same colors, words, face, promise):

  • Headline + sub-headline + form
  • Headline + sub-headline + image + form
  • Headline + sub-headline + image + 3 bullets + form

More steps = fewer leads but more qualified. Fewer steps = more leads but less qualified. Adjust based on your conversion problem.

Short Domain Value

80% of acquisition.com traffic is people hand-typing the URL. Hormozi paid $370K for the domain. Short, memorable domains are systematically underpriced. The memorability compounds over time.

Paid Ads: Money & Metrics 1:Many · Cold

The 3 Phases of Ad Spending

Phase 1: Track

Set up attribution before spending a single dollar. Without tracking you're blind. Running ads without tracking is placing casino bets without knowing the odds.

Phase 2: Lose

Expect to lose first. Of 300 ads, ~295 won't win. But the 5 winners will make up for all of them — especially the 1 home run. Do NOT stop when you find a winner and you're still net-negative. That's the mistake.

Phase 3: Print

Once you make more than you spend — spend as much as physically possible. There is no budget. A magic money machine that turns $1 into $2 — how much do you put in?

The Master Metric: LTGP:CAC

Key Definitions
LTGP = Lifetime Gross Profit per customer (not revenue — subtract COGS)
The Ratio That Matters
LTGP ÷ CAC ≥ 3 : 1 = scalable · ≥ 10 : 1 = exceptional
RatioMeaningAction
LTGP < CACLosing money per customerFix business model or targeting immediately
1:1 to 3:1Profitable but slow to recoupAdd upsells to speed up cash cycle
3:1+Home run — scalableScale aggressively
10:1+Exceptional (Hormozi's target)Reinvest everything; dominate
Hormozi's Lifetime Numbers

Personal lifetime LTGP:CAC = 36:1. Target for portfolio companies: 10:1 minimum. Some portfolio companies running 20:1+. The benchmark is high because the business model work came first.

Critical: Gross Profit ≠ Revenue

A business making $500/customer LTV with 10% gross margins has $50 LTGP. Spending $100 to acquire them = losing money, even though LTV looks fine. Always work from gross profit, not top-line revenue.

Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA)

The goal: break even or better on Day 1, so you can reinvest immediately and compound — instead of waiting 3–6 months to recoup CAC.

The Compound Math
2:1 return Day 1 → reinvest → 4× → 8× → 16× → 32× in 5 months

How to Get to Day-1 Break Even

  • Add an upsell that 15–25% of customers take immediately after they buy
  • The upsell revenue offsets CAC without touching LTV from recurring purchases
  • Result: you get free customers, and all recurring revenue is pure profit from day one

Upsell Framework: More / Better / New

  • More — more of what they just bought (bigger quantity, more sessions)
  • Better — premium version of the same thing (VIP tier, done-for-you)
  • New — complementary product (fries with the burger, Coke with the fries)

Diagnosing What's Broken

SymptomDiagnosisFix

Ads running, wrong people showing upTargeting problemRefine list, change audience filters
Right people, won't scheduleOffer problem or landing page problemBetter CTA, simpler form, stronger offer

The Learning Budget Mindset

Expect to lose money for 6–12 months before the paid ads machine "clicks." The money spent during that time is tuition for the skill of client acquisition — a skill worth millions once mastered. The business owner who quit at $150K spent = was 6 inches from a $30M enterprise value unlock.

Budget Reverse Engineering

Know your max capacity? Reverse into your budget.

Example: Can handle 20 new customers/month × $100 CAC = $2,000/month budget ($67/day).

Fix delivery constraint → raise capacity → raise budget. The delivery bottleneck, not the ad budget, is often the real limit.

Scaling: More / Better / New

Once you've validated any advertising method, this is how you systematically grow it. Always exhaust the lower-leverage options before jumping to the next level.

The Sequence
More → Better → New (then repeat at each new level)

More

The simplest question in business: what's stopping you from doing 10× more of what's already working? Most businesses have headroom here they've never explored.

The Rule of 100

100 primary actions per day, 100 days in a row. For each channel:

  • Warm/cold outreach: 100 messages or calls per day
  • Content: 100 minutes of content creation per day
  • Paid ads: $100 per day (or scale proportionally)
Before $1M/yr Annual Profit

Just do more. The business doesn't have enough volume for percentage improvements to make a meaningful difference yet. Raw volume is the lever.

Better

Get more leads from the same effort. The only way to get better is testing — one variable at a time.

The Testing Protocol

  • One test per week per platform
  • Reason 1: Understand what worked (single variable = clear causation)
  • Reason 2: Split tests affect each other (more leads may be less qualified)
  • Reason 3: Forces prioritization of highest-impact changes
  • Reason 4: Long enough sample for statistical significance (use a significance calculator)

What to Test First: Front-to-Back Priority

The earliest steps in your funnel have the lowest conversion percentages — meaning tiny improvements have the largest absolute impact. Fix the call-out before the landing page headline. Fix the headline before the form.

Gym Launch Example
40% growth in 18 months by testing 1% improvements — no new channels, no new methods
After $1M/yr Annual Profit

Flip the order: Better → More → New. At this scale, percentage improvements compound on larger volume. A 1% CTR improvement on $100K/month spend is worth more than adding 10% more volume.

New

After you've maxed "more" and "better" on your current approach, expand. But most businesses think they've maxed out long before they actually have.

The Fallacy of "Tapped Market"

"I've tapped my market" almost always means "I've tapped one channel on one platform." A $2M/year agency owner told Hormozi he'd tapped the chiropractic market. Hormozi Googled it live: $10–15B industry. If any competitor can still acquire clients in your space, you haven't tapped the market — you just haven't learned their methods yet.

Building a Team (Lead-Getter Employees)

Employees don't just deliver your product. Lead-getter employees do the Core Four for you. This is how you go from a high-paying job to a business.

The Wealthy Owner

Training: Document → Demonstrate → Duplicate

  1. 1
    Document — Film yourself doing the task. Build a checklist from what you observe, not from memory of doing it. The video becomes the training asset.
  2. 2
    Demonstrate — Do it in front of them. They observe. Real-time modeling is more effective than written instructions alone.
  3. 3
    Duplicate — They do it in front of you. You give feedback. Repeat until they can self-critique. That's mastery.
Cut Fast

If someone is properly trained and still below expectations after 3 weeks — cut them. Keeping underperformers tanks team culture and signals to your A players that standards don't matter.

Calculating Employee ROI

Lead-Getter Cost Per Lead
Total payroll ÷ engaged leads generated = cost per engaged lead
Employee-Based CAC
Cost per engaged lead × leads per customer = true CAC (same benchmark: <1/3 of LTGP)

The 3× Industry Average Diagnostic

If your CAC is more than 3× the industry average, you have a sales or advertising problem. Diagnose with one question:

"Do my engaged leads have the problem I solve AND the money to spend?"

  • No → Advertising problem (wrong audience)
  • Yes, but not buying → Sales problem (can't close)
  • Yes, but not enough of them → Advertising problem (insufficient volume)

Buy vs Build Talent

Build early: Train frontline people. Lower cost, takes time. Worth it while you're learning the role yourself.

Buy as you scale: Director-level and up, buy experienced people. A players are worth 5× B players. Pay 25% above market for 5× output — one of the best ROI investments in any business.
