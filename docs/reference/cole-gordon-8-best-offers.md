# Cole Gordon × Daniel Fazio — The 8 Best Offers They've Seen ($50–100M+)

**Source:** "I Studied 5,000+ Offers. These Are The 8 BEST ($50-100M+)" — Cole Gordon podcast with Daniel Fazio (Client Ascension / ListKit), YouTube (1:29:42), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XQ4hr48ikwY
**Ingested:** 2026-07-07 (full transcript). Timestamps are approximate.
**Format:** Gordon breaks down nuclear offers he's seen at $2M–$15M/month; Fazio covers the most reliable beginner offers ($0 → $250k/mo). The cross-cutting principles at the end are the reusable part.

## Gordon's nuclear offers ($1M–$15M/month)

### 1. Timeshare-exit law firm — $10–15M/mo (06:50–12:15)
Why it works (his canonical three reasons, repeated across the episode):
1. **"$20 bill for $1":** the timeshare cost is a SUNK, certain future expense. "Pay $10k now, save the $20k you were definitely going to spend." Not a promise of future results — an automatic saving. **"Leaky bucket" offers beat inflated-claim offers.**
2. **Barrier to entry:** you must be a law firm. No 20-year-olds ripping the ad.
3. **Self-qualifying problem:** by the nature of the problem, everyone who responds has money (they bought a timeshare; they're boomers — the wealthy cohort). The offer itself sets the wealth floor.
- Bonus in the same family: immigration/expat attorneys (Nomad Capitalist had a 3,000-person waitlist purely from YouTube brand).

### 2. Health coaching / functional medicine — $4–10M/mo (20:20–28:10)
- Multiple clients above $4M/mo; all of them priced ~$10k — **the highest price in the industry. Every category king he knows is the most expensive in their niche.** Pricing flywheel: higher price → better clients → better margins → better salespeople/fulfillment → higher conversions → more profit (compounding).
- John Mattson ($4M/mo): call funnel + messaging that **gut-punches pains only wealthy prospects resonate with** ("your Rolex doesn't look good hanging over your gut") — self-segments high-net-worth from broke within a big market.
- The $10M/mo one: **low-ticket ascension funnel** — webinar sells a cheap at-home test kit; reading your results requires attending the sales call (huge show rates; the diagnostic IS the discovery process; "the best discovery process in the world is having the prospect's blood work"). Compliance-wrapped (MD oversight).
- **Brand as a horizontal enabling layer (23:50–25:40):** a famous founder's objectively mediocre ads outperform, because the ad is really the prospect's 13th–14th impression — "retargeting without retargeting." Brand's main value isn't organic leads; it's making ALL paid acquisition cheaper.

### 3. UGC agency pivoted to home service — $1M/mo, very high margin (30:05–36:00)
- Started selling UGC creative to the coaching/agency industry, **pivoted to home service (remodeling, HVAC, solar) and took off.**
- Why: home-service owners have real businesses, money/credit, intent — and marketing is NOT their core skill or interest, so **churn is structurally low** (they never take it in-house; coaches/marketers always eventually do).
- **Productized deliverable = "selling oil, not the finished car":** you deliver the asset; performance responsibility stays with the client's business. Much more stable.
- Upside layers: rev-share deals with winning clients ($10–20k/mo contracts); kept the team lean (avoided "playing business" / mastermind-driven overhiring — "who not how" often just means throw money at it).

### 4. Medical device via call funnel — $4M/mo (45:50–50:10)
- **Differentiation via acquisition channel, not offer structure:** took the standard call-funnel/ads meta from the internet-marketing industry into an industry that sells via field reps and trade shows. $10 MQLs for a $10k product at launch (another client: $50–100k/mo → $3M/mo in 14 months this way; a legislation change had just made advertising legal — nobody else was doing it yet).
- $70k financeable product → **high price point means you hit $4M/mo without breaking the hard 6→10+ sales-rep scaling barrier.**
- Barrier to entry + vertical integration (bought the manufacturer) + patents → real enterprise value, big exit multiple, no charismatic founder required.

### 5. Eddie Maloof's agency (BAD Marketing) — ~$2–4M/mo (54:40–58:40)
- **Horizontal scaling via acquisition/launch of adjacent agencies** (email, Amazon, TikTok Shop, volume-tier) that cross-sell one client base. Hard to push ONE agency offer past $1M/mo; stacking $400–600k/mo offers is much easier. Cross-sell also buffers churn (they churn Facebook ads but keep email).
- Partnerships with info/creator brands on rev-share; takes 10–20% equity in select clients in exchange for services, scaled with existing infrastructure.
- **When to go horizontal ("new" in Hormozi's more-better-new):** when the value chain is ~90% maxed (only incremental gains left) AND you're 10–30x your next competitor.

### 6. Virtual family office / tax strategy — $10–30M/yr, some on pure referrals (1:02:16–1:07:05)
1. **$20-bill-for-$1 again:** tax savings are found money against a certain bill — client makes back the fee 20x/yr.
2. **Switching costs compound:** once they hold your K-1s, trusts, asset protection, insurance, deal flow — "where would I even start if I wanted to leave?" Nobody churns after year one.
3. **Self-qualifying:** nobody frets over tax strategy without a big tax bill; a big tax bill means high income. The problem callout does the qualification.
- One firm went ~$2M → $20M/yr with **hosted dinners as the entire marketing strategy** (clients bring friends, city to city) — network marketing built into a high-trust category.
- Key ad-platform insight (1:04:45): **Facebook always optimizes toward the cheapest = brokest lead.** Unless the offer/messaging sets a floor, you get the bottom of any market. Self-qualifying problems ARE the floor.

### 7. Committed Coaches — $10–15M/mo (1:10:41–1:13:20)
- Pure sales/high-ticket-program company that **partners 50/50 with direct-response brands** (supplements, info, fitness products doing $200–400M/yr on ~20% margins) that have huge low-ticket buyer lists and NO back end.
- They run a ~100-person commission-only sales floor over those buyer lists, selling a $3–5k coaching program, split 50/50.
- Recruiting flywheel: pays overrides to famous sales trainers (Matt Ryder, Eli Wilde) for reps recruited from their audiences, plus those trainers coach inside the community — reps come for the coaching, stay for the overrides.
- Meta-lesson: **monetize someone else's dead asset (buyer list) with your capability (sales floor) on a rev split** — zero CAC, instant scale.

### 8. Daniel Isles (Viral Coach) — done-for-you content, $3–5M/mo (1:21:02–1:24:10)
- Started selling DFY content to coaches/course creators; **the shift that 5x'd him: selling the same service to traditional businesses** (nationwide financial-adviser firms etc.).
- Traditional owners: know they need content, have money, want NOTHING to do with doing it → **price went from ~$10k (coaches can barely afford) to ~$36k+, LTV way higher, churn way lower, broader ad audience.**
- General law (1:23:20): selling marketing services to marketing-inclined people is the hardest sale ("I could do that myself"); selling to owners who just want the outcome is stickier and pays 3x more.

## Fazio's beginner offers ($0 → $250k/mo)

1. **Performance-based cold-email lead gen (00:47–06:30):** "$500/mo tech fee + $300 per booked call." Easiest first-client offer ever (no ad spend required from client, no credibility needed). Cold email itself: works only with high-LTV clients; anything that works on cold email works on ads (reverse not true); needs DFY + guarantee + social proof. Proof stacking ("PS — 30 famous clients") took response from 1% → 3%.
2. **E-comm email as a one-time setup (13:32–15:00):** "We'll set up 52 emails across 9 automated flows for a ONE-TIME payment. No retainers" (the anti-retainer angle is the hook in a market of thousands of retainer agencies). On the call: "$4,000 one-time — or take the $4,000/mo recurring service and the setup is FREE." **~80% take the recurring. Client hit $500k+/mo on this flip.**
3. **Static ad creatives pegged to a trend (28:14–29:30):** "10 new static creatives per day optimized for Meta Andromeda." $5k/mo clients at ~$1k CAC. When the whole market is panicking about one named change, saying you have THE solution to that named thing rips. Fulfillment is mostly AI.
4. **DFY cold calling (40:11–45:47):** working because it's the mirror image of cold email's death — cold email got cheap → supply exploded → results collapsed; cold calling is **logistically brutal (hiring/managing callers) → almost nobody sells it → supply is near zero → results are huge** (5–10 meetings/mo on email vs 50–60 on calls). Same CAC as his cold-email offer but 3x the price ($6–12k/mo). Expansion revenue built in: an 80-rep client pilots with 5 reps → expands. **"I want it to be hard continuously" — logistical intensity IS the moat.**
5. **AI UGC / animated ads (1:00:00–1:02:10):** clearly-AI creative (claymation skits etc.) that converts for clients; sold by just POSTING the work — client has a 2-week waitlist collecting $6–8k payments in DMs, no sales calls.
   - Related (50:12–53:40): **AI automation agencies — outbound "we automate your business" is 0%; posting actual builds on X/YouTube prints inbound** ("could you build that for me?"). Two clients at $200–300k/mo purely from posting builds. If starting from zero: do free builds for real businesses in exchange for the right to document and post them. For ads: pick ONE specific painful process (e.g. healthcare compliance notes), show the build, attach a guarantee.
6. **In-person content agencies (1:17:57–1:21:00):** physically showing up with cameras, filming, editing, posting — client does literally nothing. **Transferring logistical intensity from client to you is what they pay for:** a zero-authority beginner can close $5–7k/mo in-person more easily than $3k/mo remote. Constraint: geography + your time; graduates into hiring crews.
7. **Reddit agency (1:07:08–1:10:30):** thread-planting / reputation management / AI-answer seeding (what ChatGPT says about you is downstream of Reddit). Sold via cold email promising X mentions; the inbound flip side is removal work. Noted as against Reddit ToS — recorded here as market intel, not a recommendation.

## Cross-cutting principles (the reusable layer)

1. **$20-bill-for-$1 beats inflated claims.** Recover certain, already-lost money rather than promising uncertain future gains (timeshare, tax, Hyros's "your tracking is wasting 30–40% of ad spend", dial.io's "pickup rates 9% → 20% = found money").
2. **The best offers self-qualify.** Facebook optimizes to the brokest lead in any market; the problem you call out must be one only funded prospects have. (Timeshare, tax bills, "have a sales team", "have 300+ dormant patients".)
3. **Moats: barrier to entry OR logistical intensity.** If anyone can wake up and copy your offer, margins die. Being a law firm, owning a device patent, managing cold callers, physically showing up — all keep the 20-year-olds out.
4. **Highest price = category king, every time.** Pricing is the initial condition that sets the whole flywheel (clients, margins, talent, conversions, LTV).
5. **Port the meta.** Take the acquisition playbook from a sophisticated industry (call funnels, VSLs, setters) into an unsophisticated one (medical devices, home service, financial advisers) — differentiation by distribution, not just offer structure.
6. **Sell to owners who DON'T want to do it themselves.** Marketing-inclined buyers churn into DIY; traditional owners pay more and stay for years.
7. **One-time / no-retainer as the ad hook, recurring as the close.** Advertise the one-time setup; on the call, waive it into the subscription. ~80% take recurring.
8. **Engineer switching costs.** The more of the client's data/history/infrastructure you hold, the less churn is even conceivable.
9. **Expansion revenue by design.** Land a slice (5 of 80 reps; one location; 200 patients), prove it, expand to the whole.
10. **Guarantee reality (18:48):** guarantees don't cause refund waves; their job is getting people on the phone, not closing. State terms up front (ethics + they'll re-see the ad after buying).
11. **Cold-traffic-viable offers work even BETTER warm** (1:24:12). If it converts strangers, brand multiplies it. Some things only sell warm (1:1 coaching with a creator, events, courses) — audience relationships change the dynamic.
12. **Paid challenges/workshops (1:26:10–1:29:15):** Gordon's most profitable channel ever. ~500 × $67 tickets monthly (half ads, half list), 4-day virtual event, ~10% upsell to a $10–12k offer. B2B version: $800 cost-per-purchase on cold looked scary until the intake forms showed nearly every buyer was 30k+/mo and perfectly qualified — **the paid ticket is the qualification.**
13. **Don't overhire ("playing business").** People at $200–400k/mo with C-suites and no profit. Stay lean; delete roles until you delete too much, then you know the line.
14. **Why Gordon avoids B2C bizop (1:13:36–1:17:50):** call-funnel B2C margins < B2B (weak LTV:CAC, no post-purchase spending power), FTC/compliance risk scales with size, zero enterprise value, and fulfillment promises require prospects to make a HUGE leap (first-client-ever) vs a small step (add 2 reps to a working team). **Sell small steps to working businesses, not big leaps to beginners.**

## Dentiflow application notes (2026-07-07)

Mapped against the **current live offer** — the 200-Patient AI Reactivation Pilot, $1,250 one-time → $499/mo continuity, money-back guarantee. **No free offer** — [docs/gtm/ads/dentiflow-ai-reactivation-ads.md](../gtm/ads/dentiflow-ai-reactivation-ads.md) supersedes offer-stack-stage1.md.

**The pilot already embodies the big ones:**
- **$20-bill-for-$1:** dormant patients are sunk CAC sitting in the PMS; "5 bookings × ~$300 = $1,500 against a $1,250 pilot — it pays for itself or it's free" is exactly the found-money structure. Lead with recovered money, never "growth."
- **Self-qualification:** "300+ dormant patients" can only be true of an established producing practice, and the $1,250 fee is a hard wealth floor Facebook can't optimize under (principle #2 + #12 — paying is the qualification).
- **Client-financed acquisition:** one pilot/month funds the ad budget (principle from the challenge/workshop economics).
- **One-time → recurring flip:** pilot fee + rollover credit into $499/mo continuity = Fazio's setup-flip. Consider making the rollover the DEFAULT close ("take continuity and the pilot was free"), not a fallback.
- **Small step, not big leap (#14):** we add bookings to an already-working practice — the highest-probability fulfillment promise there is.
- **Port the meta (#5):** Dentiflow IS the internet-marketing meta (two-way SMS, AI reply handling, attribution) applied to an unsophisticated vertical. This is the thesis; keep choosing unsophisticated buyers.

**To apply next:**
1. **Target the owner who wants nothing to do with it (#6):** the 50-something traditional practice owner beats the marketing-savvy young dentist — pays more, churns less. Creative and qualifying questions should select for "I know I need this, I don't want to touch it."
2. **Moat = logistical transfer (#3):** today the practice only exports a CSV + approves copy. When Ascend API access matures, pull the export FOR them — each task removed justifies price and deepens the moat. Long-term moat is the reply-safety + compliance machinery (TCPA, intent classification, validator) that a GHL-snippet copycat can't replicate.
3. **Switching costs (#8):** attribution history, message-performance data, per-practice learnings accumulate in the dashboard. Say it in the continuity close: "the longer it runs, the smarter your recall gets."
4. **Expansion revenue (#9):** pilot 200 patients → whole list ($499/mo) → sister locations → DSO groups (Scott's Familia background = credibility). Blocked until multi-location isolation ships — wire that before any non-Village recall.
5. **Price up after proof (#4):** $1,250/$499 is proving-ground pricing. Category-king move once case studies stack: raise continuity, add per-module tiers, and/or a performance component (per booked patient / rev-share — the UGC agency's upside layer).
6. **Trend-pegging (#Fazio-3):** when a named disruption hits dental (insurance reimbursement changes, PMS platform shifts, AI-answer visibility), an offer explicitly pegged to THE named thing converts disproportionately. Keep a hook in reserve.
7. **Skip for now:** paid challenges/workshops (needs a public founder — conflicts with the SourceClub visibility constraint) and anything Reddit-planting-shaped (ToS/ethics; noted as intel only).
