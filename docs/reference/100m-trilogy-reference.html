What Is a Grand Slam Offer?

A Grand Slam Offer creates a category of one — it cannot be compared to any other product or service available. It combines an attractive promotion, an unmatchable value proposition, a premium price, and an unbeatable guarantee with a money model that allows you to get paid to acquire new customers.

No offer

No business, no life.

Bad offer

Negative profit, miserable life.

Good offer

Some profit, okay business.

Grand Slam Offer

Fantastic profit, insane business, freedom.

Three effects: Increased response rates · Higher conversion · Premium prices — all without changing your fulfillment.

Finding the Right Market — A Starving Crowd

The Hot Dog Stand Rule

You could have the worst hot dogs, terrible prices, and a terrible location — but if you're the only stand when the college football game breaks out, you sell out. Market > Offer Strength > Persuasion Skills.

Four Criteria for a Good Market

  1. Massive pain — degree of pain is proportional to the price you can charge. "The pain is the pitch." If you can articulate a prospect's pain accurately, they'll almost always buy.
  2. Purchasing power — they must be able to afford what you charge. Unemployed people with resume problems can't pay.
  3. Easy to target — associations, mailing lists, Facebook groups. If you can't find them, a great offer falls on deaf ears.
  4. Growing — growing markets are tailwinds. Lloyd sold great software to newspapers (a market shrinking 25%/year). Everything failed.

Charge What It's Worth

"Charge as high a price as you can say out loud without cracking a smile." Higher price → higher client investment → better results → more referrals → higher prices. The virtuous cycle only works if you start it.

Lower Prices

  • Lower perceived value
  • Worse client results (less invested)
  • Attracts worst clients
  • No margin to improve
  • Business withers

Higher Prices

  • Higher perceived value
  • Better results (more adherent)
  • Attracts ideal clients
  • More margin to reinvest
  • Business compounds

The wine study: same wine at three price tags, subjects consistently rated the "expensive" wine best — and neurologically experienced more pleasure. Raising your price changes value delivered without changing the product.

The Value Equation

The Value Equation
Value = Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement Perceived Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice

Increase the numerator. Drive the denominator toward zero. If the denominator reaches zero, value approaches infinity.

Dream Outcome ↑

The experience in their ideal future. People buy to increase status. Frame benefits in terms of status gained from others' perspectives.

Likelihood ↑

How confident are they that they specifically will get the result? Build through track record, testimonials, guarantees, proof.

Time Delay ↓

Gap between purchase and value. Give a "big emotional win" within 7 days. Fast beats free — FedEx, Uber, Spotify won by being faster, not cheaper.

Effort & Sacrifice ↓

Every ancillary cost. Fitness = 10 hrs/week + hunger + soreness. Liposuction = fall asleep, wake up thin. Same outcome, orders-of-magnitude different prices.

Creating the Grand Slam Offer

Offer creation requires divergent thinking (many possible answers) not convergent thinking (one right answer). Generate possibilities first; edit later.

  1. 1Identify the Dream Outcome. Stop selling the product; sell the destination. "Lose 20 lbs in 6 weeks" not "gym membership." You're selling the vacation, not the plane flight.
  2. 2List Every Problem. Every obstacle a prospect faces before, during, and after using your product. For each step they must take, list every reason they might fail using the four value drivers. Aim for 32–64+ problems — more problems = more opportunities to provide value.
  3. 3Turn Problems into Solutions. For each problem: "How to [reverse the obstacle] so that [avatar] can [outcome]." You must solve every problem — even one unanswered objection can kill a sale.
  4. 4Create Delivery Vehicles. Think of every possible way to deliver each solution. Dimensions: personal attention (1:1 → group → 1:many), client effort (DIY → DWY → DFY), format (live → recorded → written), response speed.
  5. 5Trim & Stack. Remove high-cost/low-value items. Bundle everything remaining into the final deliverable.
The 10× / ÷10 Test

If a customer paid 10× your price — what would you provide? If they paid 1/10th and you still had to make them successful — how? Stretching in both directions unlocks delivery options you'd never think of at the status quo.

Trim and Stack

"Create flow, monetize flow, then add friction." Get demand moving first. Optimize for efficiency only after cash is coming in.

Stack Example — Gym Weight Loss

Foolproof Bargain Grocery System ($1,000) + Busy Parent Cooking Guide ($600) + Personalized Meal Plan ($500) + Fat Burning Workouts ($699) + Travel Blueprint ($199) + Accountability System ($1,000) + Eating Out System ($349) = $4,351 perceived value — price: $599. Eventually sold for $2,400–$5,200.

Scarcity — Sold Out

Scarcity limits quantity. Fear of missing out beats desire for gain. The simplest strategy: be truthful about your capacity. "We can take 5 new clients this week." Announce your sellouts — desire intensifies for the next opening.

Total Cap

Only X clients total. Create a waitlist. Bump by 10–20%, then cap again.

Growth Rate Cap

"We only accept 5 new clients per week. First 3 spots are taken."

Cohort Cap

X clients per class/cohort. Creates operational choreography + legitimate urgency simultaneously.

Urgency — Deadlines Drive Decisions

Urgency limits time. 50–60% of sales happen in the last 4 hours of a week-long launch. Most "interested" people who haven't bought yet would never have bought without a deadline.

Rolling Cohorts

"Our next group kicks off Monday." The less frequent the start, the more powerful.

Rolling Seasonal

New Year → Valentine's → Spring → Summer. Same offer, new wrapper and date.

Pricing/Bonus

"Not sure how long we're running this — we change promos every 4 weeks." FOMO on the deal.

Exploding Opportunity

Real arbitrage that decays with time. True urgency, not manufactured.

Bonuses — It's All Gravy, Baby

"A single offer is less valuable than the same offer broken into its component parts and stacked as bonuses." This is how infomercials work — anchor to the core price, then expand perceived value with each bonus until it's irrational not to buy.

10 Rules for Powerful Bonuses

  1. Always offer them — present the entire bundle
  2. Give each a special name containing a benefit (not "bonus 3")
  3. Explain: what it is, how you discovered it, how it improves their life
  4. Provide proof — statistic, past client, personal result
  5. Paint a vivid image of life after using it
  6. Always assign a dollar value and justify it
  7. Tools and checklists beat additional training — lower effort = higher perceived value
  8. Each should address a specific obstacle the prospect believes will stop them
  9. Solve their next problem before they encounter it
  10. Total bonus value should eclipse the core offer value
Add Bonuses, Never Discount

Discounting teaches customers your prices are negotiable and signals weakness. Adding bonuses signals strength and goodwill — and protects your core offer price forever.

Free Bonuses — Other People's Products

Approach adjacent businesses. They give you their product/service as a bonus for exposure to your qualified clients. You get high-value bonuses at zero cost; they get warm referrals. Negotiate affiliate commissions — each "free" bonus becomes a revenue stream.

Guarantees — Reversing Risk

The #1 objection for any offer is risk — risk that it won't work for them. If a guarantee increases closes by 30% but doubles refund rate (5% → 10%): 130 sales − 13 refunds = 117 net vs. 100 − 5 = 95 net. That's 23% more revenue. Do the math, not the emotional calculation.

Stack guarantees: unconditional 30-day money-back + conditional 90-day triple-your-money-back. The stack future-paces the prospect into believing the result is achievable.

Naming — The MAGIC Formula

A great name on the same offer can get 2–10× the responses. When offers fatigue, rename the wrapper — not rebuild the offer. The actual value stack rarely changes. Only the presentation does.

M — Magnetic Reason Why

"Free," "88% off," "Grand Opening," seasonal hook. Answers: why respond now?

A — Announce Your Avatar

Call out exactly who this is for. "Towson moms" beats "Baltimore moms." Hyper-specific beats generic.

G — Give Them a Goal

Dream outcome in one phrase: "celebrity smile," "pain-free," "first client in 5 days."

I — Indicate an Interval

Duration or time to result: "21-day," "6-week," "12-month."

C — Container Word

Challenge, Blueprint, Intensive, Masterclass, Boot Camp, Sprint, Accelerator, Transformation.

When Offers Fatigue — Change Order

1) Creative → 2) Body copy → 3) Headline/name → 4) Seasonality → 5) Duration → 6) Free/discount component → 7) Entire monetization structure (last resort only).

Engaged lead = a person who has shown interest in what you sell. They raised their hand. Advertising = the process of making your offer known. Nothing more.

Lead Magnets

A lead magnet is a complete solution to a narrow problem — given free or heavily discounted — that creates engaged leads from strangers. It solves one problem so well it naturally reveals the next problem your core offer solves.

Reveals a Problem

Diagnosis, audit, assessment. Shows them something is wrong they didn't know about. Creates urgency.

Free Trial / Sample

A taste of the real thing. Once they've experienced value, they want more.

One Step of Many

Solve step 1 for free. The natural question: "what's step 2?" — which you sell.

Three Rules for a Good Lead Magnet

  1. Name it well — the title IS the offer. Specificity wins. "Lose 10 lbs in 30 days" beats "weight loss tips."
  2. Make it easy to consume — offer multiple formats (video + PDF + audio). Friction = drop-off.
  3. Make it genuinely good — give away the secrets, sell the implementation. Mediocre lead magnet implies mediocre paid product.
Always Attach a CTA

Every lead magnet must end with a call to action with scarcity or urgency. "You got the free thing — here's the next step. Only X spots left."

The Core Four

There are exactly four ways to let people know about your stuff. Every lead generation tactic in history maps to one of these four boxes.

The Right Order

Start with warm outreach (fastest revenue). Add cold outreach (scalable, reliable). Build content (compounds over time). Run paid ads last — requires data + cash flow from the first three.

The Rule of 100

Do 100 primary actions per day for 100 days straight. 100 outreaches/day. 100 minutes of content/day. $100/day in ads. Baseline, not ceiling.

Warm Outreach 1:1 · Warm

Fastest way to get your first 5 customers. You have more warm contacts than you think.

  1. 1Build the list — every person you know: past clients, friends, family, former colleagues, phone contacts. No filter yet.
  2. 2Warm them up first — engage genuinely for a few days before pitching. Like, comment, react.
  3. 3ACA Framework — Acknowledge (what they're up to) · Compliment (genuine, specific) · Ask (one question to move forward). Never lead with your pitch.
  4. 4Offer the lead magnet — when natural. Then: "Do you know anyone who might benefit from this?" Referrals compound warm outreach.
  5. 5The Pricing Ramp — start free (get 5 case studies), raise price 20% every 5 clients. Never stay free once you have results.

The Hinge Method (3-Way Referral)

"Hey [friend], I want to connect you with [prospect] — I think you'd both get value." You're the hinge. Both parties are pre-sold via the relationship. Works cold into warm in seconds.

Content: Creation 1:Many · Warm

Critical Rule

Make all content for strangers. If your audience already knows you, it should still make sense to someone who has never heard of you. Forces clarity. Makes content shareable.

Content: Monetization 1:Many · Warm

Give:Ask ratio — Hormozi's rule: "Give in public, ask in private." Use integrated asks (CTA woven into value pieces) 4–10× more often than intermittent asks (occasional full-sell piece).

Key Data Point

78% of Gym Launch customers consumed 2+ pieces of long-form content before buying. Content doesn't just acquire — it pre-sells at scale. Your best posts become your best sales scripts.

7 Hard-Won Content Lessons

  1. How-to → How-I — "How I lost 30 lbs while running a company" beats "How to lose weight." Story + specific proof.
  2. Repeat yourself — Your best ideas need 5–7 repetitions before they land. Say them in different ways.
  3. Puddles → Ponds → Lakes → Oceans — Start niche, expand as you master it.
  4. Content creates sales tools — The market writes your pitch deck for you.
  5. Free content retains paying customers — Existing customers who consume free content churn less.
  6. No shorter attention spans — People have higher standards, not shorter spans. Make better content.
  7. Record when inspired, post when relevant — Batch 2 days/month (~30 shorts + 2–4 long-form pieces).

Paid Ads: Money & Metrics 1:Many · Cold

The Master Metric
LTGP ÷ CAC ≥ 3:1 = scalable · ≥ 10:1 = exceptional (Hormozi's personal: 36:1)
Critical: Gross Profit ≠ Revenue

A business with $500 LTV but 10% gross margins has $50 LTGP. Spending $100 to acquire them = losing money even if LTV looks fine. Always work from gross profit.

Three Phases of Ad Spending

Phase 1: Track

Set up attribution before spending a single dollar. Running ads without tracking is placing bets without knowing the odds.

Phase 2: Lose

Expect to lose first. Of 300 ads, ~295 won't win. The 5 winners make up for all of them. Don't stop when you find a winner but are net-negative.

Phase 3: Print

Once you make more than you spend — spend as much as physically possible. A magic money machine that turns $1 into $2: how much do you put in?

2:1 return Day 1 → reinvest → 4× → 8× → 16× → 32× in 5 months

Scaling: More / Better / New

The Sequence
More → Better → New (then repeat at each new level)

More

What stops you from doing 10× more of what's working? Rule of 100: 100 primary actions/day, 100 days. Before $1M/yr profit: just do more. Volume is the lever.

Better

One test per week per platform. Fix the earliest steps first — they have lowest conversion %, so tiny improvements have largest absolute impact. After $1M/yr: flip to Better → More → New.

New

New placement → new platform → new media type → new Core Four method. Most businesses think they've maxed out long before they actually have.

The "Tapped Market" Fallacy

"I've tapped my market" almost always means "I've tapped one channel on one platform." If any competitor can still acquire clients in your space, you haven't tapped the market — you just haven't learned their methods yet.

Building a Team (Lead-Getter Employees)

Employees don't just deliver your product. Lead-getter employees do the Core Four for you. This is how you go from a high-paying job to a business. Same revenue — difference is entirely who's doing the work, and therefore the enterprise value.

Recruiting = Advertising. The exact same Core Four applies: warm outreach to your network for referrals · cold outreach to headhunt · content to attract · paid to promote job postings.

Book 3 $100M Money Models How to Make Money

Overview: Why Money Models Matter

Offers tells you what to sell. Leads tells you how to find people. Money Models tells you how to structure the transaction so you make more money in 30 days than it cost you to acquire the customer — removing cash as a constraint on growth.

The Big Idea
30-Day Gross Profit > CAC = Self-Funding Growth

"Simple scales — have as few offers as possible to make maximum GP in the shortest time." Start with one. Perfect it. Then add the next layer.

Upsells often make up the majority of total profit. McDonald's: burger alone = $0.25 GP. Burger + fries + soda = $1.75 GP — 8× more with the same CAC.

Attraction Offers (6 Types)

1 · Win Your Money Back

Set a goal. If they hit it, they get their money back as cash or store credit. The goal drives behaviors your business needs anyway.

  • Three versions: Results-only / actions-only / both. 78% success rate.
  • At the halfway point, credit winnings toward a long-term program — "make everyone a winner"
  • Goal must: be objectively trackable · drive results that advertise your business · include mandatory sales appointments · use credit over cash rebate (extend over 12 months)

2 · Free Giveaways

Advertise a chance to win a grand prize. Everyone who enters wins a participation trophy (10–30% discount). You get contact info, pain-point data, and a built-in sales opportunity.

  • Urgency at 3 moments: to enter / to claim / to use
  • "Refer a winner and you win too" = double prizes, double leads
  • Add eligibility questions to the entry form — they become your sales ammo

3 · Decoy Offers

Advertise a free or discounted version. When the lead engages, present both the decoy and the premium side-by-side. 70–80% take the premium — it's an assumed close.

  1. 1Advertise the outcome, not the vehicle. Gives you flexibility in how you present.
  2. 2Ask the sorting question: "Are you here for free stuff or lasting results?" Everyone says results.
  3. 3Present premium with excitement, then contrast with decoy. Let the gap do the work.
  4. 4Ask which will get them to their goal faster — forces them to choose premium themselves.
  5. 5If they take the decoy: surprise them with a free premium feature, then upsell at the halfway point.

4 · Buy X Get Y Free

A 33% discount sounds weak. "Buy two get one free" is the same math but converts dramatically better. More free things beats fewer expensive free things — "buy 1 get 2 free" beats "buy 2 get 1 free" even if economics are identical.

Cash Management

If you take prepayments for undelivered service, put cash in a separate account and pay yourself monthly as you deliver. Cap prepayments at 10% of recurring customer base.

5 · Pay Less Now or Pay More Later

Two flavors: (1) Card down for $0 today → billed full price after they've consumed. (2) Discounted price + bonuses if they pay now. Always get the card first on the "pay later" version, then offer "pay now" as the upgrade. 10% cancellation on pay-later = normal; above 10% = fix the promise or price.

6 · Free with Consumption

Webinars, challenges, events, books — free content that microwave-accelerates trust. Compress weeks of trust-building into days. Benchmarks: mass virtual 2–5% · live webinar ~10% · strategy calls 25%+. Teach concepts, not tactics — concepts make them want the done-for-you version.

Upsell Offers (4 Types)

An upsell is whatever you offer next. Fire at the point of greatest deprivation (immediately after sale, 24–72h later, after first big win, halfway through, or at exit).

Upsell 1 · Classic Upsell

"You can't have X without Y." Provide something for free, make the natural next purchase feel required. Target: 80%+ close rate. Free month of storage → must buy lock + boxes + insurance → $127 average transaction from $0 front end.

Upsell 2 · Menu Upsell

  1. 1Unsell — Cross out what they don't need. Builds goodwill. Feels like a "homie hookup" but is scripted.
  2. 2Prescribe — Tell them exactly what they need and how they'll use it, as if they've already bought it.
  3. 3A/B Choice — Never ask "do you want it?" Ask "do you prefer chocolate or vanilla?" Every choice is a buying choice.
  4. 4Card on File Close — "Do you want to use the card you have on file?" Eliminates the second buying decision entirely.

Economist Decoy: Add a third option (C) that's just A at the price of A+B — makes A+B feel like a no-brainer.

Upsell 3 · Anchor Upsell

Present the most expensive version first (5–10× main offer price). Get the gasp. Come to the rescue with the main offer, which now feels like a bargain. 1 in 10 buying at 10× = double average ticket. Anchor doesn't need to always close — it reprices everything else.

Rolls-Royce Lesson

Rolls-Royce stopped showing at car shows (where $400K seemed egregious) and started at yacht and jet shows (where $400K feels like candy). Everything is relative to what it's positioned next to.

Upsell 4 · Rollover Upsell

Credit some or all of a previous purchase toward your next offer. Four "whos": old customers (win-back "I owe you money") · upset customers (alternative to refund) · competitors' upset customers (scrape 1-star reviews, offer credit for switching) · current customers (credit toward premium upgrade). Upsell must be at least 4× more than the credit amount.

Downsell Offers (3 Types)

Cardinal Rule

Never offer the same thing for less (destroys trust). Always offer something different for less. They said no to this offer, not all offers.

Downsell 1 · Payment Plans

People reject offers because they cost too much today, not in total. Seesaw Anchor: Present payment plan price first ($12K), then offer prepayment discount ($10K) — now paying in full feels like a win.

  1. 1Anchor with payment plan price. Offer prepay discount to "save money."
  2. 2Third-party financing — adds ~35% revenue. Credit card: "would you rather I pick your terms or you?"
  3. 3Half now, half on payday. "When do you get paid?" Schedule on their paycheck date.
  4. 4Temperature check. "Scale of 1–10, how badly do you want this?" Below 8 → pivot to feature downsell.
  5. 5Three-payment split. ⅓ now, ⅓ next paycheck, ⅓ paycheck after. 3 payments inside 30 days.
  6. 6Spread evenly over service duration.
  7. 7Free trial with penalty — the final option.

Downsell 2 · Trial with Penalty

Customer gets it free as long as they meet your terms. If they don't, they pay. Always get a card first ("just for your account profile"). Sell staying and paying before you start. Explain fees after you have payment info — much lower resistance.

  • They love it: Auto-billing is already set up. Pitch upgrades.
  • They hate it: "That's on me." Offer the higher-tier version. Half buy it.
  • They didn't use it: Waive the fee to re-engage. Fees are leverage, not revenue.

Downsell 3 · Feature Downsells

Remove a highly valuable feature + tiny price drop (customers often re-upsell themselves). Then remove something they don't care about + big price drop (drives purchase). Proactively downgrade unused features in existing customers → highest LTV and lowest churn. "Wow, these guys actually want me to pay less."

Continuity Offers (3 Types)

LTV Formula
LTV = (Price / Monthly Churn %) × Gross Margin %

A $10M transactional business trades at 4× EBITDA. A $10M recurring business trades at 8–15×. Same revenue, $24M–$45M difference in enterprise value. "If they stay, buyers will pay."

Continuity 1 · Bonus Offers

Advertise the bonus, not the membership. The bonus must be worth more than the first continuity payment. Tested pricing: standalone at 2× monthly rate → 70% choose continuity. At 3× → ~95%.

Status titles: Silver/Gold/Diamond/Double Diamond. Place first milestone just past your highest-churn month. Customers care more about their title than any tangible bonus.

Continuity 2 · Discount Offers

Four structures: front-loaded (free months up front, delays cash) · back-loaded (free months earned after commitment, highest LTV) · spread (discount across all payments) · first + last (pay first and last upfront, get free months in between).

  • Bill every 4 weeks: 13 billing cycles/year = 8.3% more revenue
  • Add 3% processing fee: Get ACH/bank account as backup → dramatically reduces involuntary churn
  • Lifetime discount at churn point: Hang the carrot just past month they normally drop

Continuity 3 · Waived Fee Offer

Present a startup fee (3–5× monthly rate). Waive it entirely with 12+ month commitment. If they cancel inside the term, they pay the fee. "The cost of quit exceeds the cost to stay = they stay."

Simple Formula
Fee = Monthly Rate × 5 · Waived with 12-Month Commitment

Fees get them to start (avoid the fee) and get them to stick (avoid paying it on exit). After they complete the commitment, the fee goes away permanently — that's what makes it fair.

Build Your Money Model

  1. 1Pick an Attraction Offer. Starts with getting leads to engage. One of the 6 — pick the simplest that works.
  2. 2Pick an Upsell Offer. The natural next thing. Classic upsell is the easiest start. Target 80%+ uptake.
  3. 3Pick a Downsell Offer. Start with payment plans. Add feature downsells once you know what customers value.
  4. 4Pick a Continuity Offer. Add last. Downell the upsell — offer continuity at a lower monthly rate than the intensive.
