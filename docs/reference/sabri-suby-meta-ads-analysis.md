# Part 1 — What's operative

## V1 — the hyper-dopamine ad framework (copy/creative doctrine)

**The core thesis:** the half-life of media-buying knowledge is short; the durable edge is
psychology and copywriting [00:25]. "You can have the best media buyer on the planet who doesn't
understand high-dopamine ads, and they'll get slaughtered by an amateur with broad targeting, the
worst account structure known to man, and an ad that's dynamite" [02:15].

**The two jobs of an ad** [04:09]:
1. Deliver **news, value, or findings** — because that's what people actually consume on Meta
2. **Sell the click** — enough curiosity that CTR outruns every other advertiser, which is what
   earns you the best inventory and the lowest CPMs

**Where the headline formats come from:** he reverse-engineers Meta's own *Widely Viewed Content
Report* [02:39]. The most-viewed pages on Facebook are all news/gossip — LADbible, UNILAD, E! News,
People, TMZ. His argument: don't fight what the platform's audience already consumes, ride it
[03:48]. He then shows the direct translation move — take a viral gossip headline structure and
swap the subject:

| Their headline | His translation |
|---|---|
| "Taylor Swift fans going wild after spotting X-rated detail…" | "Aussies going wild after spotting home loan hack" |
| "iPhone users advised to do one thing to stop battery draining" | "CEOs advised to do one thing before going home to their family" |
| "Secret way to find out who called you without using Google" | "Secret way to find $5M of business funding without banks" |

**The central framework — the clickbait × benefit intersection** [15:02–17:39]:

```
pure clickbait  ──────────  HYPER-DOPAMINE AD  ──────────  pure targeted benefit
(blind intrigue,            (intrigue + specific            (no curiosity,
 attracts everyone,          benefit for a specific          nothing to click for)
 no qualification)           audience)
```

- **Blind intrigue** ("You won't believe what Ivanka Trump does in the morning") is a failure mode —
  it tells the algorithm nothing about who to find, so you get everybody.
- **Benefit with no intrigue** is also a failure mode — nothing compels the click.
- The winner is the intersection: *"Supermodels apply these three simple tricks to look younger"* —
  curiosity **plus** a benefit that self-selects an audience.

**The three-criteria test for any ad** [17:39]: (1) does the creative **interrupt the pattern**?
(2) is there **burning intrigue** in creative *and* headline? (3) is a **big specific benefit**
promised if they click? All three, or it's not a hyper-dopamine ad.

**Anatomy of the ad, in the order the eye moves** [06:59, 25:12–32:07]:
1. **Scroll-stopping image** (the pattern interrupt) — seen first
2. **Eyeball-grabbing headline**
3. **Slippery lead-in copy** — "the most overlooked part of any ad." He claims changing *only* the
   lead-in has 5x'd ROAS and cut CPA 5x [25:12]. Treat the magnitude as a sales claim; the
   principle (the first line is disproportionately load-bearing) is sound.
4. **Intriguing link description** — also overlooked ("Watch carefully, then click")
5. **"Butter" body copy** — long form, 2,200 characters (the limit that runs on both FB and IG)
6. **Non-threatening CTA** — **"Learn More" won his split tests.** Not "Buy Now," not "Inquire Now."

**Creative styles he names** [20:57–24:28] — all described verbally, not verified from frames:
- **Raw native** — shot on a phone, doesn't look professional. Multiple people in frame performs
  well. His #1 or #2 go-to.
- **SMS ad** — a fake text message screenshot; swap the sender and message to fit the offer
- **Breaking news** — his other go-to. "If I had to bet my life on something working, it would be
  breaking news… it works in every single market." Warning: **can be overdone — never run all ads
  in this format.**
- **Native highlight** — a native photo with a red circle + arrow drawn on it
- **Native social post** — mimics the LADbible/E! News post layout
- **Secret info / reveal** — "Revealed: this secret Victorian suburb is set to boom in 2024"

**The primary + secondary image device** [10:13]: nearly every viral news post pairs a main image
with a small inset image. His stated reason — the brain has to comprehend two things at once,
which stalls the scroll long enough for the headline to get read.

**Copy rules** [26:42–30:27]:
- **5th-grade reading level or below**, checked with the Hemingway app. He personally targets
  grade 3–4. "Consumption precedes conversion." "You don't want people to notice your writing —
  you want them moved by your message."
- Write like you talk. One- and two-syllable words. Short sentences, short paragraphs.
- **Line-by-line breaks** — split tested and won, because a wall of text "looks like work"
- **"You," not "our customer."** **"We/us," not the company name.** Write to one person.
- **Specificity is believability** — "lose 12 to 19 kilos in the next 5 weeks," not "lose weight."
  "90 to 480 inbound leads every month," not "explode your leads." The more general the claim, the
  more doubt it generates.
- **Long copy beats short copy** — but only if it's interesting. Write it, ignore length, then edit
  aggressively down to 2,200 characters.
- **Theme everything to the winning concept** — once a headline wins, align creative, lead-in, and
  body copy to that one angle and fully commit to it.
- ⚠️ **"Avoid using 'you' and 'your' a lot"** [30:04] — he says the platforms dislike ads that call
  out individuals. Note this **directly contradicts** his own rule four bullets up. He states both
  in the same video without reconciling them. My read: the second is about Meta's *personal
  attribute* policy (implying you know something personal about the viewer), not about second
  person as a grammatical choice. Treat it as "second person is fine; implied personal knowledge
  is not."
- **Positive beats negative 8 out of 10 times** [32:33] — a big-benefit promise out-pulls
  pain-avoidance framing, *even in markets full of pain*. He says test both, but that's the base rate.
- Above all of it [33:02]: proof, credibility, scarcity, sentence structure — all of it "pales" next
  to hitting the bullseye of what the market actually wants. You form a hypothesis about the desire
  and let ad performance tell you if you were right.

**The market pyramid** [00:47] — 3% buying now · 17% information-gathering · 20% problem aware ·
60% not problem aware. His argument for Meta: it's the one channel that lets you reach the whole
pyramid, because you're pushing a message at people regardless of whether they're in-market.
(This is the Chet Holmes buyer's-pyramid construct; it maps onto Schwartz's awareness ladder.)

## V2 — eight scaling/account hacks

**1. Statics over video** [00:00–02:30]. Since Andromeda, the account needs an absurd volume of
fresh creative to avoid fatigue, and video can't be produced at that rate — he has a full creative
team and 17 VSLs running and still can't keep up. He also claims Meta's algorithm is *biased toward
statics* because it can serve more of them per session (unverified — treat as his read, not fact).
His own "down and dirty native statics" outperformed a VSL that took 2.5 months to produce.
**Action item:** one hour per week, on the calendar, dedicated to fresh creative.

**2. The keyword/niche insertion hack** [02:30–04:52]. Andromeda reads the image, copy, offer, and
landing page to decide who to serve. So **to target a different person, put that person in the
creative** — not in the audience settings. Take the winning ad, duplicate, insert the niche word:
"462 leads per week" → "462 **dental** leads per week" / "**lawyer** leads" / "**property
investment** leads." He calls it an *identity trigger*: it both attracts the reader and tells the
algorithm who to hunt. Claims it drops CPL materially and opens audience pockets a generic ad
can't reach.

**3. The winning-format clone + zombie campaign** [04:52–07:10]. Riding a single winner until it
fatigues is "amateur hour." Instead, feed the winner to an LLM with this framing: *"be the author of
this ad — if I showed the next ad to 100 people, not one in a hundred could tell it was written by
someone else."* Then generate variants per segment ("write it for a 30-year-old woman"). Order of
operations: body copy first → headlines → creative. Pool the winners into a **CBO** and let Meta
allocate. Then take the ads that got **zero spend but you still believe in**, put them in their own
ad set — the **"zombie campaign"** — and he claims ~20% more winners come out of it.

**4. Don't make ads look like ads** [07:10–09:06]. Hundreds of millions of people installed ad
blockers; people hate ads. So mimic what's natively consumed. He runs a 46-minute organic YouTube
video *as an ad* because it already proved itself organically. **The burner-account tactic:** make a
clean TikTok/Instagram account, follow every influencer and page in your niche, like their content —
now your feed surfaces exactly what the algorithm is already promoting in that niche. Make ads that
look like that.

**5. Broad targeting + hyper-specific creative** [09:06–11:27]. Interest-stacking is the wrong
lever. **"The creative is the targeting now."** Short copy gives Meta a small context window, which
limits who it can find; **long copy gives the algorithm more data about who you want.** Stop trying
to outsmart the most highly-paid engineers on earth whose only incentive is putting your ad in front
of the right person. His test: duplicate your best interest-stacked ad, strip targeting to just a
country, run both 7 days, compare CPA.

**6. Match the ad to the landing page** [11:27–13:26]. Most advertisers put 90% of effort into ads
and 10% into the page. If the page doesn't match what was clicked, they bounce — high CTR, cheap
clicks, and CPA in the toilet, because the scent doesn't match. **Meta is the best split-testing
tool on earth** for headlines: a headline is exposed to ~1,000× more people than ever reach your
page, so headline data reaches significance far faster there. Run 20–40 headline variants, find the
winner, then **mirror it on the landing page headline, sub-headline, and lead-in.** He claims a
15–20% conversion-rate lift minimum. His personal rule: at least three split tests running at all
times.

**7. Retarget with a different offer** [13:26–15:35]. The #1 reason someone didn't buy is that
**the offer wasn't right for them** — so re-serving the same offer is the wrong response. Retarget
with a *different* one (his example: protein powder buyers at 2–3 ROAS; retarget the non-buyers with
creatine and hit 6). His full retargeting stack, in order:
   1. **Objection-handling ad** — built from the actual reasons people gave for not booking. He
      gets these by outbound-calling everyone who downloads the free report and asking directly.
   2. **Proof / testimonial ads** — run as carousels, showing the claims deliver
   3. **Other offers** — pooled into a CBO of best performers
   4. **An audit** — something that makes the call itself valuable whether or not they buy

**8. Track the metric that actually matters** [15:35–17:57]. Founders throttle spend to protect a
ROAS *percentage*. Wrong metric. Optimize **blended ROAS at the business level**, and above that,
**net free cash flow**. Find your break-even ROAS — the true maximum you can pay to acquire a
customer — and scale toward it. Dropping from 10 ROAS to 5 ROAS while going from $1k/mo to $100k/mo
makes vastly more money. "Data gives you confidence, and confidence lets you move with speed."
**Action item:** three hours a month, on the calendar, personally looking at the numbers — not your
analyst's summary, not your media buyer's. Yours.

## ADAPT — right idea, wrong parameters for us

- **"Breaking news" and gossip-format creative.** It works because it's native to the feed, and our
  buyer is on that feed like everyone else. But a dental practice owner evaluating a vendor who will
  touch their patient list is making a **trust purchase**, and gossip formatting cuts against the
  exact thing our offer sells. **Adapt:** keep the *structural* moves (pattern interrupt, primary +
  secondary image, curiosity gap, specificity) and drop the tabloid register. The "raw native" and
  "native highlight" styles transfer cleanly; "breaking news" should be one test ad at most, never
  the portfolio.
- **Character count.** Suby writes to 2,200 characters. Our locked 230–250 words is roughly
  1,300–1,500. We're leaving ~40% of the allowed context on the table — and per hack #5, that's
  algorithmic targeting data, not just copy. **Worth an explicit test:** one long variant of a
  proven ad at ~2,200 characters against the 250-word control. This needs Trevor's sign-off since
  230–250 is a locked directive.
- **Hundreds of AI-generated variants + CBO + zombie campaign.** The mechanic is sound; the *scale*
  isn't ours. At a $600 campaign-viability budget, 200 ads means every ad gets $3 and learns
  nothing. **Adapt:** run the clone process to generate the pool, but ship 4–5 at a time. Revisit
  the zombie campaign only when monthly spend can support it.
- **Statics over video.** Directionally right and it matches what our own strategy doc already says
  (static-led). But our proof is a *conversation* — SMS threads and transcripts are inherently
  visual and static-friendly. Statics aren't a compromise for us, they're the natural format.
- **Burner account for native-content research.** Genuinely useful, but our niche's feed is dental
  practice-management content, which is a much smaller pool than consumer verticals. Worth one
  afternoon, not a standing process.

## CONFLICT — flag, don't silently resolve

**1. "Positive benefit beats negative 8/10 times" vs. our loss-aversion positioning.**
Our entire frame is loss recovery — "you already paid to acquire every patient on that report."
Suby's base rate says big-benefit promises out-pull pain-avoidance framing even in painful markets.
**These are more reconcilable than they look:** loss *recovery* ("get back what's yours") is a
benefit frame, while loss *avoidance* ("don't lose another year of production") is the negative
frame he's warning about. The distinction is recovery vs. avoidance, and our copy currently mixes
both. Worth a deliberate test, not a rewrite.

**2. "Avoid 'you'/'your'" vs. Hormozi-style direct callouts.**
Suby contradicts himself within V1. Reconciliation: Meta's restriction is on **implied personal
attributes**, not second person. "PPO owners with 400+ overdue patients" describes a *business*
fact, which is fine. "Struggling to make payroll?" implies knowledge of their finances, which is
not. Our existing compliance rule already draws this line correctly — no change needed, but the
line is now better understood.

**3. Clickbait doctrine vs. our no-inflated-claims rule.**
Suby's defense of clickbait is that the platform rewards clicks and the opposite of clickbait is an
ad nobody wants to click. Fair. But our market is Schwartz stage 3/4 — **claims are exactly what
they've stopped believing**, and a tabloid hook from a vendor asking for their patient list is a
trust cost, not just a CTR gain. **Our resolution: take the curiosity mechanics, refuse the
credibility trade.** Curiosity built from a *true specific* ("Nobody in the building made a phone
call") is the version of this that survives our constraints.

**4. His numbers are marketing.** $200M/$300M spend, 5x from a lead-in change, "20% more winners"
from zombie campaigns — all unverified, all in videos selling a $tool. The frameworks stand on
their own logic; the magnitudes shouldn't be quoted anywhere.

## DROP

- Kong (his AI tool) — no reason to adopt a black box; we generate variants in this repo already.
- "Not one in 100 could tell a different person wrote it" as a variant-generation goal — for us
  that produces near-duplicates, which Andromeda collapses into one entity. **Our constraint is the
  opposite: conceptual separation.** See the angle matrix.
- Gossip/tabloid register in the primary ad set (see ADAPT).
