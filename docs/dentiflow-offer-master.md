## 1. The offer in one line

> **The 200-Patient Reactivation Pilot — we take 200 patients from your overdue list, our AI runs two-way text conversations with them, and books the ready ones back onto your schedule. If it books fewer than 5 patients in 30 days, you get every dollar back. $1,250 one-time. Whole list after: $499/mo.**

The ad never says the price. The ad sells the application. The application sells the call. The call sells the pilot. The pilot sells continuity. Continuity sells the system.

---

## 2. The strategic decision: ads lead with reactivation — not the system

The full system is real and it's where the money is. It is also the wrong thing to advertise. Ads lead with **one component — dormant-patient reactivation** — because:

1. **It's the only proven component.** One locked case study (657 texted → 145 clicked → 32 booked → ~$8,000 production for ~$55 in texts). Everything else is a promise; this is a receipt.
2. **It's a "found money" offer, not a "future growth" offer.** The strongest direct-response structure that exists: the practice *already paid* to acquire every name on the overdue report. We recover sunk cost — certain money — instead of promising uncertain gains. Leaky-bucket offers beat inflated-claim offers at every scale.
3. **It self-qualifies.** "300+ overdue patients" is only true of an established, producing practice. Meta always optimizes toward the cheapest (brokest) lead in any market — the problem callout itself is the wealth floor.
4. **Small step, not big leap.** We add bookings to an already-working practice. Highest-probability fulfillment promise there is; the guarantee is mathematically safe at 200 patients.
5. **Conveyor belt.** One entry SKU means every client arrives with the same problem, same onboarding (one CSV export + copy approval), same delivery, same proof format. That's what makes solo, after-2pm delivery possible — and what makes the next case studies cheap to produce.

**Advertising a "system" in this market is a commodity claim.** Every dental vendor says "all-in-one platform." Nobody else says "we'll put 5+ of your own lapsed patients back on the schedule in 30 days or you pay nothing." The system gets sold *inside* the relationship (§9), never inside the ad.

## 3. The named mechanism: **Two-Way Recall**

Dental recall is a sophistication-stage-3/4 market — every owner has heard recall promises from Weave, RevenueWell, postcard vendors, and their own front desk. At this stage, claims and guarantees alone underperform; the offer must carry a **mechanism**: why everything they tried failed, and why this is different.

**The mechanism paragraph (use verbatim in ads, landing page, and on the call before the price reveal):**

> Most recall is one-way. Postcards, no-reply text blasts, a front-desk call list nobody finishes. One-way messages get ignored because the patient's real blockers — "how much will this cost?", "who is this?", "I can't deal with scheduling right now" — never get answered. So the patient does nothing, and the practice concludes recall doesn't work.
>
> We run **Two-Way Recall**: the AI opens the conversation, and when the patient replies, it answers like your best front-desk person — handles the cost question, the insurance question, the "can I come Saturday?" question — and walks them to a booked appointment. That's why 657 texts produced 145 replies-and-clicks and 32 booked patients, when a typical one-way blast books 1–3%.

**Naming rules:**
- The mechanism is called **Two-Way Recall** everywhere. Consistency builds the category; the name IS the differentiation ("one-way vs two-way" does the explaining on its own).
- **"AI" stays out of the offer name.** Dentists don't wake up wanting AI — they want full hygiene chairs. AI is the *how* (mechanism credibility), never the *what*. Test "AI" in hooks (it's a novelty pattern-interrupt for some segments, a skepticism trigger for older owners) — the offer name never depends on the test.
- Claim boundary (unchanged, non-negotiable): the AI texts, converses, and books **via booking link**. Never "books directly into your PMS" until Ascend integration is live. The mechanism paragraph above stays inside this line.

## 4. The offer stack (what the prospect gets)

Presented on the call as a stack, not a feature list — each element answers a fear:

| Element | What it is | The fear it kills |
|---|---|---|
| 200-patient overdue segment | We select and run the first 200 from their export | "Will this spam my whole patient base?" |
| Done-for-you everything | They export one CSV + approve the copy. That is their entire job. | "I don't have time for another tool" |
| Two-Way Recall engine | AI outreach + AI reply handling, partner-reviewed message bank, safety rails (opt-out, emergency escalation, compliance validator) | "Will a robot embarrass my practice?" |
| Booked-patient attribution | Named list of who booked, verified against their schedule — agreed counting method | "How do I know it worked?" |
| Live dashboard access | Watch clicks and bookings land in real time during the pilot (already built — sell it) | "How do I know anything is happening?" |
| The guarantee | Fewer than 5 bookings in 30 days → their choice: full refund OR we keep working free until they hit 5 | "What if it doesn't work on MY patients?" |

**Bonuses (added 2026-07-13 — named at the close, each with its own line; presented as bonuses, never silent scope, because unnamed work adds zero perceived value):**

1. **The Reactivation Report** — end-of-pilot artifact: full funnel breakdown, named booked-patient list, and the "here's what's still sitting in the rest of your list" projection. This is the continuity close in disguise — the report ends with the whole-list number.
2. **The Whole-List Opportunity Audit** — we segment their *entire* export (not just the 200) and hand back the size of the remaining opportunity. Zero marginal cost: we parse the full export anyway to select the 200. Deliberately a bonus, NOT the core offer — the 200-cap is what keeps the guarantee math safe and delivery bounded.
3. **The Conversation File** — anonymized transcripts of every AI conversation from their pilot. Doubles as the control-story asset for AI-hesitant owners ("you can read every word it said").

**Dream outcome:** production they already paid for, back on the schedule, with zero staff effort.
**Time-to-first-value:** first bookings typically within days of the first send (Day 0/1/3 sequence) — say this on the call; speed is a value multiplier.
**Effort & sacrifice:** one export, one copy approval. The offer's DFY completeness is a core selling point — protect it as the moat deepens (when Ascend API matures, we pull the export *for* them; every task removed justifies the next price tier).

## 5. Terms (locked)

- **Pilot: $1,250 one-time.** Presented only on the call, immediately after the case-study proof ("~$8,000 in production for ~$55 in texts… the pilot is $1,250"). Never in ads, creative, or landing page.
- **Guarantee (choice structure, adopted 2026-07-13): fewer than 5 booked appointments within 30 days of first send → THEIR CHOICE: every dollar back, or we keep working the rest of their list free until they hit 5.** The ad line is unchanged either way ("or you don't pay" / "or it's free"). Why a choice and not both: the remedies are substitutes (refund ends the relationship, keep-working continues it), "both" would burn a pilot slot on a non-customer and invite attribution gaming, and at the failure moment nearly everyone close to the goal picks keep-working — the choice converts would-be refunds into saved relationships. Math: 5 × ~$250 average production ≈ $1,250 — *the pilot pays for itself or it's free.* At the case-study base rate (4.9%), 200 patients expect ~9.8 bookings; failure risk ≈ 4%. **200 is the minimum segment — never shrink it to close a deal.** Very stale lists (24+ months): upsize to 250 or reset expectations at onboarding, never in the ad.
- **Counting method agreed at onboarding, before the send:** a booking = appointment scheduled in their PMS within 30 days, attributable to the outreach (link click or reply thread).
- **Continuity: $499/mo** — Two-Way Recall on the whole list, every month.
- **The rollover close is the DEFAULT, not the fallback** *(Stage 1 rule — flips when Stage 2 activates, §10)*: at pilot debrief, the close is *"Start continuity today and the $1,250 credits to your first months — the pilot was effectively free."* (One-time-fee-waived-into-recurring is the highest-converting flip in the book; ~80% take recurring when it's framed as the pilot becoming free.) Only if they hesitate does the pilot stand alone as a completed one-time purchase.
- **Split-pay fallback (adopted 2026-07-13): 2 × $675** (total $1,350) — the ONLY downsell. For qualified owners who stall on price: terms change, the price never does, and pay-in-full stays the best deal. Never a smaller segment, never a discount.
- **Capacity: 3 concurrent pilots max.** This is real scarcity — state it truthfully ("we take on 3 practices at a time") and never oversell past it.

## 6. Why each piece exists (the psychology map)

| Lever | Where it shows up |
|---|---|
| **Loss aversion / sunk cost** | "You already paid to acquire every patient on your overdue report." Recovering a loss is more motivating than an equivalent gain — this is the lead frame in copy. |
| **Certainty over promise** | Found money (patients they own) vs. speculative growth (new-patient ads). We sell the sure thing. |
| **Risk reversal** | The guarantee's job is to make a stranger reply to a faceless brand with one case study — it wins the *ad*, not the close. State terms plainly; a guarantee routinely honored builds the brand. |
| **Anchoring** | $8,000 case-study number lands ~60 seconds before "$1,250." The price should feel like a rounding error. |
| **Default effect** | Rollover-to-continuity is presented as the natural next step, not an upsell decision. |
| **Paid = qualified** | The $1,250 fee is itself the qualification filter — it keeps the funnel full of owners, not tire-kickers, and finances the ads (one pilot/mo covers a $1,000 ad month). |
| **Real scarcity** | 3 concurrent pilots is true. Truth scales; fake countdown timers don't. |

## 7. Qualification floor (application form gates — unchanged)

PPO practice · 300+ overdue patients · owner/decision-maker · can approve a paid pilot with a money-back guarantee · willing to do a 20-min call + provide a patient export. Auto-decline below the floor.

**Golden segment to write copy at:** the established, high-producing owner with a visibly leaky hygiene schedule who wants *nothing to do with doing it themselves* — this owner pays more, churns less, and never takes it in-house. Gut-punch that specific picture ("holes in your hygiene columns while 400 of your own patients sit overdue"), never generic "grow your practice."

## 8. The call: one-call close + component routing

The ad sells ONE thing, but the call is where the system play begins. 20–30 minutes, no discovery call, no follow-up call:

1. **Confirm** (~5 min) — re-verify form answers; decision-maker on the line.
2. **Diagnose** (~5 min) — one question: *"Where does it leak worst — patients who never come back, new calls you miss, or booked patients who no-show?"* Their answer determines the **continuity conversation later**, not today's sale. Note it.
3. **Proof + mechanism** (~5 min) — case study numbers, then the Two-Way Recall paragraph (§3). The mechanism goes *before* the price: it converts "sounds too good" into "oh, that's why it works."
4. **Offer + price** (~5 min) — the stack (§4), the guarantee, then $1,250 — first time they hear a number, anchored after the $8,000.
5. **Close + onboard on the spot** (~10 min) — payment, request the export before hang-up, set copy approval. $499/mo mentioned only if asked.

**Routing rule:** whatever leak they named in step 2 becomes the first continuity attach *after the pilot wins* — speed-to-lead for "we miss calls" (built today), no-show recovery / reviews / AI voice as they ship. **Never sell a module that can't be turned on. Never route away from the pilot itself** — reactivation is the entry SKU for everyone, because it's the one with the guarantee and the proof.

## 9. Ascension ladder (sold in the relationship, never in the ad)

1. **Pilot → whole-list continuity ($499/mo)** — rollover-default close (§5). "That was 200 patients. Your export had [X]."
2. **Continuity → first attach** — the leak they named on the sales call, offered when they *feel* it ("patients book but a third no-show"). Built ladder today: **recall → speed-to-lead → dashboard.**
3. **Attach → system** — the practice grows into the full revenue system module by module. Switching costs compound and should be said out loud at each step: *"the longer it runs, the smarter your recall gets"* (attribution history, message performance, per-practice learnings live in the dashboard).
4. **Location → group** — 200 patients → whole list → sister locations → DSO groups (Scott's Familia background is the credibility asset). **Blocked until multi-location isolation ships — wire it before any non-Village recall.**
5. **At scale:** custom audience of pilot buyers + continuity customers gets rest-of-system creative, funded by pilot cash.

The $6k–$12k premium system anchor from the [100m blueprint](100m-money-model-blueprint.md) remains the Stage-2 destination — the full architecture for it is pre-designed in §10 and activates on its gates, not before.

## 10. Stage 2 — full build-out architecture (PRE-DESIGNED, GATED OFF)

**Decided 2026-07-10.** Once the full system ships, the default post-pilot close changes from recall continuity to the system install. This section is the pre-committed design so it doesn't get re-litigated deal-by-deal — but **nothing here is sellable until the gates clear.**

**Gates (all required):**
1. All six modules built and live: recall, speed-to-lead, no-show recovery, reviews/referrals, AI voice receptionist, dashboard
2. System-level guarantee math done (see below) — actuarial table per module, same rigor as the pilot's 5-booking floor
3. Multi-location isolation shipped before any group/DSO sale

**The Stage 2 ladder (what changes and what doesn't):**
- **The pilot stays the front door, unchanged** — same ad, same $1,250, same guarantee. Cold traffic still can't be sold a $5k install; the pilot's job upgrades from "module trial" to "paid diagnostic + trust engine for the system sale."
- **The debrief pitch order flips: premium first.** The system install is presented as the *recommendation*, built from their diagnosis answers and their own pilot numbers. Recall-only continuity at $499/mo becomes the deliberate fallback — it now works as the decoy that makes the install the serious choice. Sorting frame: "keep patching this one leak, or fix the boat?"
- **Rollover mechanic carries up:** the $1,250 pilot fee credits toward the install ("the pilot was a down payment").

**Positioning rule (the most important line in this section): price against payroll, never software.** At $499 the buyer compares us to Weave and we win. At $3–5k/mo we cannot win a software comparison — so the pitch runs the staffing comparison instead: a full-time front desk employee costs roughly $3,500–4,500/mo fully loaded *(validate locally before quoting)*, works 8 hours, and never chases the overdue list. The system is an **AI front office** — answers every call, texts every overdue patient, chases every no-show, asks for every review, 24/7. Against a hire, the retainer is cheap. The entire Stage 2 pitch is built on the staffing frame.

**Terms (all figures are PROPOSALS — set final numbers from real attributable-production data, never gut):**
- **Install: ~$5,000 one-time**, pilot fee credited in. Anchor against the blueprint's $8–12k figure or the annualized staff cost.
- **Retainer tiered by practice size:** ~$2,500–3,500/mo single location; scales per location for groups (groups/DSOs are where $5k+/mo clears easily — Scott's Familia credibility is the wedge). A blanket $3–5k on small practices will churn on ROI; tiering is not optional.
- **Founding-client cohort:** first 3–5 system clients at ~$3k/mo locked, in exchange for the case study + testimonial. The system-level case study is the unlock for full pricing — same logic as the pilot: the early deal's real product is proof.

**Why Stage 2 is the only model that fits the capacity constraint:** solo after-2pm delivery can white-glove ~8–12 accounts. At $499 that's ~$5k/mo forever; at $3.5–4k/mo it's $30–45k/mo inside the same hours. The premium model isn't just better economics — it's the only architecture where revenue isn't capped by the calendar.

**Why the ROI story clears at Stage 2 when it couldn't before:** recall alone can't carry $4k/mo after the first dormant-list harvest. The full system stacks independent streams — reactivations + answered/converted missed calls + recovered no-shows + compounding reviews — so the monthly "is this worth it?" question has five answers instead of one, all on one dashboard. Six modules deep, switching costs (phone routing, review flows, recall history, attribution data) make churn nearly unthinkable — which is what a premium retainer requires.

**System-level guarantee: do not wing it.** "The system pays for itself or we keep working free" needs a per-module expected-value table built from real data before it enters any script. The pilot's guarantee works because 200 × 4.9% made refunds a 4% event; Stage 2 gets the same treatment or no guarantee is offered beyond the pilot's.

## 11. Pricing evolution (pre-committed triggers — don't re-litigate in the moment)

$1,250 / $499 is **proving-ground pricing**. Category kings are the most expensive in their niche; price is the flywheel's initial condition. But premium pricing needs proof volume we don't have yet.

| Trigger | Move |
|---|---|
| 3 arms-length (non-32 Dental) pilot case studies delivered | Raise pilot to ~$2,000–$2,500; keep the same guarantee math (raise the booking floor proportionally only if avg production supports it — verify from real data first) |
| 10+ proof assets / waitlist forming | Raise continuity; introduce module tiers |
| Ascend integration live (we pull the export for them) | Price the deeper DFY in — each task removed from the practice justifies a tier |
| A named market disruption hits dental | Deploy a trend-pegged variant of the same pilot (hook reserve — offer structure unchanged) |
| All six modules built and live + guarantee math done | Activate the Stage 2 architecture (§10): debrief pitches the install first, $499 becomes the decoy fallback |

Rule: grow revenue by **raising price and attaching modules**, not by adding client count past what solo after-2pm delivery can hold. Never estimate the new numbers — pull avg production and conversion from the actual pilot data before each move.
