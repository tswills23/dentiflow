## 1. The Problem (Told as a Story)

Imagine you walk into a busy dental office on a Tuesday morning. Here's what's actually happening:

**At the front desk:**
The receptionist has six tabs open. Dentrix for the schedule. Weave for the patient texts. Outlook for emails. A spreadsheet for the recall list. The Aetna website for insurance verification. The phone is ringing. She can't answer because she's confirming an appointment on Weave while looking up the patient in Dentrix. The system on Weave doesn't know what's in Dentrix because they're separate companies. So when a patient texts back "reschedule please," she has to manually find them in Dentrix, find a new slot, type the new time back into Weave, and hope nothing got lost.

The owner is paying $480/month for Dentrix, $620/month for Weave, $400/month for NexHealth (because Weave's online booking isn't great), $350/month for Zuub (because Dentrix's insurance check is broken), $500/month for Dental Intelligence (because Dentrix's reports are unusable), and $250/month for Yapi (because patients hate paper intake forms). That's **$2,600 a month** — $31,200 a year — for software that mostly doesn't talk to itself.

**In the operatory:**
The hygienist is doing a perio exam. She's reading numbers out loud while typing them into a keyboard. It takes 10–15 minutes. In a modern hospital, doctors just talk and the computer writes everything down. In dentistry it's 2003.

**In the billing office:**
The insurance person is on hold with Cigna. She's been on hold for 47 minutes. She does this 10–15 times a day. She spends roughly **15 hours a week** just verifying insurance. Cigna pays out three weeks late because half the claim attachments didn't transmit through Vyne correctly. Nobody knows why. Vyne and Dentrix are in a fight.

**At the end of the day:**
The owner pulls up reports. Production by hygienist? Has to export to Excel. AR aging? Doesn't match Dental Intelligence's number. Treatment acceptance rate? The PMS doesn't track it. He doesn't really know how the practice did this week.

**The result:**
Staff hate their tools. Patients get bad experiences (long phone holds, confusing texts, repeated paperwork). Owners overpay for software that creates more problems than it solves. Everyone knows this is broken.

Now imagine you walked into that same office and said, "What if all of this was one system, where everything talked to each other, and AI handled the boring repetitive parts?" The owner would write you a check today.

That's the product.

## 2. The Solution

**Dentiflow is one platform that does everything a dental office needs, built AI-first from the ground up.**

Think of it like the iPhone moment for dentistry. Before the iPhone, you carried a phone, a camera, an MP3 player, a GPS, and a calculator. Five separate devices. The iPhone didn't beat each one on every feature — but it put them all together, made them work as one thing, and the duct-taped stack became history.

Dental software today is the duct-taped stack. We're the iPhone.

**What's in the platform:**

| Module | What it replaces today | Monthly cost we save the practice |
|---|---|---|
| **AI receptionist** | Weave, NexHealth, RingCentral | $400–700 |
| **Smart recall + recare** | PracticeMojo, RevenueWell | $200–400 |
| **Online booking + waitlist** | LocalMed, NexHealth | $200–400 |
| **Insurance verification + claims** | Zuub, Vyne, eAssist | $300–800 |
| **Patient forms + e-sign** | Yapi, Modento | $200–400 |
| **Real-time analytics** | Dental Intelligence, Jarvis | $300–600 |
| **Scheduling + charting** *(later)* | Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental | $400–800 |
| **Treatment plan + financing** | Zuub, ShowCase | $200–400 |
| **AI clinical notes (scribe)** | Bola, Denti.AI | $200–400 |
| **TOTAL today** | 6–9 vendors | **$2,400–$5,000/mo** |
| **TOTAL with Dentiflow** | One vendor | **$1,200–$2,000/mo** |

The practice saves $1,200–$3,000 per month, gets a better product, and stops being their own IT department.

**Why "AI-first" actually matters:**

Every existing dental software was built before AI was good. Dentrix is from 1989. Eaglesoft is from the early 90s. Even the modern cloud ones (CareStack, Curve) were architected in 2010–2014, before LLMs existed. Their AI is bolted on like a spoiler on a Honda Civic.

When you build AI-first, three things change:
1. **The AI knows about every part of the practice.** It can see the schedule AND the chart AND the insurance AND the billing in one query. The competitors' AI can only see one piece at a time.
2. **The product gets smarter over time.** Every recall conversation, every appointment, every patient reply teaches the system. The data flywheel is real.
3. **The same engineers who built it know how to extend it.** When competitors try to add AI, they're glueing it on. We're adding capabilities to a system that was always built to use AI.

This is the structural advantage. It compounds over 15 years.

## 4. The Market — Real Numbers

There are about **135,000 dental practices** in the United States. Roughly:
- **100,000 are general practices** (cleanings, fillings, crowns — the bulk)
- **20,000 are specialty** (ortho, oral surgery, periodontics, endodontics, pediatric)
- **15,000 are part of a DSO** — a Dental Service Organization, basically a chain that owns multiple offices (think Heartland Dental, Pacific Dental Services, Aspen)

The DSO segment is growing fast. About 1 in 6 dentists is DSO-affiliated today, up from almost zero 15 years ago. By 2035, that could be 1 in 3.

**The money:**

| Spend category | Per practice / month | Total US market |
|---|---:|---:|
| PMS (Dentrix, etc.) | $400–$1,500 | ~$1.6 billion |
| Patient communication (Weave, etc.) | $300–$600 | ~$700 million |
| Insurance verification | $300–$500 | ~$650 million |
| Analytics (Dental Intelligence, Jarvis) | $200–$400 | ~$500 million |
| Online scheduling | $100–$300 | ~$300 million |
| AI scribe / voice tools | $200–$500 | $500M+ and growing fast |
| Imaging/charting add-ons | $200–$600 | ~$700 million |
| **TOTAL ADDRESSABLE PER PRACTICE** | **$1,700–$4,400/mo** | **~$5–6 billion/year US** |

Add Canada, UK, Australia and the English-speaking market is roughly **$10 billion a year**.

**What we need to capture:**

We don't need most of it. To build a $500 million revenue company:
- 25,000 practices × $1,700/month = $510M/year revenue
- That's 18% of all US dental practices
- OR 12,000 practices at $3,500/month = $504M (DSO/mid-market focused)
- That's 9% of US practices

We're not aiming for 90% of the market like Dentrix has today. We're aiming for ~10–18%. **That's the difference between trying to be Microsoft and trying to be Apple.** Apple has 17% of global phone market share and is worth $3 trillion.

**New practices every year:**

Roughly 8,000 brand-new dental practices open in the US each year. Plus another 3–5% of existing practices switch software each year — that's 4,000–7,000 more. So there are roughly **12,000–15,000 "in play" practices every year** — offices that are either opening fresh or actively shopping for new software. We don't need to convert happy Dentrix users. We just need to win a fair share of practices that are already choosing.

If we capture **5% of those each year** starting Year 2, by Year 7 we have ~3,500 practices. At $2,000/month average, that's $84M/year revenue. That's the path.

## 10. The Honest Risks

This is the section your dad will read most carefully. I want to tell you what I'm afraid of and what I'm doing about it.

**Risk 1: Texting compliance (real, immediate, fixable)**

Sending SMS to patients is governed by laws (TCPA). Send a text to someone who didn't consent and the fine is $1,500 per text. A million bad texts is a $1.5 billion lawsuit. We had a near-miss in April 2026 where Twilio (our phone provider) flagged our account.

**Mitigation:** Setting up a backup phone provider (Bandwidth) so we have two independent rails. Strict consent logging on every customer onboarding. Audit log of every text sent. This is a Month 1 fix.

**Risk 2: HIPAA breach (manageable with discipline)**

Dental practices store sensitive patient health information. A breach (someone hacks us, or our code leaks data between practices) triggers federal fines and lawsuits.

**Mitigation:** SOC 2 audit in progress. Vendor agreements (BAAs) signed with everyone who touches patient data. Encryption everywhere. Cyber insurance. We've already designed the system to keep each practice's data fully isolated. But we have to stay paranoid forever.

**Risk 3: Building 6 products at once is hard (real, structural)**

Most companies that try to be a "platform" early die because they spread too thin. The plan above mitigates this by phasing modules — recall first, then comms, then verification, then analytics, then PMS core. Never more than 1–2 new product areas per year.

**Risk 4: Competitors are better-funded**

Archy has $47M. CareStack has $145M. Henry Schein has $13B in revenue. We can't outspend them.

**Mitigation:** Speed and focus. Capital efficiency matters more than capital. The companies that beat Microsoft (Salesforce, Slack, Zoom) didn't outspend them — they out-shipped them in a narrow domain. We do the same.

**Risk 5: 15 years is a marathon**

The biggest founder risk in long builds is the founder. Burnout. Marriage strain. Health issues. Loss of motivation.

**Mitigation:** You're solving for this by bringing on partners — Dad and Best Friend distribute the load. We agree upfront on work-life boundaries. We take vacations. We have an executive coach by Year 1. We support each other. We don't let any one of us burn out alone.

**Risk 6: AI hallucination causes patient harm**

If our AI tells a patient something wrong about their medication or dismisses a serious symptom, we could harm a real person and get sued out of existence.

**Mitigation:** The "AI safety layer" I built is the most paranoid part of the codebase. The AI is not allowed to discuss medications, dosages, diagnoses, or anything clinical. If the patient asks about anything risky, the AI hands off to a human. We will never relax this. Ever.

**Risk 7: Strategic shifts in the market**

Big Tech could enter dental (Microsoft, Google). Insurance companies could buy a PMS and force their providers onto it. A regulatory shift could change the AI rules. These are real but mostly unpredictable.

**Mitigation:** Stay flexible. Keep the product good. Build customer love. Most strategic shifts don't kill the well-positioned companies — they kill the weak ones.

**The honest probabilities:**

- 35–45% chance the company fails entirely (we walk away with little money)
- 40–50% chance of a meaningful exit ($15–50M to each founder)
- 15–20% chance of a big exit ($100M+ to each founder)
- 8–12% chance of a generational exit ($300M+ to each founder)

The math works *because* the upside is huge. Every founder hopes for the top outcome but plans for the middle one.

## Appendix A: Glossary (for the parts that get confusing)

- **PMS** — Practice Management Software. The main computer system a dental office runs on (Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental).
- **ARR** — Annual Recurring Revenue. The money we earn every year from subscriptions.
- **DSO** — Dental Service Organization. A company that owns multiple dental offices (like a chain).
- **AI-native** — Built using AI from day one, vs. an old system that added AI later.
- **AE / SDR** — Account Executive / Sales Development Rep. Salespeople.
- **CSM** — Customer Success Manager. Person who keeps existing customers happy.
- **TCPA** — The law governing texts to consumers. Sending texts without consent is illegal.
- **HIPAA** — The law protecting patient health information.
- **BAA** — Business Associate Agreement. A legal doc our software vendors sign to handle health data.
- **SOC 2** — A security audit. Required by big customers (DSOs).
- **Clearinghouse** — Middleman that submits insurance claims. Cannot bill insurance without one.
- **Vesting** — The schedule on which founders earn their equity. Standard is 4 years.
- **Dilution** — When you sell investors stock, your slice of the company gets smaller.
- **Burn** — How much money the company loses per month.
- **Runway** — How many months of money the company has left.
- **NRR** — Net Revenue Retention. Whether existing customers spend more or less over time.
