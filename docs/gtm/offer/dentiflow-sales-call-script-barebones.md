## Metrics

"Awesome. So I have a couple basic questions about the practice here… cool if we rapid-fire through them?"

**Block 1 — New patients (where new money leaks):**

- How many new patient inquiries do you get a month — calls, forms, all of it?
- How many of those actually get on the schedule?
- And how many of those actually show up?
- Of the ones who show, how many go ahead with the treatment you recommend?
- Where are most of them coming from — Google, insurance lists, referrals, ads?

*[LABEL IT:]* "So out of [30] people a month raising their hand, you're seating about [12]. That's [18] who wanted to come in and never made it to a chair. Fair?"

**Block 2 — Your existing base (where found money sits):**

- How big is your active patient base?
- Roughly how many are overdue or unscheduled right now?
- When a patient finishes a cleaning, do they leave with the next one booked — or does someone have to chase them later?
- What's your no-show or same-day-cancel rate, ballpark?
- How many Google reviews do you have, and what's the rating? *(fill this in before the call)*
- How many referrals do you get a month?

*[LABEL IT:]* "So you've got [400] patients you already paid to acquire just sitting there, and about [1 in 5] of the ones who do book don't show up. Fair?"

**Block 3 — The economics:**

- Monthly production?
- Average production per visit? (what do you charge)
- Roughly what do you spend on marketing a month?
- What's a patient worth to you over the years they stay? (LTV)
- What are your expenses running a month — and does that include paying yourself?
- What are you actually taking home?

*[CAC math:]* "So if you're spending [$4k] a month and pulling [15] new patients, that's about [$270] to acquire each one. Which means those [400] overdue patients? You already paid something like [$100k] to get them the first time — and they're just drifting out the door."

*[OWNER-DEPENDENCY — go here, not at 'can you survive':]* "And you're producing most of that yourself, right? So if you slow down, it slows down."

**The trend question:**

"And where was all this a year ago — better, about the same, or worse?"

*[LABEL IT:]* "So it's been [flat / sliding] for about a year now. Is that fair to say?"

**Recap:**

"So just recapping what you told me: [18] people a month raise their hand and never make it in, [400] overdue patients nobody's touching, [1 in 5] no-showing, and it's been flat about a year. That's the picture. Fair?"

## The Problem

"So that's the picture on paper. What would you say is the biggest bottleneck in the practice?"

*[repeat it back, then dig — operator to operator, genuine curiosity, not a pain funnel:]*

- "Walk me through how that actually shows up — on a normal week, what does it look like?"
- "How often's that happening — a few times a week, every day?" → *[then YOU do the math: "so at [$250] a visit, that's real money walking out — and that's just this one thing." Don't make them calculate their own pain.]*
- "What've you already tried on it? …and where'd that come up short?"
- "And is it getting worse, holding steady, or fixing itself on its own?"

*[Then go quiet. The specifics + silence do the work — never ask "how does that make you feel."]*

## The Plan

*[Diagnostic: prescribe ONE thing — the biggest leak. Everything else is the ladder, teased not sold. Don't re-teach the problem; discovery did that.]*

**Frame:**

"So here's how I think about this. A practice only grows as fast as its biggest bottleneck — everything else is noise until that one's handled. So I'm not going to throw a stack of stuff at you. We fix the thing that's costing you the most right now, get it working, then we look at what's next. Sound fair?"

**Name the constraint:**

"From everything you just told me, the biggest hole is [X]. That's where I'd start."

**Then pitch ONLY that one:**

*[Overdue patients →]* "We call it Two-Way Recall. Here's why what you've tried didn't work — it was one-way. A postcard or a text blast can't answer 'how much will this cost?' or 'who is this?' — so the patient does nothing. Ours is an actual conversation: we text your overdue list, our recall agent handles the questions, then books them right onto the schedule like a dream front desk person. We ran it on a practice in the Chicago area — 657 overdue patients nobody had touched. It put 32 of them back on the schedule. About $9,600 in production, for roughly $55 in texts."

*[Inquiries not getting scheduled →]* "We plug in speed-to-lead. Right now when someone calls and nobody picks up — after hours, or the desk is slammed — that person just calls the next office. The AI texts them back within a minute, answers what they need, and books them. The practice that answers first almost always wins the patient."

*[No-shows →]* "We turn on no-show recovery. The second someone cancels or doesn't show, the AI texts them and gets them rebooked — before that slot becomes a hole in tomorrow's schedule."

*[Reviews / referrals →]* "We turn on the review and referral system. After a good visit, the AI asks your happy patients for a Google review right when they're most likely to leave one — and nudges your best ones to refer. That's what makes a stranger pick you over the office down the street."

**The dashboard:**

"And you watch the whole thing land on one dashboard, in real time. You're never guessing whether it's working."

**The ladder (tease — do NOT sell it):**

"Once that's running and paying for itself, the next thing I'd look at is [second gap]. But I don't want to touch that until this one's working."

## Objections

**Price / "it's a lot":**

"Totally fair — that's not a problem. When you say it's a lot — do you mean the timing of the cash, or you're not sure it's worth it yet?"

"Finances totally aside — just on the plan itself, how do you feel?"

- *Value:* "Fair. Here's how I'd think about it though — we're not turning on five things and hoping something sticks. We're fixing the one thing you just told me is costing you the most, and you'll see it working on the dashboard within days. The bigger risk is another year of [their leak]. Does that change it?"
- *Cash timing:* "That I can work with. Most people do it in full, but we can split it — [two payments]. Terms move, the price doesn't. Would that get it done today?"
- *Sticker shock:* "Compare it to a hire, not software — a front-desk person is [$3,500–4,500] a month, one shift, and never touches your overdue list. This runs 24/7. And we're only turning on the one piece right now, not the whole system — you decide on the rest once you've watched this one work."

**"I need to think about it":**

"Totally get it. Just so I'm clear — what's keeping you from being 100% sure this'll work for you?"

*[if "it'll work"]* "Then what's really left to think about? Let's just get it going."

**Spouse / partner:**

"Got it — other than running it by [partner], is there anything else holding you back?"

"What's that conversation actually about — whether to do it, the money, or the timing?"

"Crazy question — what if they say no?"

*[if "I'd have to think"]* "What would make them say no?" *[handle that, then]* "Sounds like you're solid on [it] — so would it be crazy to get you set up and just loop them in?"

**"How's this different from Weave / my front desk?":**

"On the surface everyone says they do this. The difference is one-way vs. two-way. Weave, your software, a postcard — they send. One direction. When the patient has a question, there's nobody on the other end, so they do nothing. Your front desk could answer, but they can't personally text 600 dormant patients for days. Ours holds a real back-and-forth with every one, at the same time, and answers the exact question standing between them and booking."

**"Will this work on MY patients?":**

"Honest answer — I'd rather show you than promise you. What I can tell you is we ran this on a practice in the Chicago area and it put 32 patients back on the schedule off a list nobody had touched. Same approach, your list. And we're starting with one piece, not the whole system — so it's not a big bet to find out."

**"I got burned before":**

"I get it — that's most people I talk to. If you made one bad hire, would you stop hiring? Every other vendor stops the second they hand you a lead or send a text. We measure in booked appointments. They never did that, right? The only real risk left is letting one bad experience talk you out of the thing that works."

**"Isn't this spammy?":**

"Fair concern — and it's built in. This isn't a blast. Every message is reviewed copy, with opt-out handling and emergency escalation baked in, and it talks like your best front-desk person, not a robot. You approve every word before a single text goes out. I can even send you the actual conversation transcripts."

**"We're slammed right now":**

"That's the argument for starting now — this takes work off your team's plate, not on. Your job is one export and a copy approval; we do the rest. Would it be crazy to get it running while you're slammed, so it's working in the background?"
