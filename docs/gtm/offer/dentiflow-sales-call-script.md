# Dentiflow — Sales Call Script & Objection System

**Model:** Diagnostic full-system sale, custom-priced live. ~35–40 min, one-call close.
**Backbone:** JP Middleton's consultative full-system structure. **Front of call + mechanics:** Acquisition.com intro/frame, metrics section + tonality, looping objection matrix, tie-downs, assumptive close.
**Status:** v3 — 2026-07-15. Defers to [dentiflow-offer-master.md](./dentiflow-offer-master.md) on every offer term. If this script and the master ever disagree on price, guarantee, or mechanism, **the master wins.**

---

## 0. The one thing this call does

The prospect already watched the [VSL](../vsl/vsl-reactivation-pilot-v3.md) — they've seen the full system and the recall receipt. They booked because something in it named their problem. **Your job on the call is not to teach the system again. It's to find where their practice is bleeding the most money, make them *feel* it, and prescribe the fix — starting with the one piece that comes with a guarantee.**

You are not selling a package off a shelf. You diagnose, then you prescribe. Some practices need the whole system. Some need recall plus one attach. **You only pitch what solves the leaks the diagnosis surfaces.** A prescription they didn't earn is a feature list; a prescription built from their own numbers is a decision.

**The non-negotiable spine of every deal (do not improvise around these):**

1. **Recall is deployed first on every single client.** It's the only module with proof and guarantee math. Even on a full-system close, recall is what turns on Day 0 — it earns the trust that makes the rest an easy yes.
2. **The money-back guarantee lives on recall only.** "5 booked patients in 30 days or it's free." Never guarantee a dollar outcome on the whole system — that math doesn't exist yet. Recall carries the risk reversal; everything else is upside.
3. **The pilot fee credits into the install.** Whatever they buy, the $1,250 is never lost money — it becomes a down payment.
4. **Never pitch a module that can't be turned on today.** See the module status tags in §6. Roadmap modules are shown as "what's next," never sold as live.
5. **Price against payroll, never software.** Above $499/mo you cannot win a software comparison. You win the staffing comparison. (§7)

## 1. Tone & delivery guide

Acquisition.com's tone table, recalibrated for **this** audience. You are not talking to a 28-year-old gym owner. You're talking to a practice owner — often 40–60, skeptical, pitched by a vendor every week, allergic to hype. Match the case-study register: **an operator talking to another operator. Not a pitchman.**

| Cue | When you use it |
|---|---|
| **Certain** | The mechanism, the proof, the guarantee, the price. Never waver here. |
| *Curious* | Every diagnosis question. You're genuinely trying to find the leak, not leading a witness. |
| **Understanding** | When they name a frustration. Land on it before you move. |
| Calm | Default. Founder energy, not closer energy. Silence is a tool — after the price, you go quiet. |
| \* = emphasis | Slow down and hit the word. Don't rush the number. |

**Voice rules (match the brand — same as the VSL and patient copy):**
- Sentence case, contractions, em-dashes for pauses.
- **One exclamation point per call, max. Preferably zero.** Conviction comes from certainty, not volume.
- Never "our team" / "our staff" — say "I" and "we."
- Don't out-talk them. The person asking the questions controls the call. The person talking the most is losing it.

## 2. Pre-call (2 minutes before you dial)

The sale starts before the call.

- **Have their application open.** Overdue count, practice name, location, what they said hurt worst. You reference these by memory, not by reading them a form back.
- **Pre-fill what you can.** Google review count + rating, number of locations, years open — look them up before you dial. Walking in already knowing their numbers reads as sharp and saves diagnosis time.
- **Confirm they're the decision-maker** from the form. If there's a partner/spouse/associate who shares the call, you want them *on* the call — flag it in the first two minutes, not at the close.
- **Notepad open.** You will write down: every metric, every leak, the numbers attached, their goal, their timeline, and their exact words for the pain. You'll quote those words back in the pitch.
- **Physiology:** shoulders back, smile before you dial, energy up one notch from theirs. You set the tone; they match it.

## 3. The call

### Part 1 — Intro (near-verbatim Acquisition.com — this is where authority is built)

Crisp, high-status, assumptive. **No chit-chat — "where are you calling from" is weak and cedes control.** You lead from word one. The "recorded line" line does the authority work instantly.

> "[Name], this is Trevor with Dentiflow on a recorded line — how's it going?"
>
> *[response — brief]*
>
> "Excited to chat. So I know you booked in after watching the video — we've only got about 35 minutes here, cool if we jump right in?"
>
> *[yes]*
>
> "Perfect. And just to confirm — you've got an ownership stake in the practice, right?"

Why it works: no warm-up to talk your way out of, a tight time box that says *your time is valuable and so is mine*, and the ownership question qualifies them while quietly asserting you only work with decision-makers.

### Part 2 — Frame & set the agenda (near-verbatim Acquisition.com — the takeaway posture)

This is the max-authority move. You are not auditioning for them — they're being assessed for fit. Keep the detached, assumptive control. **Do not soften "make sure it's a fit" into "I'd love to show you…"** — the takeaway posture is the whole point.

> "Great — so all we'll do today is spend a few minutes digging through a couple things on the practice, just to make sure it's a fit. And if it is, I'm happy to walk you through exactly what we'd do for you. Then from there, we can get you set up if it makes sense. Sound good?"

That's it. No disclaimer, no over-explaining. "Just to make sure it's a fit" + "if it makes sense" flips the frame: they're qualifying *for you*. Move straight into the diagnosis.

### Part 3 — Diagnosis: metrics first, then the pain (12–15 min)

Two phases, in this order — it's the Acq sequence, and the order matters. **Phase A (metrics)** is fast, easy, low-threat: you gather the numbers, and you quietly capture every pillar leak *as a number* so it never feels like an interrogation. **Phase B (the problem)** is slow: you take what hurts most and *sit in it*. Phase A gives you breadth and the anchoring ammo; Phase B is where the emotional selling happens.

Rule: Phase A stays cognitive and quick — don't linger. All the *weight* goes into Phase B. Getting the numbers is not the goal; getting them to *feel* the numbers is.

#### Phase A — The metrics section (~4–5 min, rapid but warm)

Frame it as getting the lay of the land, then rapid-fire. This is Acq's metrics move — the trick is that half these "metrics" are actually your pillar leaks in disguise, so you gather whole-system ammo without it feeling like a checklist.

> "Before I show you anything, I just want to get the lay of the land on the practice — a few quick numbers so I actually know what I'm working with. Cool if we rapid-fire through them?"

**Standard numbers (your anchoring ammo):**
- Monthly production *(revenue)*
- Average production per patient visit *(avg ticket)*
- New patients a month — and where most of them come from *(also your acquisition pillar)*
- Roughly what you spend on marketing a month *(feeds CAC)*
- Patient lifetime value — what's a patient worth over the years they stay? *(ballpark live if they don't know: annual value × years retained)*
- How long you've been open / how big the active patient base is

**Disguised pillar leaks (your breadth — gathered as data, not a survey):**
- "Roughly how many patients are on your overdue or unscheduled list?" *(→ recall)*
- "What's your no-show or same-day-cancel rate — ballpark?" *(→ no-show recovery)*
- "How many Google reviews are you sitting on, and what's the rating?" *(→ reviews — pre-filled before the call)*
- "When a new patient calls and nobody picks up, how fast does someone get back to them?" *(→ speed-to-lead)*
- "How many referrals do you get in a month?" *(→ referrals)*

**Then do the CAC math out loud — this is the first anchor:**

> "So if you're spending [$4k] a month and pulling [15] new patients, that's roughly [$270] to acquire each one. Which means those [400] overdue patients on your list? You already paid something like [$100k] to get them the first time — and right now they're just drifting out the door. That land the way it does for me?"

**Recap the stats that sting (Acq's move — pick the 2–3 that hurt most):**

> "Okay — so just recapping what you told me: [400 overdue, ~$270 to acquire each, almost 1 in 5 no-showing, 40 reviews]. That's the picture. Fair?"

You now have breadth — every pillar has a number — and your anchor is set. Now you slow down.

#### Phase B — The problem: what actually bugs them, then dig (~7–9 min — this is where you sit in it)

The weight here comes from **genuine curiosity and specificity — not a pain funnel.** Owners have heard "and how does that make you feel?" from a dozen closers; the second a question smells like a sales move, you lose them. So you ask like an operator who actually wants to understand their practice, get concrete, and let *them* hear themselves describe the broken thing. Silence after every answer. Specificity + silence does the emotional work — you never have to reach for it, and you never make them calculate their own pain (that's the tell that kills it — *you* do the math out loud instead).

> "So that's the picture on paper. What's the part that actually bugs you day to day — the one you'd fix first if you could?"

*[they name it — repeat it back concise, then dig. Do NOT move on. This is the excavation.]*

**The dig — operator to operator, not a script (pull the ones that fit):**
- "Walk me through how that actually shows up — on a normal week, what does it look like?" *(concrete + present-tense beats "tell me about a time it cost you")*
- "How often's that happening — a few times a week, every day?" → *then **you** do the math out loud: "so at [$250] a visit, that's real money walking out the door — and that's just this one thing." Never make them multiply their own pain.*
- "What've you already tried on it? …and where'd that come up short?" *(curious about why it failed — not "how'd it feel")*
- "And is it getting worse, holding steady, or fixing itself on its own?" *(grounded trajectory — the honest answer is almost always "worse," which future-paces the bleed without the fear theater)*

*Curious, quiet, unhurried.* The pain surfaces because they're describing a broken process in detail — not because you prompted the emotion. That's the difference between sitting in it and reaching for it.

#### The expand move (this is how you sell the system, not one module)

Once you've excavated their self-named constraint, use the metrics from Phase A to widen the wound — surface the leaks they *didn't* name:

> "So [new patients] is the big one — and we'll fix that. But here's what I can't unsee from your numbers: you're also sitting on [400] overdue patients nobody's working, and losing almost [1 in 5] booked patients to no-shows. Those aren't small — they're two more holes bleeding money right next to the one you named. When I build your plan, do you want me to fix just the one, or plug all three?"

Their answer sizes the deal. Because you *have the numbers*, this isn't a pitch — it's you showing them leaks they were blind to. That's the whole-system prescription, earned instead of pushed. **This is the escape from the self-diagnosis trap:** their answer is the entry to the deep dive, never the ceiling on the deal.

#### Goal + why-now (make change necessary)

> "Last thing before I show you the plan — where do you actually want the practice to be, and by when?" → "Why that number specifically?" → "And why now, versus a year ago?"

The tie-down:

> "So — [the leaks], costing you for [time], nothing you've tried has moved it, and you want [their goal] by [their timeline]. Staying on the current path… does that get you there?"

*Curious, flat.* When they conclude "something has to change" **in their own words**, discovery is done. You don't say it for them.

### Part 5 — The prescription (pitch only what they need) (5–7 min)

**Discovery already sold the problem — don't re-teach it here.** Name the fix, prove the one you can prove, and move. Recall is the only piece you spend real words on (the mechanism is your differentiator); every attach is a sentence or two. If you catch yourself re-explaining their pain, stop — they lived it ten minutes ago. Prescribe **only the leaks you confirmed in the diagnosis** (their named constraint + whatever they green-lit in the expand move). Recall always goes first.

**Frame (one line):**

> "So here's what I'd do. Think of it as an AI front office that never clocks out — it handles the repetitive stuff that's costing you. We turn it on easiest-win first, so you're booking patients before we spend a dollar on ads. For you, it's [N] pieces."

**Recall — always first (the one place you spend words, because the mechanism is the differentiator):**

> "First is Two-Way Recall — and here's why everything you've tried failed: it was one-way. A postcard or a text blast can't answer 'how much will this cost?' or 'who is this?' — so the patient does nothing. Ours is two-way: the AI texts your overdue list, and the second they reply, it handles the cost and scheduling questions like your best front-desk person, and books them. Same kind of list a blast ignored booked *32 patients* for us."

**Proof + guarantee:**

> "That was about **$9,600 in production for $55 in texts.** And it's the piece I put my money behind — fewer than 5 booked in your first 30 days, it's free. Five roughly covers the cost, so it pays for itself or it's free. Fair?"

**Attach — one or two sentences per module, only the confirmed leaks (no Formula A on the call):**

> "Then, based on what you told me — [no-shows] the second a patient cancels, the AI re-books that slot before it's a hole in tomorrow's schedule. [reviews] after a good visit, it asks your happy patients for a Google review automatically. Each one plugs a leak that's costing you right now."

**Dashboard:**

> "And you watch all of it land on one dashboard, in real time. Make sense?"

*(The full market-mistake → problem → consequence → fix "Formula A" build in §6 is the long form — use it only if a specific module genuinely needs the case made. Default to the one-liner; the tension was already built in discovery.)*

---

### Part 6 — Temp check (1 min)

Before you name a price, find out if they believe the *process*. Not you, not the price — the process.

> "Just in terms of the plan itself — how are you feeling? On a scale of 1 to 10, how well do you feel this would actually fix [their #1 leak]?"

- **9–10 + certain tone** → move to the close.
- **8 or below** → "Got it — and I appreciate the honesty. What's keeping it from being a 9 or 10?" → that's your real objection. Handle it (§8), then re-temp before pricing. **Never price into hesitation.**

---

### Part 7 — The close (onboarding → investment → assumptive)

#### 7a. Onboarding first (paint the "after")

Reduce uncertainty by showing there's a real, structured path — before the number.

> "So if we move forward, here's exactly what happens. We knock out the agreement and payment right now so nothing stalls. Then you send me one thing — a CSV export of your patient list — and you approve the message copy. That's your entire job. From there we select your first 200 overdue patients, and the first texts go out inside a few days. First bookings usually land within days of that first send. You watch it happen on the dashboard. Sound good?"

#### 7b. The investment — say it, then stop

You already confirmed they believe the plan on the temp check, so you've earned the right to just name the number. **No preamble, no defending it after.** The more you justify, the cheaper it sounds — and the more you signal you expect a fight. Say it slow, land it, then go quiet. The staffing frame (§7) stays holstered — you only draw it if they flinch on price, never before.

**If they green-lit the system:**

> "So here's the investment. We start with the recall pilot — guaranteed — at **$1,250**, and it credits straight into the build. The full system is **[$X] to set up and [$X] a month.**"

**If they green-lit recall only:**

> "So here's the investment. The pilot's **$1,250**, guaranteed — then it's **[$499] a month** to run it across your whole list."

*[Say the number slowly. Land it. Then stop.]*

**Then silence. Whoever talks first loses.** Don't add a sentence, don't explain the number, don't fill the air. Let them sit in it and answer.

> **Pricing rules — read before every call:**
> - **Recall pilot: $1,250 one-time. Split-pay fallback: 2 × $675 (the ONLY downsell — never discount, never shrink the 200-patient segment).** Continuity: $499/mo.
> - **Full-system install & retainer are custom, built live.** The figures above (`~$5k install`, retainer) are **proposals — confirm the real numbers from actual attributable-production data before quoting. Never wing a system price from gut.**
> - **Multi-location / group / DSO deals are still gated** until multi-location isolation ships. Do not close a multi-location practice on a full rollout yet — sell the single-location pilot and flag the group expansion as "next, once we prove it here."
> - **Capacity is real: 3 concurrent pilots max.** State it truthfully as scarcity; never oversell past it.

#### 7c. The assumptive close

If they're in, don't ask "so do you want to do it?" — assume it and move to logistics:

> "Awesome — I'm genuinely excited for you on this. Let's get you set up. For the pilot, are you using Visa, Mastercard, or Amex?"

Then: process payment live → request the CSV export before you hang up → set the copy-approval step → book the onboarding call. **The deal is not done until payment is collected AND the export is scheduled. Do it live. Sending things "after" loses deals.**

## 6. Module library (prescribe from here — status tags are law)

Pitch a module **only** if (a) its pillar came back empty or weak in the metrics section, and (b) it's tagged LIVE. ROADMAP modules are shown as "here's what's next once recall proves out" — never sold as turn-on-today.

| Module | Status | Prescribe when the diagnosis showed… |
|---|---|---|
| **Two-Way Recall** (win-back) | **LIVE · PROVEN · GUARANTEED** | Always first, every deal. |
| **Speed-to-lead** (missed-call/new-lead text-back in minutes) | **LIVE · unproven** | slow / no follow-up on missed calls and new leads |
| **No-show recovery** | **LIVE · unproven** | high no-show / same-day-cancel rate, no refill process |
| **Reviews & referrals** | **LIVE · unproven** | low review count, not automated / no systematic referral engine |
| **Live dashboard** | **LIVE** | Connective tissue — mention on every deal ("you can watch it work"). |
| **AI voice receptionist** | **ROADMAP — confirm live before pitching as turn-on-today** | Show as "what's coming" only, until you confirm it ships. |
| **New-patient ads** | **LIVE — but always the LAST piece turned on** | not enough new patients — and only after the internal systems are converting. |

**Attach pitch = one or two sentences (default).** Name it, say what it does for them, tie it to their leak. Example (no-show): *"The second a patient cancels, the AI texts them and re-books that slot before it becomes a hole in tomorrow's schedule — every save is production you'd have lost."* Only expand into the full market-mistake → problem → consequence → fix build if a module genuinely needs the case made — and even then, keep it under a minute.

> **Unproven-module honesty rule (do not skip):** For any module tagged *unproven*, you may pitch what it *does*, but you do **not** attach a number, percentage, or guarantee to it — recall is the only piece with a receipt. If they ask "what results do you get on no-shows?" → *"Honestly, recall's the one I have hard numbers on, which is why it's the one I guarantee. The rest I'd rather prove on your practice than quote you a stat I can't back."* That honesty closes more than a fake number does.

## 7. The staffing frame (memorize this — it's how you win above $499)

At $499/mo you're compared to Weave and you win. Above that, a software comparison kills you. So you never let the comparison be software.

> "Compare it to a hire, not a subscription. A front-desk person is [$3,500–4,500]/mo fully loaded *(validate the local number before you quote it)*, works one shift, takes vacation, and never once works your overdue list. This is an AI front office — answers every after-hours call, texts every overdue patient, chases every no-show, asks for every review, 24/7. Against a salary, the retainer is cheap."

Every time price tension shows up on a full-system deal, you return here.

## 8. Objection system

**The framework (Acquisition.com's matrix + JP's isolate/tie-down):**

**Step 1 — Loop.** Never answer the surface objection cold. First: *"I completely hear you — do you mind if I ask you a quick question?"* → "Do you think fixing [their #1 leak] is something that'd genuinely grow the practice?" Get them re-affirming value.

**Step 2 — Isolate.** *"Got it — so other than [the objection], is there anything else holding you back?"* This flushes out whether it's the real objection or a smokescreen. If more appears, you just found the actual objection.

**Step 3 — Handle, then ask for the order.** Overcome the isolated objection, then go straight back to the close. Don't leave the door open.

### Price / "it's a lot" / "can't do that right now"

1. **Align:** "Totally fair — that's not a problem."
2. **Clarify:** "When you say it's a lot — do you mean the timing of the cash, or you're not sure it's worth it yet? Just so I understand." *(These are different objections. Cost-of-cash = payment structure. Not-sure-worth-it = value, loop back to proof.)*
3. **Isolate:** "Finances totally aside — just on the plan itself, how do you feel?" *(Make sure money's the real issue, not a mask.)*
4. **Handle:**
   - **If it's the value** → back to the recall guarantee. *"Here's the thing — this is exactly why we start with the guaranteed piece. You're not betting on my word. If recall doesn't book 5 in 30 days, you're not out a dollar. So the risk isn't really on the money — it's on doing nothing. Does that change it?"*
   - **If it's the cash timing** → *"That I can work with. Most people do the pilot in full, but for some it makes more sense to split it — we can do two payments of $675. Terms move, the price doesn't. Would that get it done today?"* (Split-pay is the ONLY concession. Never discount, never shrink the segment.)
   - **Full-system sticker** → run the **staffing frame (§7)**, then de-risk down to the guaranteed pilot: *"Tell you what — you don't have to decide the whole system today. Start with the guaranteed pilot. Prove recall on your own patients, it credits toward the rest, and you decide on the system once you've watched it work. Fair?"*

### "I need to think about it"

The smokescreen. Make the intangible tangible:

> "Totally get it — no problem. Just so I'm clear on my end: what's keeping you from being 100% sure this'll actually work for you?"

- If *"no, I think it'll work"* → *"Then what's really left to think about? Let's just get it going."*
- If they name a real concern → you just converted "think about it" into a specific objection. Isolate and handle it.

### Spouse / partner / associate / office manager

(And this is why you asked about decision-makers in Part 1.)

> "Got it — other than running it by [partner], is there anything else holding you back?" *[isolate]*
>
> "So I can help — what's that conversation with them actually about? Is it whether to do it, the money, or the timing?" *[context]*
>
> "Crazy question — what if they say no?" *(curious)*
> - *"I'd do it anyway"* → close.
> - *"I'd have to really think"* → "Fair. What would make them say no?" → **that's the real objection.** Handle it, then: "I know you want to talk to [them], but it sounds like you're solid on [the objection they'd raise] — so would it be crazy to get you set up and just loop them in?"

### "How's this different from Weave / RevenueWell / my front desk?"

This is the mechanism objection — and it's a gift, because the mechanism *is* your answer.

> "Great question — because on the surface everyone says they do recall. The difference is one-way vs. two-way. Weave, your software, a postcard — they *send*. One direction. When the patient has a question — cost, insurance, scheduling — there's nobody on the other end, so they do nothing. Your front desk *could* answer, but they can't personally text 400 dormant patients and stay on it for days. Ours holds an actual back-and-forth conversation with every single one, at the same time, and answers the exact question standing between them and booking. That's the whole game — and it's why the same list that ignored the blast booked 32 appointments for us."

### "Will this even work on MY patients?" / skepticism

> "Honestly? That's the exact reason we built it the way we did. I'm not asking you to believe me — I'm asking you to let me prove it on your own list, with a guarantee that you don't pay if it doesn't hit 5. The only way to know if your dormant patients come back is to reach out to them the right way. Worst case, you get every dollar back. Best case, you've got a system that pays for itself every month."

### "I got burned by an agency/vendor before"

> "I completely get it — and honestly, that's most people I talk to. Quick question though: if you made one bad hire, would you stop hiring? Probably not. The difference here is simple — every other vendor stops the second they hand you a lead or send a text. We measure in *booked appointments*, and I put money behind the first thing we turn on. They never did that, right? So the only real risk left is letting one bad experience talk you out of the thing that actually works."

### "Isn't this just spammy mass-texting?" / compliance fear

> "Fair concern — and the answer's built in. This isn't a blast. Every message is partner-reviewed copy, it's got opt-out handling and emergency escalation baked in, and it talks like your best front-desk person, not a robot. You approve every word before a single text goes out. And I can send you the actual conversation transcripts from the case study — you can read every word it said."

### Dates / timing / "we're slammed right now"

> "That's actually the argument *for* starting now, not later — because this is the thing that takes work *off* your team's plate, not on. And your job is literally one export and a copy approval; we do the rest. Would it be crazy to get the guaranteed piece running while you're slammed, so it's working in the background?"

## 9. If they don't close today (pipeline)

- **Never end without a next step booked.** Lock the follow-up on the calendar before you hang up.
- **Between now and then, send value:** the case study, the anonymized conversation transcripts, Scott's reference-call offer *(only if they questioned whether the numbers are real — Scott can vouch as the practice owner who ran it; "I'm happy to hop on 15 min with you to verify")*.
- **Log everything** in the CRM: the metrics, the leaks the diagnosis surfaced, their goal, the exact objection. Set the follow-up task. Move them to the right pipeline stage.
- Weekly touch after that. They'll buy from someone — make it you.

## 10. Guardrails baked into this script (do not violate to close a deal)

These are not negotiable in the moment:

- **Recall guarantee is the only guarantee.** No dollar promises on the whole system. (§0, §7)
- **Recall deploys first on every deal.** It's the trust engine. (§0)
- **Pilot fee credits into the install.** Always. (§7)
- **Never sell a ROADMAP module as live.** Confirm status before pitching. (§6)
- **No "books directly into your PMS"** — the AI books via booking link until PMS integration is live.
- **200-patient minimum segment. Never shrink it to close.** Split-pay is the only concession; price never drops. (§8)
- **Multi-location/group/DSO full rollouts are gated** until isolation ships — sell single-location, flag the group as "next." (§7)
- **Scott is a client, not a co-founder.** Frame as "the practice we ran this in / our first client." Familia name-drop needs Scott's sign-off. Trevor stays off the ads; Scott is the face.
- **Never quote an unverified number.** Every non-recall stat is off-limits until it has real data.
- **All Twilio safety rules apply to every pilot send** once they onboard.
