## Why interview format

Memorized monologues read stiff on camera, especially for non-actors. An interview pulls the same credibility beats out conversationally — the subject answers a question instead of reciting a speech. Trevor (off camera, voice cut out in the edit) asks; Scott and Dr. Phillip answer in their own words.

**How to run it:**
- The questions below are the interviewer's. The "beats to land" under each are what a strong answer covers — give them to Scott/Dr. Phillip ahead of time so they can think, but **do not have them memorize lines.**
- Record each answer as its own clip. Re-ask if an answer rambles or misses a beat — first takes are usually rough.
- Off-camera interviewer voice gets cut; only the subject is heard. So answers should restate the question's premise ("The reason recall is so hard is…") rather than starting with "Yeah, so…".
- Keep Trevor's name and any "founder of Dentiflow" framing out of every take. Scott and Dr. Phillip are 32 Dental — an arms-length practice that ran the test. They are not Dentiflow.

**Locked numbers (the current, confirmed set — the old A/B "11 vs 0" figures are retired):**
- 657 dormant patients texted across the pilot → **32 booked** (~4.9%)
- **145 booking-link clicks** (~22%)
- Industry-typical: 2–5% click, 1–3% dormant→booked
- Revenue: **$8,000 in new production, confirmed** (32 bookings × ~$250; $54.38 actual texting cost → "$8,000 for ~$55 in texts"). **Nobody quotes a dollar figure on camera** — on-screen data carries the money; the people carry the credibility.

## Logistics (both subjects)

- **Shot:** head-and-shoulders, eyes slightly above center frame, plain or practice background. Solid shirt, not white.
- **Audio:** quiet room. Phone or webcam is fine if the room is quiet and lit.
- **Length target:** Scott ~3–4 min of usable answers (cut to ~2 min). Dr. Phillip ~2–3 min (cut to ~45–60 sec + one pull-quote for a slide).
- Send raw files to Trevor; no editing on their end.

# Part 1 — Scott Wills

**Lower-third:** Scott Wills · Co-founder, 32 Dental | Former CFO + CEO, Familia Dental

**Conversion arc:** each question quietly answers one objection between the viewer and a booked call — (Q1) why listen to you · (Q2) is this even my problem · (Q3) isn't this just another vendor · (Q4) did it actually work · (Q5) why doesn't my current setup already do this · (Q6) why bother / why now / what's the risk. Scott's two identities both get used on purpose: **current PPO practice owner = peer/relatability (the lead), former 50-location DSO operator = authority/scale (the support).** Owner first, expert second.

### Q1. "Tell me who you are."
Beats to land:
- **Lead with the peer identity:** co-owner of 32 Dental, three PPO locations in Elk Grove Village, IL — "I'm in the same seat you are."
- **Then stack the authority:** before that, CFO and then CEO of Familia Dental, a ~50-location DSO across the Midwest.
- Say it fast — owner first, DSO second. That ordering says *peer* before it says *expert*. Don't meander into 32 Dental's history.
- (Don't claim any Dentiflow title. You're the practice the test ran at.)

### Q2. "As a practice owner yourself — what was sitting in your own system before you ran this?"
*Job: make the viewer feel seen. First-person, not a generic industry answer.*
Beats to land:
- His own dormant list — hundreds of patients who were good patients and just drifted. "I knew they were there."
- The owner's admission: "I'm the owner, and I *still* wasn't doing anything about them — not because I didn't care, but because nobody has the time to chase them one by one."
- The authority kicker: "I had 50 locations and a full team at Familia and couldn't crack this at scale either." → tells the viewer *don't feel bad you haven't solved this.*

### Q3. "When yet another vendor showed up pitching recall, what went through your head?"
*Job: voice the viewer's own skepticism, then overcome it.*
Beats to land:
- Honest "oh great, another one" — he'd seen plenty of recall tools, none did much.
- Why he said yes anyway: basically no downside — his own dormant list, patients already in the system.
- He wasn't expecting much. (A skeptic who's more skeptical than the viewer makes the payoff land harder.)

### Q4. "What surprised you?"
*Job: the emotional payoff. Specific and human, not a stat.*
Beats to land:
- These were **his** patients, coming back to **his** chairs — people he'd written off.
- The surprise: it didn't lead with a discount and still got them back.
- (Let the on-screen numbers carry the scale — **don't quote a dollar amount or a percentage you'd have to remember.** "More than I expected from a list I'd basically written off" is the right altitude.)

### Q5. "You've used the other tools. Why did this work when those didn't?"
*Job: invalidate their current setup ("my front desk does recall" / "my software does this").*
Beats to land:
- It's not fancier SMS technology — it doesn't out-engineer Weave or NexHealth.
- It follows up the way a person would *if they actually had the time* — different message, different angle, over a few days. A front desk can't text 600 dormant patients like that; a blast tool sends once and stops.
- Built by people who thought about how patients *reply* to texts, not just how to send them.

### Q6. "You ran this on your own practice. What do you tell another owner who's on the fence?"
*Job: carry the booking — risk reversal + measured urgency + personal vouch. This is the money question.*
Beats to land:
- It's your own list — patients already in the system, that **you already paid to acquire the first time.** (the reframe, stated as fact, not a pitch)
- Measured urgency: every month they sit there they drift further out — and eventually they find another dentist. The line: *"You already spent the money to get these patients the first time. They're just sitting there — the only question is whether you go get them before someone else does."*
- Risk reversal: it's your own patients and it costs basically nothing to test, so there's little risk in finding out.
- The vouch (only Scott can say this): *"I ran it on my own practice. I'm not pitching you something I read about — I did it."*
- *(No "guaranteed," no hard sell. The urgency is a fact he states flatly, not volume.)*

**Scott — avoid:**
- ❌ Any Dentiflow title or "I work with them" framing — you're the practice, period.
- ❌ Dollar figures or specific percentages from memory — the edit shows those.
- ❌ Competitor takedowns — naming Weave as the industry default is fine; trashing them is not.
- ❌ Promising the viewer's practice a specific outcome, or the word "guaranteed."

# Part 2 — Dr. Phillip `[LAST NAME — confirm]`

**Lower-third:** Dr. Phillip `[LAST NAME — confirm]` · Co-founder + Practicing Dentist, 32 Dental

> **Note on quotes:** the beats below are *prompts*, not words to put in his mouth. Whatever ends up on screen — especially a pulled quote slide — must be **his own words**, recorded or written and approved by him. The `[DRAFT]` example lines exist only to show the *kind* of sentence we're after; he approves, rewrites, or replaces them.

### Q1. "Introduce yourself — your role at 32 Dental and what your day looks like."
Beats to land:
- Name, role: co-founder and a practicing dentist at 32 Dental — he's chairside with patients, not just on the business side.
- This grounds the clinical credibility: the person endorsing it actually treats the patients who came back.

### Q2. "From a clinical standpoint, what does it mean when a patient goes a year without coming in?"
Beats to land:
- Patients fall out of their cleaning rhythm — life gets busy, they're not in pain, so they don't book.
- The longer the gap, the more likely small things become bigger things — routine care is what keeps it from getting there.
- *Keep it matter-of-fact and non-alarmist. The point is "regular care matters," not scaring anyone.*

### Q3. "These patients who came back through the test — what were they like? Did they actually show up?"
Beats to land:
- They were real, normal patients — people who'd just drifted, not problem patients.
- They showed up and got the care they were due for. (Speak to whatever he actually saw — don't invent specifics.)
- It didn't create chaos for the front desk; the bookings came in like any other appointment.

### Q4. "These patients came back without being offered a discount. As a dentist, why does that matter to you?"
Beats to land:
- He doesn't love discount-driven recall — it trains patients to wait for a deal and quietly says the care is worth less.
- Getting people back because the outreach was *human*, not because it was cheap, is the right way to do it.
- `[DRAFT — Dr. Phillip to approve or say in his own words]:` *"I'd rather a patient come back because someone reached out like a person, not because we waved a coupon at them."*

### Q5. "Would you keep using this? What would you tell another dentist?"
Beats to land:
- A clear, plain sign-off — he's comfortable having it run on his patients.
- One clean sentence usable as the pull-quote slide.
- `[DRAFT — Dr. Phillip to approve/replace]:` *"It got patients I'd basically given up on back in the chair, and it did it in a way I'm comfortable putting my name on."*

**Dr. Phillip — avoid:**
- ❌ Specific clinical claims about individual patients he can't stand behind.
- ❌ Diagnosis-flavored or fear-based language ("you'll lose teeth," etc.) — stay calm and routine.
- ❌ Dollar figures.
- ❌ Anything implying he's part of Dentiflow — he's a 32 Dental dentist who let it run on his list.
