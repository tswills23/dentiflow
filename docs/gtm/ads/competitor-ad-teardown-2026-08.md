## 2. Format census

### RevenueWell — "the big stat card" ⭐ the 8-month survivor

Reproducible spec:
- **338 × 600** (9:16 vertical), solid dark ground with a **thin bright border frame** inset ~8px
- Colorways rotate — navy `#012440` / border `#00A5FD` · plum `#221A31` / `#9773F7` ·
  deep teal `#01333A` / `#00CBA3`
- **Top:** small angled parallelogram tag, "Attract. Simplify. Engage." — one of the three words
  bolded, and which word is bolded rotates
- **Center:** an **enormous numeral** in the accent color, occupying ~35% of canvas height
  ("88%", "+432", ">40%")
- **Below:** 2–3 lines of sans-serif at ~1/8 the numeral's size. The key phrase is *italic + bold in
  the accent color*; the rest is white
- **Bottom:** dotted vertical rule leading to a centered RevenueWell logo
- **No photo. No person. No CTA button.** Very high negative space
- **The variable that changes between creatives: the statistic and the colorway.** Same skeleton.

### NexHealth — two formats, both current

**(a) Charcoal illustration** — the June 4 collections cluster, 338 × 600, ground `#1F2025`
- Headline top-aligned, two lines: first in white, second in **mint/teal**
- Centered **monochrome ink/pencil illustration** of a staff member holding the problem object —
  a stack of statements, an open appointment book
- No logo, no CTA button, no color beyond the accent line
- Examples: *"Stop mailing statements. / Start collecting balances."* · *"Your front desk is doing /
  the software's job."*

**(b) Photo + caption bar** — the April 9 survivor, 600 × 600 square
- Real photograph (a monitor showing an aging-AR report; a practice owner, arms folded, in an op)
- **White bar across the bottom third** with a heavy black headline
- Reads like a news thumbnail, not an ad: *"How Do Dentists Collect Old Unpaid Balances"*

### Dental Intelligence — lifestyle photo + bottom headline
338 × 600, full-bleed lifestyle photo (man asleep in bed), headline bottom-third in white with the
payoff line in brand green, logo lockup **"dental intelligence | AI Receptionist"**.
*"Rest easy. / We've got the night shift."* Selling the AI receptionist specifically.

### Weave — meme + brand cards
The oldest Weave creative is a **Star Wars Anakin/Padmé four-panel meme**:
*"We had 20 no-shows this week.." / "We're using Weave right..?" / [stare] / "Right..?"*
480 × 600. The rest of the sample is product/feature and brand cards. Highest churn, lowest
consistency of the four.

## 3. The five findings

**1. The entire category is selling the front desk's workload — not patient revenue.**
NexHealth's whole June cluster is accounts-receivable. Dental Intelligence is after-hours phone
coverage. Weave is no-shows and phones. RevenueWell is retention and admin time. **Nobody in this
sample is advertising dormant-patient reactivation.** That lane is open.

**2. The longest-running creative in the category is one statistic on a colored card.**
No photo, no person, no testimonial, no button. RevenueWell's stat card has been live 8.5 months.
And their numbers are **averages** — "on average, 88% patient retention," "an average of 432 new
patients." Ours is a receipt from one named group. Same format, stronger payload.

**3. Zero risk reversal across ~348 active ads.** Every CTA in the sample is **Learn More** → demo.
Not one guarantee, not one performance promise, in any of the four accounts. This confirms at the
*ad* level what the landing pages already showed.

**4. NexHealth is running our exact psychological frame, on a different asset — and has been for
months.** *"Collect What You've Already Earned."* *"Turn Aging Balances Into Revenue."* *"Every
unpaid balance is revenue your practice already earned."*

That is **loss-recovery framing on money the practice has already banked the work for.** It's the
Dentiflow frame applied to receivables instead of patients, running since April and scaled in June.
**This validates recovery framing against our exact buyer** — and it resolves the open
recovery-vs-avoidance question: recovery is proven here, avoidance appears nowhere.

**5. "Your front desk" is the category's proven callout — and everyone resolves it with a tool.**
NexHealth: *"Your front desk is doing the software's job"* · *"Your Staff Was Hired for Patients, Not
Paperwork"* · *"Stop Hiring to Keep Up with Busywork."* RevenueWell: *"Your Front Desk, Always
Covered."* Three of four advertisers, repeatedly. Every one of them resolves it by giving the front
desk better software — which still means the front desk operates it.

## 4. What to build — four ads, mapped

**A. The stat card, carrying a receipt instead of an average.**
Take RevenueWell's 8.5-month-proven format — huge numeral, dark ground, thin accent frame, one line
of context, logo, no button — and put **32** in it. *"32 patients rebooked from one overdue list."*
Their proven format carrying the one claim they structurally cannot make. Lowest-risk build here.

**B. Move NexHealth's recovery frame from balances to patients.**
They recover money already earned. We recover **patients already paid for** — and the acquisition
cost of a patient dwarfs an aging balance. Same proven frame, bigger asset, and it's the frame our
offer was already built on.

**C. Take the front-desk callout, resolve it harder.**
Every competitor's answer is "better software for your front desk." Ours is *nobody on your team
touches it.* Same validated pain, an answer none of them can give without changing their product.

**D. Lead a variant with the guarantee.**
348 active ads, zero risk reversal. "12 bookings in 30 days or we work free" isn't a better ad in
this category — it's the only one of its kind.

**Format guidance from the sample:** build **338 × 600 vertical** first — it dominates the
long-runners across two different advertisers. Square 600 × 600 for the photo/caption-bar variant.

## 5. Honest limits

- **No spend or performance data exists.** Longevity and volume are commitment signals only.
- **Sampled 26–68% by advertiser.** Weave especially is under-sampled; I did not see 156 of its 210
  active ads and cannot say what angles are in them.
- **Only currently-active ads are visible.** There's no archive of what these advertisers tried and
  killed, so absence of an angle here is not evidence it failed.
- Video and carousel creatives were not captured — this is a **static-ad** teardown.
