This is the generator, not the ads. The ads are the output of running our real pain-point research
through Hormozi's 3-part structure. Every hook below is grounded in a verbatim quote from the
research — that's the point of doing both: **his structure, their words.**

## 0. Why v2 needed rebuilding

The first batch was structured with the 13-beat SGS layout from
[meta-campaign-build.md](./meta-campaign-build.md) §3. That's a real framework and the copy is sound,
but measured against Leads it under-delivers on the dimension that matters most:

| Dimension | v2 batch | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Call Out type | 8 of 8 were **Labels** | Zero Yes Questions, If-Then, or Ridiculous Results. Hormozi: *"80% of your effort goes here."* |
| Value elements | Dream outcome + Risk | Speed, Ease, Time Delay, Sacrifice largely unused |
| WHO (status) | 0 of 8 | The single biggest untapped source of angles |
| WHEN (timeline) | Present tense | *"Negative future visualization is especially powerful"* — used once |
| Volume | 8 ads | *"The top 0.1% of Facebook advertisers test 11× more creative than everyone else."* |

**Diagnosis:** 8 ads that are really 1–2 angles restyled. Meta collapses look-alike ads into one
entity, so that portfolio performs like one or two ads no matter how many we upload.

## 1. WHAT — the 8 value elements, mapped to dental recall

Hormozi's grid: four positive (toward) and four negative (away from). Each cell below carries the
VOC quote that proves the element is real for this buyer.

### Toward

**① Dream outcome**
Full hygiene columns, filled by patients they already own, with zero staff effort.
> *"There should be absolutely no patient that leaves your office without an appointment booked."*
> — office administrator, 30 years, r/Dentistry

**② Perceived likelihood of achievement**
The case-study numbers, the named booked list, the counting method agreed before send, the guarantee.
Hormozi's 11 ways to raise this are the whole reason the pilot is structured the way it is.
> *"None of the results they promised were ever put in writing and no measurable improvements were
> delivered."* — RevenueWell, SoftwareAdvice ★1

**③ Speed**
First bookings land within days of the first send (Day 0/1/3 sequence), not months.
> *"I was told to be patient and the money spent on the SEO and Paid Google Ads will pay off."*
> — practice owner, Capterra — *the exact opposite experience*

**④ Ease**
One CSV export. One copy approval. That is the practice's entire workload.
> *"we have to go in manually to sync the schedule almost everyday"* — Solutionreach, Capterra ★1

### Away from

**⑤ Nightmare — life without it**
The list grows silently; hygiene goes soft two months later; nobody can name the week it started.
> *"it just quietly grows until someone panics about the schedule being soft two months out."*
> — r/DentalAssistant

**⑥ Risk — the chance it won't work**
They've been burned. This is the strongest negative element in this market by volume.
> *"That is not a partnership, it is a trap."* — Podium, BBB ★1

**⑦ Time delay — how slow the current path is**
A front desk can realistically make 20–30 recall calls a day between patients. A 500-name list is
weeks of work that never gets a clear run.
> *"They gave me packet after packet of people to recall… It really never ended."* — r/DentalAssistant

**⑧ Sacrifice — what they give up by doing nothing**
Every patient on that list was acquired once at full cost, and is now walking into a competitor.
> *"No one ever bothered reaching out or gave a damn."* — patient of 20 years, explaining why they left

## 2. WHO — status perspectives (the untapped dimension)

Hormozi: show each value element through the eyes of every person whose opinion the buyer cares
about. *"Your wife notices you pulling your belt two notches tighter"* hits differently than
*"you lost 20 lbs."* Zero of the v2 ads did this. Each of these is a fresh ad angle.

| Perspective | The angle | VOC anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **The hygienist** | She's checking her phone at 2pm because there's nobody in her chair — and she's updating her résumé | *"I'd get 4 to 5 paid hours instead of the full 8… It's throwing my budget off entirely and I'm thinking of applying out"* |
| **The front desk** | You're asking the person who's already drowning to do the one job nobody thanks them for | *"i feel like a telemarketer"* |
| **The associate** | Empty columns mean they're doing the maths on leaving | *"I'm getting concerned about next month's paycheck"* |
| **The patient who left** | They're on someone else's schedule right now, and they never told you why | *"Did not feel it was my place to let him know why I left."* |
| **The peer down the road** | Everyone assumes everyone else is full — including the ones who aren't | *"online crap leave us more on our own island since everyone else is killing it"* |
| **The DSO competitor** | Corporate is outbidding you for strangers while your own patients sit uncontacted | *"You're never going to compete with that."* |
| **Their own past self** | The owner who paid to acquire every one of these people | *"You already paid to acquire every patient on your overdue report"* (offer master §6) |

## 3. WHEN — the three timelines

| Timeline | Frame | Draft line |
|---|---|---|
| **Past** | The money already spent | "You paid to acquire every name on that list. Once, at full price." |
| **Present** | What's happening while they read | "That list is growing right now. It grew while you read this sentence." |
| **Future (negative)** | Hormozi flags this as the most powerful | "In two months the hygiene columns go soft, and nobody will be able to point to the week it started." |
| **Future (positive)** | The dream state, made concrete | "Monday morning, your hygienist opens a full column — and none of it came from a new-patient ad." |

## 4. The hook bank — 30 call-outs across all four types

Hormozi's split-test system: ~10 value pieces × ~30 hooks. **The hook is the variable to test first
— swap it before killing a concept.** Every hook below traces to research.

### Type A — LABELS (put them in a group they identify with)
1. Dental practice owners with 500+ overdue patients.
2. PPO practice owners with holes in the hygiene schedule.
3. If you own a dental practice and your recall list has more than 500 names on it, this is for you.
4. Practice owners who've already fired one marketing company.
5. Solo owners who bought a practice in the last three years.
6. Dentists who've stopped believing recall works.
7. Owners of 3+ location groups with a shared overdue list.

### Type B — YES QUESTIONS (implied yes; makes them nod)
8. Is there a list of overdue patients in your software that nobody has called in months?
9. Does your front desk have a recall list they never get a free hour to work?
10. Did your hygiene schedule go soft this month, and you can't point to why?
11. Have you ever paid a vendor and genuinely couldn't tell whether it did anything?
12. Do your patients confirm — and then not show up anyway?
13. Are you still being billed by a company you already tried to cancel?
14. Is your recall report the thing that gets dropped every time the day gets busy?
15. Do you know how many patients left last year without telling you why?
16. Would you recognise the name of a patient who hasn't been in for two years?

### Type C — IF-THEN (conditional qualification — self-selects the wealth floor)
17. If you've got 500+ overdue patients sitting in your software, some of them are ready to book right now.
18. If your hygiene columns have holes and your overdue list has 500+ names, those two facts are related.
19. If you've been burned by a dental marketing company, read this before you hire another one.
20. If your recall software reports messages sent but can't tell you who booked, you're paying for the wrong number.
21. If your front desk is already drowning, adding a call list is not the fix.
22. If you're about to cut hours because the schedule is soft, do this first.
23. If a patient hasn't been in for 18 months, you've already paid for them twice — once to get them, once to lose them.

### Type D — RIDICULOUS RESULTS (bizarre/rare outcome → curiosity)
24. A 3-location PPO group put 32 patients back on the schedule without the front desk making a single phone call.
25. 657 texts. Nobody on staff picked up a phone. 32 patients rebooked.
26. This practice's recall problem was fixed by someone who never set foot in the building.
27. 22% of a dead patient list clicked a booking link. The industry average is 2–5%.
28. They booked 32 patients from a list their own team had written off.
29. The best-performing recall campaign this practice ever ran involved zero phone calls and zero discounts.
30. One file export. One copy approval. 32 patients back on the schedule.

> **Claim check:** hooks 24–30 use only locked facts (657 / 145 / 22% / 32 / 4.9%) plus the true
> operational fact that delivery is SMS-based and requires one export and one approval. No hook
> promises the viewer a dollar result. No hook names a competitor.

## 5. The angle matrix — how to generate more without repeating yourself

Pick one cell from each column. Every unique combination is a genuinely distinct ad, which is what
Andromeda rewards and what look-alike clustering punishes.

| Call Out type | × | Value element | × | WHO | × | WHEN |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Label | | Dream outcome | | Owner | | Past |
| Yes Question | | Perceived likelihood | | Hygienist | | Present |
| If-Then | | Speed | | Front desk | | Future − |
| Ridiculous Result | | Ease | | Associate | | Future + |
| | | Nightmare | | Patient who left | | |
| | | Risk | | Peer / DSO | | |
| | | Time delay | | | | |
| | | Sacrifice | | | | |

4 × 8 × 6 × 4 = **768 theoretically distinct combinations.** Most are nonsense; perhaps 40–60 are
strong. That's still 5–7× the current portfolio, which is the actual answer to "why don't my ads
scale."

**Combinations already used in the v2 batch** (don't rebuild these):
Label × Nightmare × Owner × Present (Ads 1, 2) · Label × Dream × Patient × Present (Ad 3) ·
Label × Perceived-likelihood × Owner × Present (Ad 4) · Label × Risk × Owner × Past (Ad 5) ·
Label × Dream × Owner × Present (Ad 6) · Label × Sacrifice × Peer × Present (Ads 7, 8)

**Highest-value unbuilt combinations:**

| # | Combination | Why it's strong |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Yes Question × Time delay × Front desk × Present | Opens on a nod, then names the one constraint they can't solve by trying harder |
| **B** | Ridiculous Result × Ease × Owner × Future+ | Curiosity hook + the "one export" payoff. Most scroll-stopping combination available |
| **C** | If-Then × Risk × Owner × Past | Self-qualifies on the wealth floor and speaks straight to the burned buyer |
| **D** | Yes Question × Nightmare × Hygienist × Future− | The only angle that makes the cost visible through a human being, not a number |

## 7. How to actually test this

Hormozi's finding, verbatim from the summary: *"The top 0.1% of Facebook advertisers test 11× more
creative than everyone else. Your ads don't scale because they're not good enough — not because the
platform capped you."*

**The split-test method:** hold the value body constant, swap only the call-out. That isolates the
single highest-leverage variable and matches your existing testing discipline in
[sgs-course-analysis.md](../../reference/sgs-course-analysis.md) — *one variable at a time*.

**Practical sequence at $50/day:**
1. Launch the 4 wave-1 ads from [meta-ads-voc-batch.md](./meta-ads-voc-batch.md) — these are the
   value-body proofs.
2. Once one shows signal, take its body and run **3 new call-outs** against it from §4 above, one
   from each unused type.
3. Whichever call-out wins becomes the control. Repeat.

**Reality check:** at $50/day you are learning-limited and cannot run a 300-variation test. The bank
in §4 isn't a launch list — it's the queue you pull from every 2–3 weeks when creative fatigues.
Frequency past 2.5 is your signal to reach for it.
