## The v4 sequence — and the correction behind it

**I was wrong in v3.** I claimed Suby's structure was for landing pages, not ads. He says the
opposite, explicitly, at [1:16:34]: the 17 steps are *"for ads, for landing pages, for VSL, for blog
posts."* He only mentions a landing page as an example of how step 1 renders. I turned one aside into
a rule and restructured the whole batch on it.

**His actual order puts benefit before pain, and pain before proof:**

| Step | | |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Call out the audience | |
| 2 | **Big bold specific benefit** | the promise |
| 3 | Back it up | |
| 4 | Irresistible intrigue | |
| 5 | **Floodlight on the pain** | agitate |
| 6 | Unique solution | |
| 7 | **Credentials / proof** | evidence |
| 8–10 | Benefits · social proof · the offer | |

**What was actually broken in v2** wasn't proof depth. It was a missing **step 2** — no big benefit
promise addressed to the reader. The ads opened with an observation or a wound and went straight to
narrative, so a reader got a problem before they got a reason to care.

**But proof placement is Trevor's call, and he's made it twice.** Suby's step 7 would put the proof
stack mid-ad. We tried that and it reads as buried. His 17 steps are one practitioner's template for
a sales message — not evidence that proof-late beats proof-early in a Meta feed. So the stack sits
directly under the promise, and the sequence becomes: **promise → proof → pain → mechanism → offer.**

| | v2 | v3 | v4 | **FINAL** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Benefit promise to the reader | missing | missing | line 1 | **line 1** |
| Full ✅ stack | 62% | 4–9% | 55–75% | **10–14%** |
| Pain agitation | buried the promise | stripped out | kept | **kept, after proof** |
| Longest paragraph | 39–73 words | 19–34 | under 40 | **under 40** |

Holding: 230–250 words · no price · no cost anchor · ~$9,600 only · 500+ gate · Learn More ·
headlines under 40 · no competitor named · 5th-grade reading level.
