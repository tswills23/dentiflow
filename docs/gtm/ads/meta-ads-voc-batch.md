> built to be expensive.

## Ad 6 — Two-Way Recall (mechanism) ⚑ keep live at all times

> **Built from:** offer master §3 requires mechanism language in ≥1 live variant. Reinforced by the
> research finding that the demographic most likely to be overdue is the least likely to follow a link
> without a human on the other end.

**Primary text** *(241 words)*
> Dental practice owners with 500+ overdue patients: here's why recall keeps failing on you.
>
> Most recall is one-way. Postcards. No-reply text blasts. A front-desk call list nobody ever
> finishes. One-way messages get ignored, because the patient's real blockers never get answered. How
> much will this cost? Who is this? I can't deal with scheduling right now.
>
> So the patient does nothing. And the practice decides recall doesn't work.
>
> We run Two-Way Recall instead. The AI opens the conversation. When the patient replies, it answers
> like your best front-desk person — the cost question, the insurance question, the "can I come
> Saturday?" question — and walks them to a booked appointment.
>
> At a 3-location PPO group:
>
> ✅ About $9,600 in production from one overdue segment
> ✅ 32 patients booked — 4.9%, when a one-way blast typically books 1–3%
> ✅ 145 replies and clicks — 22%, when typical is 2–5%
>
> Two things people always ask. Anything urgent goes straight to your front desk, with your phone
> number in the message — the AI never handles an emergency. And nothing clinical is ever sent. No
> chart talk, no treatment talk. You approve every word before the first text goes out.
>
> This isn't for you if you have fewer than 500 overdue patients.
>
> If it doesn't book patients, you don't pay.
>
> Tap below to see if you qualify.
>
> — The Dentiflow team

**Headline:** `One-way recall is why it fails` (30)
**Description:** `Urgent replies route straight to your desk.`
**CTA:** Learn More · **Visual:** two message threads side by side — one unanswered bubble vs a real exchange.
**Pre-handles:** "I already do recall" · "will a robot embarrass my practice"

## 11. Not used, and why

- **No-show and cancellation angles** — not the entry SKU; offer master §8 forbids routing away from
  the pilot or selling a module that isn't on. Hold for continuity attach.
- **Review-volume angles** — research argues against it. 94 quotes about retaliatory 1-stars vs a
  handful wanting volume, plus "my PMS already does this" and "it's literally two texts." If sold
  later, sell *defence*, not volume.
- **Missed-call angles** — unproven, no case study, and the top-voted comment in the after-hours
  thread refuses the premise entirely: *"If they choose another practice because I won't answer the
  phone at 8pm on a Saturday then I count myself lucky."*
- **Competitor names** — quotes from real vendor reviews stay; the vendor names come out.
