3. Why we built it (origin = mechanism)

Here's what got me stuck on this.

The practices I saw mostly weren't struggling to grow — they were busy. What they were doing was quietly leaking patients they'd already won. People who'd sat in the chair, already trusted them, and then just fell off the schedule. Nobody's fault — the front desk is buried, the phone's always ringing, and the overdue list is forever tomorrow's problem.

So I wanted to know why nobody ever gets those patients back. And the answer turned out to be almost stupidly simple.

4. The enemy — why the old way fails (soft)

Every tool a practice uses for this does the same thing. Postcards. A "you're due, call us" text. A voicemail. They send one message and then go quiet. One-way.

But a patient who's been gone a while has a question sitting in the way — how much is this going to cost me, is my insurance still good, can I even come in on a Saturday. A postcard can't answer that. So the patient does nothing, and the practice decides recall doesn't work. The message was fine. There was just nobody on the other end to answer back.

5. The new way — the mechanism

So we built the other end.

We call it Two-Way Recall. It opens the conversation by text — and the moment the patient replies, it answers. Like your best front-desk person on her best day, except it never gets busy and never lets a message sit. It handles the cost question, the insurance question, the scheduling — and it walks a ready patient all the way to a booked appointment.

That's the whole difference between [32] booked and the one or two percent a normal blast gets. It isn't that we sent more messages. It's that we answered the reply.

6. How it actually works for you (concrete)

Now — how this runs for you, because I want it to sound boring and clear, not like magic.

We take the first 200 patients off your overdue list. Not your whole base — a defined, bounded group. You do two things: export one list out of your software, and approve the messages before they go out. That's your entire job. We build it, we run it, we handle every reply, and there are hard safety rails on all of it — instant opt-outs, anything urgent routed straight to your team, every message checked before it sends.

And you're never in the dark while it runs. You get a live dashboard — you watch the clicks and the bookings land in real time. At the end you get a named list of exactly who booked, checked against your own schedule, so there's no guessing whether it worked.

7. The outcome + the honest part

The goal is simple: a batch of your own lost patients back in the chair, in 30 days, with your team doing basically none of the work.

And let me be straight with you, because I'd rather you trust me than be impressed. That [$8,000] was one practice's list. Yours will be your own number — every list is different, and I'm not going to stand here and promise you a dollar figure. What I'll promise is the process. And I'll put my own money on it.

8. Guarantee

Here's how. If the pilot books fewer than 5 patients in 30 days, you don't eat it. Your choice: every dollar back, or we keep working your list for free until you hit 5. Five patients is roughly what the whole thing costs — so the deal is, it pays for itself, or it's free. I'm not being generous. I've watched it work, so putting my fee on the line is the easy part.

9. Price — via cost-transparency

So let me be transparent about what this costs, the same way I'd want someone to be with me.

It isn't free for us to run. There's the outreach, the AI handling every conversation, the setup in your voice, and my time on your account for the month. Set that against roughly [$8,000] in recovered production — or against a front-desk hire, who runs you several thousand a month and still wouldn't have time to work this list.

Against all of that, the pilot is $1,250. One time. Not a contract, not per month. And if you want us to keep it running across your whole list afterward, that's a conversation for the call — not now.

10. Scarcity + logistics (real)

One honest limit: we run three of these at a time. That's it. It's done-for-you and I'm the one delivering it, so three is the real ceiling — when they're full, the calendar closes until a slot opens. And if the times on the call don't line up, we'll put you first in line for the next opening.

11. Self-selecting close

So here's all I'd ask you to sit with before our call.

If that case study was useful to you — if it made you picture your own overdue list a little differently — then the call will be worth your time, and the pilot probably will be too. If it didn't land, then this honestly isn't for you, and that's completely fine.

But if it did — come to the call ready. Have a rough idea of how many overdue patients you're sitting on, and let's find out if you're a fit. I'll talk to you there.
