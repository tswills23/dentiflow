## 1. Strategy — why this shape

**What changed (Andromeda, plain English):** Meta no longer leans on your targeting settings.
It *reads the ad itself* and decides who sees it — "the creative is the targeting." Two
consequences that drive this whole portfolio:

1. **Diversity beats volume.** Meta collapses look-alike ads into one "entity," so 30 ads
   that all look the same perform like 1. You win by feeding it **conceptually different**
   ideas — different hook, different visual, different angle. Target: **8–12 distinct concepts.**
2. **Creative is ~70–80% of performance.** Budget and targeting tweaks barely move the needle
   now. So we spend our effort on angles and hooks, not audience micromanagement.

**Static-led, not static-only.** Static drives the majority of B2B conversions, is cheaper to
produce at the volume Andromeda wants, and a proof-heavy offer ("32 patients, $55 of texts")
reads instantly as a static. We add **2–3 short video/UGC cuts** because video tends to win
cost-per-lead and adds the diversity Meta rewards. This is a portfolio, not a static-vs-video bet.

### Recommended account setup
- **Objective:** Leads (test **Instant Form** vs. **landing page** — see below).
- **Campaign:** 1 campaign, **Advantage+** audience (let Andromeda find the operators;
  seed with a broad interest stack: dental practice owner, DSO, practice management, Dentrix,
  Weave, dental CE — then let it expand).
- **Ad sets:** 1–2 to start. Don't over-segment — Andromeda wants pooled signal.
- **Ads:** **Dynamic Creative ON**, 4–5 ads live per ad set, drawn from the concepts below.
- **Refresh cadence:** new concepts every **2–3 weeks** to beat fatigue; kill clustered/dead ads.
- **Geo:** start Midwest (matches the proof + Scott's network), expand on winners.

### Destination test (run both)
- **A — Meta Instant Lead Form:** removes the landing-page step; research shows 2–3x conversion
  for B2B free-offer asks. Best for raw lead volume. Qualify with 1–2 questions
  ("Do you own/manage a dental practice?", "How many locations?").
- **B — Landing page** (`dentiflow.ai/case-study-1` once live): higher intent, lets the video
  + funnel do the selling before the call. Better lead quality, lower volume.
- Run A and B as separate ads in the same set; compare cost-per-*qualified*-call, not cost-per-lead.

## 3. Static creative concepts (10 distinct angles)

Each = one ad direction. They are **conceptually separable** (different tension), so Andromeda
won't cluster them. Format note: design **square (1:1)** + **vertical (4:5 / 9:16 story)** of each.
On-image copy must be readable with **sound off** and at thumbnail size.

> Convention below: **HERO** = the big on-image line · **SUB** = smaller supporting line ·
> **VISUAL** = design direction · **TENSION** = the buyer pain it hits.

### C1 — Result-first (the ratio as hero)
- **HERO:** 32 patients. ~$8,000. About $55 in texts.
- **SUB:** A real dormant-list recall test at one PPO group.
- **VISUAL:** Bold type on flat brand color, three numbers stacked, no clutter. Big-number ad.
- **TENSION:** "Show me it works before you explain anything."

### C2 — Cost asymmetry (the equation)
- **HERO:** $55 of text messages → ~$8,000 in production.
- **SUB:** 657 dormant patients. 32 booked. One PPO group.
- **VISUAL:** Literal equation layout — small "$55" on the left, arrow, big "$8,000" on the right.
- **TENSION:** "What's the cheapest growth lever I'm not pulling?"

### C3 — Waste / DSO-insider (Scott's angle)
- **HERO:** You're buying new patients while hundreds sit dormant in your chart.
- **SUB:** A former 50-location DSO CFO ran this test on his own practice.
- **VISUAL:** Plain, almost editorial. Quote-card styling. Authority over flash.
- **TENSION:** "I'm overspending on Google Ads / mailers for new patients."

### C4 — The chart-problem (reframe the asset)
- **HERO:** Your last 600 dormant patients are your cheapest marketing list.
- **SUB:** 657 texted → 32 back on the schedule (~4.9%). Industry typical is 1–3%.
- **VISUAL:** Calm, "did you know" reframe. One stat block bottom-third.
- **TENSION:** "I think about new patients; I forget the ones I already had."

### C5 — SMS-thread proof (show the real text)
- **HERO (overlay):** This is a real recall text. The patient booked.
- **VISUAL:** A **cleaned** screenshot of Thread 1 (Day 3 → "Yes" → "Perfect, see you then.").
  Mock it in a clean iMessage-style frame. **Fix the render bugs before use:** remove the
  duplicated "Village Dental Village Dental," blur the Railway URL, keep only the human beats.
- **TENSION:** "Does this sound like spam or like my front desk?" → it sounds human.

### C6 — Mechanism (why it works, no A/B claim)
- **HERO:** One generic text doesn't work. A 3-message sequence that sounds human does.
- **SUB:** Day 0, Day 1, Day 3 — three voices, no discount blast. 32 booked from 657.
- **VISUAL:** Three little message bubbles, escalating. Simple diagram.
- **TENSION:** "I already 'do recall' (one blast) and it doesn't work."

### C7 — Comparison to the default (no competitor named)
- **HERO:** What most dental vendors send vs. what actually gets a reply.
- **VISUAL:** Split card. Left: a generic "$50 off your cleaning!" blast (greyed, sad).
  Right: a warm human-sounding front-desk text (branded). **Don't name a competitor.**
- **TENSION:** "My recall tool sends robotic discount texts nobody answers."

### C8 — Authority / credibility
- **HERO:** Built by people who actually thought about how patients reply to texts.
- **SUB:** Tested at 32 Dental by Scott Wills — former CFO & CEO of a ~50-location DSO.
- **VISUAL:** Headshot-optional credibility card; logo lockup; clean.
- **TENSION:** "Who's behind this and why should I trust the number?"

### C9 — Founder note (plain-text / low-fi static)
- **FORMAT:** Looks like a plain typed note / screenshot of text, not a designed ad.
  Andromeda + IG feed reward native-looking low-fi creative.
- **COPY (on image):**
  > "We texted 657 dormant patients at a 3-location PPO group. 145 clicked the link.
  > 32 booked. Total text cost: about $55. New production: ~$8,000. No discount blast —
  > the messages just read like a real front-desk text. Want the full breakdown?"
- **TENSION:** ad-fatigue / banner-blindness — this doesn't look like an ad.

### C10 — Carousel (funnel walkthrough)
- **FORMAT:** 4–5 slide carousel, one number per slide, swipe = the story.
  - Slide 1: "We tested recall on a 3-location PPO group's dormant list."
  - Slide 2: **657** dormant patients texted.
  - Slide 3: **145** clicked the booking link (~22% — typical is 2–5%).
  - Slide 4: **32** booked (~4.9% — typical is 1–3%).
  - Slide 5: ~**$8,000** new production for ~**$55** in texts. → "See exactly how. [CTA]"
- **TENSION:** rewards the curious; great for retargeting clickers of C1/C2.

| A5 | V1 Proof-flash (video) | — | P2 | "657 texts → 32 patients back" | Apply Now → Form |

Wave 2 (after 2–3 weeks): rotate in C5 (SMS proof), C7 (comparison), C10 (carousel for
retargeting clickers), V2 (Scott cut). Kill anything fatigued or clustered.

### How to read it (run it without an agency)
- **Primary metric:** cost per **qualified** lead/call (owner/manager of a practice), not raw CPL.
  A cheap lead that isn't a practice owner is worthless.
- **Hook is king:** if an ad underperforms, swap the **first line (4a)** before killing the concept —
  it's the biggest single lever.
- **Kill criteria:** if an ad spends ~3–5x your target cost-per-lead with no qualified lead, pause it.
- **Don't fiddle daily:** give Dynamic Creative ~3–5 days / enough conversions to learn before judging.
- **Refresh every 2–3 weeks:** add new *concepts* (not restyles) to fight fatigue and feed Andromeda.
- **Winning signal:** a concept that holds CPL as you scale budget = scale it and spin variants
  (new hooks/visuals of the *same winning angle*).
- **Retarget:** anyone who clicked/watched 50%+ → serve C10 carousel + V2 Scott cut.
