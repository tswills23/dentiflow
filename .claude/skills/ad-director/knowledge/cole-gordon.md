# Lens 5 — Gordon: what kind of lead does this ad produce?

**The one question:** What lead does this ad create, and can that lead be closed on a call?

This is the lens the other four don't have. Ogilvy, Schwartz, Hormozi and Suby all optimize the
*ad*. Cole Gordon's frame comes from the sales floor: an ad that produces cheap leads who can't
buy is a worse ad than one that produces expensive leads who can. **The ad is the top of a
closing system, not a standalone artifact.**

## The 3S formula

**Solve a SPECIFIC problem for a SPECIFIC person in a SPECIFIC way.**

Generality is what makes an offer a commodity: no reason for a stranger to respond, commodity
pricing, and every client arriving with a different journey (which makes delivery unsystematizable).

Our 3S: *overdue-patient reactivation* (specific problem) for *PPO practice owners with 300+
overdue patients* (specific person) via *Two-Way Recall* (specific way).

**The conveyor-belt consequence:** one entry SKU means every client arrives with the same problem,
same onboarding, same delivery, same proof format. When an ad drifts off the 3S — "we also do
no-shows, reviews, speed-to-lead" — it breaks the conveyor belt downstream even if CTR goes up.
**An ad that widens the problem statement is a delivery decision disguised as a creative decision.**

## Desire: below-average → average beats average → excellence

Two problem types:
- **Below average → average (pain).** High urgency, loss aversion, big market, usually less money.
- **Average → excellence (unfulfilled desire).** Low urgency, small market, more money.

The nuclear combination is **wealthy people who feel below average** — high urgency *and* high
budget. Our buyer is exactly that: a high-producing owner whose hygiene schedule is visibly
leaking. He isn't trying to become excellent; he's trying to stop bleeding something he already
built. That's why "recover what you already paid for" outperforms "grow your practice" — one is a
loss to reverse, the other is an aspiration to fund.

Requirement: the messaging must **gut-punch the specific pain viscerally.** Generic callouts
("busy practice owners") don't move sophisticated buyers.

## You can't create desire — only channel it

Put the offer in front of existing desire, or bridge to it. **"Cheese in the broccoli":** wrap what
they need inside what they want. Nobody wakes up wanting AI, or a recall system, or software. They
want full hygiene columns and production. AI and Two-Way Recall are the broccoli; booked patients
are the cheese. This is the mechanical reason "AI" stays out of the offer name.

## Guarantees are a *marketing* instrument

Their main job is to stand out and get people on the phone — **not** to help the close. Ours
(fewer than 12 bookings in 30 days → we keep working that location free, plus a 14-day unconditional
refund) exists to make a stranger willing
to respond to a faceless brand with one case study. That's an ad-level function.

Corollary: if lead generation stops being the constraint, the guarantee's marginal value drops and
it can move later in the sequence. Don't reflexively lead with it forever.

## Proof volume compounds

Stacked proof alone can make cold acquisition work — and it doesn't require famous names. Ten to
fifteen case studies of people *just like the prospect* beats one impressive stranger.

**Our honest position:** we have **one** case study. That is a real constraint, not a copywriting
problem, and no amount of clever framing substitutes for case study #2. Two implications for
creative:
1. Squeeze every distinct proof *artifact* out of the one case study — the numbers, the named
   booked-patient list, the conversation transcripts, Scott's DSO credibility, the SMS threads.
   Each is a different-looking proof to the reader.
2. Flag it. When Trevor asks why response is soft on cold traffic, "proof volume" is a legitimate
   answer and the fix lives outside the ad account.

## Lead-quality economics — the diagnostic that belongs to this lens

Never optimize to cost-per-lead. Optimize to **cost per qualified call that shows**.

| Signal | What it means | Fix lives in |
|---|---|---|
| Cheap leads, low show rate | Ad promised something the application/booking flow doesn't deliver | Ad + confirmation sequence |
| Good show rate, low close | Ad pre-sold the wrong expectation (usually a promise the call has to walk back) | Ad copy |
| Leads below the qualification floor | Callout isn't gating hard enough — Meta found the cheapest human | Hook: add the wealth floor to the first five words |
| Great leads, too few | Callout is gating correctly; the constraint is budget, proof volume, or creative diversity | Not the hook |

**Escalating the gate always costs volume and buys quality.** Which direction to move is a business
decision — ask Trevor which constraint he's on before you "fix" the hook.

## What this lens catches

- Ads optimized for CTR that produce unclosable leads
- Scope creep off the 3S ("we also do…") that breaks the conveyor belt
- Aspiration framing ("grow your practice") where loss framing belongs
- Promises the call script then has to walk back
- Treating a proof-volume problem as a copy problem
