## The mechanism — Two-Way Recall

Must appear in **at least one live ad variant at all times**. It is the differentiation layer.

**Naming decision, 2026-08-05:** "Ledger-to-Chair" was proposed as a replacement and **rejected as
the ad-facing name.** Reason: a mechanism's job is to explain why everything they tried failed and
why this works. "Two-Way Recall" does that in three words — "one-way vs two-way" needs no
explanation and survives a 3-second scroll. "Ledger-to-Chair" names a path but requires a paragraph
to land, which is fine on a call and useless in a headline. **The real upgrade was the three named
stages below, not the name.** Ledger-to-Chair may be used as the *process* name on the landing page
or sales call, never as the ad-facing mechanism.

> Most recall is one-way. Postcards, no-reply text blasts, a front-desk call list nobody
> finishes. One-way messages get ignored because the patient's real blockers — "how much will
> this cost?", "who is this?", "I can't deal with scheduling right now" — never get answered.
> So the patient does nothing, and the practice concludes recall doesn't work.
>
> We run **Two-Way Recall**: the AI opens the conversation, and when the patient replies, it
> answers like your best front-desk person — handles the cost question, the insurance question,
> the "can I come Saturday?" question — and walks them to a booked appointment. Then it books
> them into the blocks the practice set aside.

### The three stages (usable in longer-form copy, VSL, landing page)

1. **Ledger Sweep** — every overdue patient pulled and scored: months since last visit, remaining
   insurance benefits, diagnosed treatment sitting unscheduled, production history. The list becomes
   a ranked queue instead of a blob. Waves are **blended** (~60% recently overdue, 40% deep overdue)
   so the rate holds month after month instead of decaying after the first sweep.
2. **The Conversation** — two-way AI text that answers the three real blockers and handles the
   reply, the follow-up, and the reschedule.
3. **Chair Lock** — the patient picks a time from the **reactivation blocks the practice set aside**,
   and it lands on their schedule.

### Reactivation blocks — new, and strong in copy

The practice sets aside two 3-hour windows a week for returning patients. We only book into those.

- Works with any PMS — no integration required
- Makes "books onto your schedule" literally true
- Guarantees availability, which is the silent killer of every reactivation campaign
- Kills the front-desk objection: "we only book into the windows you gave us"
- Great hook material: *"Tell us which chairs you want filled."*

### Naming rules

- **"AI" stays out of the offer name.** AI is the *how*, never the *what*. It may appear in hooks
  as a pattern-interrupt test; the offer name never depends on it.
- **Claim boundary (non-negotiable):** ✅ "books them onto your schedule" / "into the blocks you set
  aside." ❌ "writes directly into your PMS" / "integrates with Dentrix or Open Dental" — the Open
  Dental write path is built but untested and must not be claimed.

## The offer (for context — never stated in ads)

- **The 30-Day Empty Chair Sprint: $2,000 one-time.** One location. **500-patient overdue segment.**
  Revealed on the call only.
- **Guarantee: fewer than 12 booked appointments in 30 days → we keep working that location free
  until they hit 12.** Plus a 14-day unconditional refund. Ad line: "or you don't pay" / "or it's free."
- **Continuity ("Chair Watch"):** priced at the nearest $50 *below* one hygiene visit at their own
  fee schedule, per location, per month. Starts day 31 at full price.
- **The sprint is the foot in the door**, not the revenue product. Its real output is the right to
  sell the full system. Optimize ad copy for *yes-rate*, not for deal size.
- **Capacity: 3 concurrent sprints.** Real scarcity — state it truthfully, never invent urgency.
- **Single location only** until multi-location isolation ships. Never imply a simultaneous
  multi-office rollout.

## Who the copy is written at

**Owner or managing partner of a 3–7 location PPO group.** Established, high-producing, visibly
leaky hygiene schedule, wants nothing to do with doing it himself. Gut-punch that specific picture —
"holes in your hygiene columns while 500 of your own patients sit overdue." Never generic "grow
your practice."

Qualification floor (application gates): PPO practice · **500+ overdue patients at the target
location** · owner/decision-maker · can approve a paid engagement · will do a 20-min call, provide
an export, and set aside two blocks a week · **must state their hygiene visit fee** (prices Chair
Watch, pre-computes ROI, filters for wealth).
